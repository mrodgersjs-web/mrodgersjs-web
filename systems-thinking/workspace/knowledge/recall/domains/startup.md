## Lean Canvas traps (AI 2026)

- Ash Maurya reviewed 500+ ideas (traditional and AI rush) and the same 10 Lean Canvas mistakes keep killing models; Lean Canvas is the stress-test used at MIT, Harvard, and Techstars before a dollar is spent. [Card: Why 90% of AI Startups Will Be Dead by December](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSoN4HMhlrQ)
- Metrics without a number and a time box are not metrics. “Increase revenue” is empty; “$10,000 MRR by month 6” is an objective. Define **minimum success criteria** first — the smallest outcome that makes the project worthwhile — or you build a zombie startup that looks alive and goes nowhere. [Card: Why 90% of AI Startups Will Be Dead by December](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSoN4HMhlrQ)
- In 2026 code is near-free and the product space is crowded. Investors want a path to a **working business model**, not just a working product. [Card: Why 90% of AI Startups Will Be Dead by December](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSoN4HMhlrQ)
- Customer = “small businesses / anyone” is no one. An early adopter is the smallest group that has the problem *now* and would pay. Litmus: could you find 10 of them and get them on a call next week? If you would not know where to look, the segment is too broad. [Card: Why 90% of AI Startups Will Be Dead by December](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSoN4HMhlrQ)
- Opposite trap: local maxima. One segment × one problem × one solution looks clean and internally consistent while the opportunity shrinks to a hill next to a mountain you never saw. [Card: Why 90% of AI Startups Will Be Dead by December](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSoN4HMhlrQ)
- Generic problems (“expensive / slow / AI for productivity”) fail because of AI fatigue. Buyers purchase AI that “prevents solo tax consultants from missing 1099 deadlines,” not generic productivity. Keep asking why until you hit the behavior people will pay to change. [Card: Why 90% of AI Startups Will Be Dead by December](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSoN4HMhlrQ)
- UVP must bind **this problem × this customer**. Not “easy project management” — “the only PM tool that auto-prioritizes tasks by client deadlines so solo consultants never miss a deliverable.” [Card: Why 90% of AI Startups Will Be Dead by December](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSoN4HMhlrQ)
- Unfair-advantage test: “passionate team / great CS / innovative tech” fails because a competitor can hire, copy, or wait for a Friday OpenAI/Anthropic update. Wrappers are rented land. Real UA = network effects, proprietary data, or years of domain expertise. If you do not have it yet, write **none yet** — pretending is the danger. [Card: Why 90% of AI Startups Will Be Dead by December](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSoN4HMhlrQ)
- Runway is time, not just cash (nights-and-weekends months until burnout; full-time months of savings/funding). Founders design 18-month validation on 6 months of runway. Work backward from minimum success criteria → customers at that price through that channel, and ask whether the path fits remaining time. Pivoting price/segment/channel is near-free early and expensive after six months of building. [Card: Why 90% of AI Startups Will Be Dead by December](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSoN4HMhlrQ)
- Channel buffet (social + SEO + ads + PR) means none have been tested. One channel to PMF. Existing spend in the problem is a proxy for a monetizable model — no existing spend ≈ no business model. [Card: Why 90% of AI Startups Will Be Dead by December](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSoN4HMhlrQ)
- #1 pitfall: innovator’s bias / solution-first canvas. If the solution box is the most detailed (features, architecture, stacks) you are building a solution in search of a market. Vibe-coding a weekend app makes this the default 2026 failure mode. [Card: Why 90% of AI Startups Will Be Dead by December](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lSoN4HMhlrQ)

## Durable business design (five advantages)

- Hormozi’s five advantages that make a vehicle easier to grow: **sticky, high gross margin, expanding market, operationally easy / low capex (with a capex asterisk), unique/moat**. Portfolio cited: $250M+ revenue last year. Few firms have all five; even one beats a level-10 operator in a level-2 vehicle. If you have none, fix retention first. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)
- **Sticky** = last year’s revenue still here this year. Company A sells 100 and churns 100; Company B sells 100 and churns 0 → year 3 is 300 compounding customers vs. a treadmill. Term life, alarm systems, ISPs are sticky; education-as-graduation, roofing, car sales are one-shots. Structural membership churn cited ~2%/month after month six — so the job is getting customers *to* month six. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)
- **Gross margin** funds pay, cash conversion, and growth. Grocery / farming / restaurants are structurally thin; media, education, community, data, software, pharma (“penny to make, dollar to sell”), lotions/potions are fat. Decommoditize (Offers book) to lift margin. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)
- **Expanding industry** (energy, AI, healthcare, e-comm, alt-ed, cybersecurity) is a tailwind; if you already know demand-gen you mainly need to avoid a headwind. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)
- Low operational complexity + low capex (podcasting, online education) lets you expand without dilution. Buffett wants cash-generative firms, not ones that eat every dollar of profit to grow. Counter: capex *as moat* (shovel vs. hands; power plant; Nvidia) — high ROIC attracts capital; a franchise that costs $100k and takes 3 years to pay back is a hard raise because the deal is bad, not because marketing is weak. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)
- **Unique**: brand (Revlon vs. CVS white-label off the same belt), recipe/Coke, patents (new + nonobvious + useful), trade secrets, proprietary process. If the industry has no retention, leave. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)

## Value propositions people actually buy

- Statement shape: **For who** that is **dissatisfied with what** due to an unmet need, you offer a product that solves it with benefits compelling enough to act. “A problem well stated is half-solved.” Hair-on-fire first. [Card: Value Props: Create a Product People Will Actually Buy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q8d9uuO1Cf4)
- 4U screen for the idea: **unworkable, unavoidable, urgent, underserved**. Compra Facil’s credit-marketplace pitch failed until it named *who*. Connect-Ed (Kazakhstan digital divide) had two “whos”: device donors/funders *and* recipients — pick the buyer. [Card: Value Props: Create a Product People Will Actually Buy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q8d9uuO1Cf4)
- Stop claiming faster/better/cheaper. Aim at a **3D breakthrough: disruptive, discontinuous, defensible**. Google’s ads-funded suite vs. Microsoft’s paid Office is the canonical discontinuity. [Card: Value Props: Create a Product People Will Actually Buy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q8d9uuO1Cf4)
- **Gain/pain ratio**: founders can list gain and almost never list adoption pain (inertia, integration, “what if this startup dies with my money in it”). Venmo had to feel bank-like; Tesla still has charging-station debt. State **10x gain vs. retraining pain**, not “it’s X.” [Card: Value Props: Create a Product People Will Actually Buy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q8d9uuO1Cf4)
- Rent the Runway (Harvard) worked as urgent + one-wear-event; that is a different job than “always rent clothes.” Same product, different 4U scores. [Card: Value Props: Create a Product People Will Actually Buy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q8d9uuO1Cf4)

## AI wedge into mature software categories

- Do not sprinkle AI on a feature list. Start from the incumbent’s **balance sheet**. Salesforce ~$40B/year because ~90% of the Fortune 500 is locked in; seat-based CRM assumed a human in a seat. If AI populates/manages the CRM, the seat is a legacy tax. Salesforce Agentforce + M&A still defends a world that no longer exists. [Card: The AI Wedge: 3 Steps to Break Into a Mature Software Category](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OopKjnvniV8)
- Three-step wedge: (1) **Map the category** — primary revenue model, the constraint it depends on, how AI makes that constraint irrelevant. Surface UI disruption that the incumbent can copy without business-model pain is not a wedge. (2) **Define the thesis** in three sentences: “The incumbent was built when [constraint] was unavoidable. AI eliminated it by [mechanism]. Without that constraint, [pricing/architecture] no longer makes sense.” (3) **Identify what breaks** — force a lose-lose: defend share *or* protect legacy margin (cannibalization). [Card: The AI Wedge: 3 Steps to Break Into a Mature Software Category](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OopKjnvniV8)
- Worked example: legal research incumbents priced on search-volume; **Harvey AI** cut every step between query and attorney deliverable and is cited at **$200M ARR in 4 years** while incumbents stay trapped by search-era pricing. [Card: The AI Wedge: 3 Steps to Break Into a Mature Software Category](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OopKjnvniV8)

## What to build in 2026 (agent-first, not SaaS-with-a-chatbox)

- Karpathy stack: Software 1.0 = handwritten rules; 2.0 = train nets on data; **3.0 = the LLM is the computer, the prompt is the code, the context window is the lever**. If you have not built something end-to-end with Claude Code / Cursor agent mode in the last 60 days, you are flying blind. December inflection: he stopped correcting the model and started trusting first drafts. [Card: Karpathy Just Told Us What Startups To Build For 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rsaaVXg28-8)
- Humans will live in the agent (Claude Code, Cursor, …) and invoke your product via MCP/skill/API rather than logging into a UI. A traditional SaaS with “AI sprinkled on top” (example: Levercast social poster) is the old shape. Strip human UI; ship `llm.txt` / machine-readable how-to-use so an agent can skip marketing copy. [Card: Karpathy Just Told Us What Startups To Build For 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rsaaVXg28-8)
- Build **verifiable domain capabilities** the labs will not RL: millions of niches. Own the eval environment + fine-tune. Do not build plumbing the next model release eats — skate to where the puck is. [Card: Karpathy Just Told Us What Startups To Build For 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rsaaVXg28-8)

A personal strategy-agent “brain” (markdown world-model that refuses off-strategy work) is called out as the highest-leverage internal artifact. [Card: Karpathy Just Told Us What Startups To Build For 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rsaaVXg28-8)

[Card: Karpathy Just Told Us What Startups To Build For 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rsaaVXg28-8)

Cursor’s xAI deal is cited as ~$10B now / $60B option. Differentiation is **personal agent + skills + industry articulation**, not another chat wrapper. Orchestrator + sub-agents; keep one main agent that holds global context. [Card: Every AI Company Is Building the Same App (Here's Why)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FU2iB-UfPPA)

## Founder operating systems (SOPs, OKRs, 10-80-10)

- Four systems to install or the firm dies as a founder bottleneck (Gymreapers 0 → $100M): **(1) Operations / McDonald’s SOPs** (document the Big Mac; design agencies need “this is how we design / what good looks like / how we talk to clients”; hire *operators* to write SOPs, not the best designer; Asana for tasks, Notion for docs; new hires do not get to bring their own tool). **(2) Growth / OKRs** (John Doerr, *Measure What Matters*; example: authority objective → 100k subscribers KR). **(3) Numbers** brought *to* people daily with insight, not a Shopify login nobody opens (mobile conversion down 2% is an action). **(4) Founder 10-80-10** — you start it, team does 80%, you close it; even at 150 people the founder does not vanish. [Card: Business is hard until you build systems like this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4tV_jwspQAs)
- Deploying Claude Code in **42+ businesses**: do not hire an agent without onboarding. Foundation = context file (not a company blurb) + tools + “never do” list. Priority matrix: hours × revenue × feasibility; the common miss is automating 20 minutes/month instead of the 15 hours/week bleed (proposals from discovery, weekly reports, follow-ups). Cited effect: ~2× team output / higher gross profit per employee. [Card: We Deployed Claude Code in 42+ Businesses. Here’s What Changed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=laHR24tiXXg)
- Brain file minimums: ICP, tone (never “I hope this email finds you well”), pricing tiers, key-client notes, connected tools (Gmail, HubSpot, Drive, Slack, Notion), **never**: send email / create HubSpot deal / quote price / commit timeline without approval; persist dated decisions. [Card: We Deployed Claude Code in 42+ Businesses. Here’s What Changed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=laHR24tiXXg)
- Selling Claude Code OS (2h+ course): every task has a leverage % (30/60/0 — almost never 0); treat AI as **mentor not vending machine**; job = tree of tasks. Operator dashboards: net profit, cash, runway forecast, ClickUp commitments, at-risk work — artifacts wired to MCP. Karpathy gist: give a high-level idea and let the agent build 2026 products. [Card: Build & Sell Claude Code Operating Systems (2+ Hour Course)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCljOfCH8Ms)
