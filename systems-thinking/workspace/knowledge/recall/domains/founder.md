## Founder operating system (do not be the bottleneck)

- Two terminal states: a business with transferable value, or a business that dies when the founder is still the bottleneck. Gymreapers scaled **$0 → $100M revenue** only after the founder installed four systems; early years were “I was the bottleneck… not good at delegating… didn’t know which systems.” [Card: Business is hard until you build systems like this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4tV_jwspQAs)

- **System 1 — Operations (“become a McDonald’s”).** A Big Mac is made the same way worldwide; write SOPs that dictate “this is how we design / what good looks like / how we talk to the client.” Version them (V1, V2, V3) and make the playbook the only way work ships. [Card: Business is hard until you build systems like this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4tV_jwspQAs)

- **Do not have the star craftsperson own the SOP.** Best designer ≠ best process writer. Bring in operationally driven people to decompose the work; craftsmen execute, ops people encode. Tools named: **Asana** for task tracking, **Notion** for living docs. [Card: Business is hard until you build systems like this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4tV_jwspQAs)

- **System 2 — Growth via OKRs.** One North Star visible to everyone (example: “become an authority in the business space” → **100,000 YouTube subscribers**). Cascade: the editor’s KR is **retention 30%+** so viewers don’t drop. Book: *Measure What Matters* (John Doerr / Google). [Card: Business is hard until you build systems like this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4tV_jwspQAs)

- **System 3 — Numbers with insight, not dashboards.** People will not “just log into Shopify.” Push a **daily report into Slack** that already names the problem (example: **mobile conversion down 2% — act**). Lack of centralized daily data is listed as a primary reason businesses fail to scale; people conceal information otherwise. [Card: Business is hard until you build systems like this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4tV_jwspQAs)

- **System 4 — Founder system = 10-80-10.** Founder sets the first 10% (intent, standard), team does 80%, founder reviews the last 10% before anything ships. Still used at **~150 people** plus a portfolio. Stepping fully away is how things break: “they won’t know what they don’t know… you’re the founder for a reason.” [Card: Business is hard until you build systems like this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4tV_jwspQAs)

## Pick the vehicle: five advantages of a “perfect” business

Alex Hormozi’s S-tier screen (portfolio **>$250M revenue last year**; **10 companies**, **20–100% ownership**). Few businesses have all five; even one upgrades the vehicle. All are continuums, not binaries. If you have none: **fix retention first**, then backfill. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)

1. **Sticky (most important).** Revenue from last year still here this year. Logo retention vs **net revenue retention**; target **NRR > 100%** (stayers expand enough to cover leavers). Distinguish **structural/involuntary churn** (move, death, business dies, the user-employee is fired) from **voluntary churn** (“they think you suck”). Sticky examples: term life, alarm systems, internet, phone, banking. Non-sticky: education, roofing, car sales. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)

2. **Onboarding math that actually moves NRR.** Across “hundreds of thousands of memberships,” the worst logo drop is **month 1 (>20% churn)**; next cliff **~10% at month 3**; last big drop **month 6**. Founder job is not “keep them forever” — **make the first 30 days awesome, a path through month 3, walk to month 6**. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)

3. **Compounding vs the hamster wheel.** Company A sells 100 / 200 / 300 and loses them all each year; Company B sells 100 / year and loses **zero**, ending at 300 with the same yearly revenue but locked-in base. Personal reason: you can have no new customers and still sleep. Math reason: acquiring 300 new logos every year is expensive. Once compounding unlocks, founders stop hopping vehicles. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)

4. **Fat gross margin.** High GM → typically higher EBITDA. **$100M revenue @ 10% margin = $20M @ 50% margin** in profit, but the fat-margin firm earns **5× incremental EBITDA per dollar**. Low-GM cluster: grocery, farming, restaurants (food is elastic). *Money Models* is the companion on speeding the cash cycle. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)

5. **Expansion = sit in a growing industry.** Skill is demand-gen; still don’t fight a headwind. Shrinking: newspapers, formal education (**−6%/yr** called out), tobacco, alcohol, brick-and-mortar retail, clerical/data-entry. Growing: energy, AI, healthcare, cybersecurity, e-commerce, alternative education. School’s bet: **alt-ed CAGR > 20%**. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)

6. **“Air” = low operational complexity + low capex.** Complexity = variables you must actively manage to expand a unit. Podcast ad-read: record, post — scales. 100-restaurant chain: employees, spoiling inventory, leases, parking, permitting. Low capex means you dilute less to grow; Buffett wants cash-generative businesses, not ones that eat every dollar of profit. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)

7. **Then invert capex into a moat.** Once you have cash, spend it on assets rivals must also buy (shovel vs hands; power plant; Nvidia-class specialized skill). Trade secrets / processes / patents (new + non-obvious + useful). Brand as the cheap moat: Revlon and CVS generic can leave the **same belt**; the stamp still sells at a premium with more stickiness. Coke = capital to enter + flavor patent + brand + habitual consumption + pennies of liquid vs retail price. [Card: What Makes The Perfect Business (5 Things)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fsJFUvA6Ts)

## Sell the outcome, not the tool (founder offer design)

- YC line, decoded: *“Instead of selling software to customers to help them do the work, you use the software yourself and sell them the finished product at 100 times the price.”* Don’t sell a recruitment dashboard — sell a **hired employee**. Don’t sell billing software — sell a **resolved claim**. Don’t sell a workflow — sell the **result**. [Card: The AI-First Agency Model YC Just Revealed (6 services to sell)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pba8FIkOilY)

- Price gap is the whole model. Recruitment SaaS: **$15–$100/month**. Recruitment agency placement: **$5k–$15k**. Traditional agency keeps **15–25%** ($5k fee → $3–4k cost → ~$1k left). Software-like agency keeps **70–90%** because the system is built once. [Card: The AI-First Agency Model YC Just Revealed (6 services to sell)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pba8FIkOilY)

- Hormozi, quoted in the same card: businesses with **disproportionate AI returns** are **ops-heavy with eternal PMF** (a marketing agency that “gets more customers”). The historic constraint was **headcount and coordination**; AI attacks that drag. YC examples: design firm (custom work **before the contract is signed**), ad agency (no physical shoot), law firm (docs in minutes not weeks). [Card: The AI-First Agency Model YC Just Revealed (6 services to sell)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pba8FIkOilY)

- Six services named, with working prices:
  1. **AI lead-gen as a service** — scrape, enrich, personalize (~**$0.003/lead** icebreaker), load **Smartlead/Instantly**, deliver meetings. Delivery cost ~**$30 for 600 leads**; charge ~**$2,500/month**.
  2. **Content engine** — ~**100 pieces/month** (LinkedIn + carousels, IG, short-form, long-form clips, blogs).
  3. **AI video ads** — Kling/similar from a product photo; **$1.5k–$5k per package**; **3–4 clients ≈ $10k/month**; tool burn **$100–$200/month**.
  4. **Website buildouts** — industry template + scrape brand/colors/copy; Claude Code / Lovable / Claude Design; hours not weeks.
  5. **AI appointment setters** — known operators at **$100k/month** on IG setters; **$2k–$5k/month**, or **~$10k setup + a few hundred maintenance**, or revenue share. Build once, skill-ize onboarding.
  6. **Full AI-first OS** — audit processes, map departments, ship agents/skills (Claude Code / Cowork). Four-step internal loop: **personalize the assistant → automate departments → scale with no new headcount**. Preconfigured skills are the margin (a month of iteration, then plug-in). [Card: The AI-First Agency Model YC Just Revealed (6 services to sell)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pba8FIkOilY)

- Same doctrine from the $184,809/month operator: **n8n automations that “save a few hours” are a brick; sell the bridge** (new leads → booked sales appointments). High-ticket locals (roofers, HVAC, pool, solar, med spa, stem-cell, peptide, bookkeepers, law, mortgage). HVAC replacement **$6k–$14k** (high-efficiency **$8k–$15k**); a missed call costs **$1k–$10k**. Charge **$15k–$25k upfront** for Meta ads + **Retail AI** voice caller, not $50–$100 Upwork gigs. Cheap clients ($5) chase you; **$10k wire** clients you have to chase. First few clients: learn ads + the caller yourself; then hire fulfillment (Upwork/Fiverr) and keep the spread. Proof trail on the card: **~$25k/month at 17** still in school → dropped out at 18. Stack named: Ideogram for image ads (four creatives in **~10–15 seconds**), ChatGPT/Claude/Grok/Gemini as prompt layer. [Card: Stop selling "Ai automations", sell THIS instead (how I make $184,809/month)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S0y08EU_nUs)

- **Coopetition contract, not a headcount plan.** Prompt ChatGPT for a partnership agreement: two agencies refer overflow (Facebook-ads shop ↔ YouTube-ads shop) for a percentage. Framed as how a founder expands **before** a big team. [Card: Step-by-Step Guide: How to Write Contracts with AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QAP3iO-sFy8)

## Custom-AI studio: how a founder actually ships client work

Dave Ablar / Data Luminina: **>10 years in AI, >50 custom B2B AI solutions**, evolved freelancer → co-founders → studio. They are **not trying to scale the agency**; they see a **~2-year window** where custom software still prints, then want their own product. [Card: How I Build and Ship Custom AI Solutions for Clients](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q679gH7oszg)

- **Discovery is a 45-minute call** covering problem, permissions/security, and a use-case evaluation **before** promising a build. Recurring categories: document processing, content generation, customer support, internal knowledge assistants, data extraction. [Card: How I Build and Ship Custom AI Solutions for Clients](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q679gH7oszg)

- **Start at the simple end of the spectrum.** Clients bring the “big” LLM pipeline; shadowing one employee for an afternoon often reveals **Zapier/n8n-class** moves that do not need a model. Be brutally honest about ROI before custom software. [Card: How I Build and Ship Custom AI Solutions for Clients](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q679gH7oszg)

- **Proposal’s first job: what it will do and what it will not.** Otherwise “can it also do X?” after you have built. Educate that **v1 is often ~80%** and 80% is **not** production for many tasks; client domain time is a required input, not a surprise. [Card: How I Build and Ship Custom AI Solutions for Clients](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q679gH7oszg)

- **Delivery cadence: 2-week sprints**, not 10-week waterfall. Standardized **GenAI Launchpad** (Python backend, identical folder layout per repo) is why they can run multiple projects: proposals → markdown → launchpad scaffold. Result: **what took 2 months now ships in 2 weeks**; **5–10× output vs ~3 years ago**. Sprint-gap risk: keep another project in the pipe; Claude Code + a subcontractor covers overlap. [Card: How I Build and Ship Custom AI Solutions for Clients](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q679gH7oszg)

- **Claude Code is vanilla.** Opus **4.6** (as of recording). No worktree/sub-agent religion — “I just use it” (Boris-from-Anthropic posture). When a motion repeats **2–3 times**, wrap it in a **skill** (Anthropic skill-creator). **Superpowers** skill = planning agent + execution agent + sub-agent loop for long-running tasks. Frontend when needed: **Next.js + Superbase auth**. [Card: How I Build and Ship Custom AI Solutions for Clients](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q679gH7oszg)

- **Deploy boring and cheap.** Docker Compose from day one, **Caddy** for HTTPS, Hetzner-class VMs; **~10× cheaper** than Azure/AWS they abandoned. Observability: **Langfuse** on every LLM trace, wired through Launchpad. Long-term money: maintenance fees + pass-through API cost. [Card: How I Build and Ship Custom AI Solutions for Clients](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q679gH7oszg)

## Founder-as-creator (distribution is the company)

- First-mover is a strategy, not a personality. Day-1 ChatGPT TikTok = **the first ChatGPT video on the platform, 20M views**; **0 → ~200k TikTok followers in 2 weeks** — attributed to being almost the only person covering the technology, not to craft. Then push the same audience to IG → Twitter. [Card: Meet The AI Creator Who Doesn't Write With AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SUHktZveTJo)

- After raising **$9M** and moving to SF: **~70% of time is not content** (product, hiring). Team: founder + studio lead + **6 developers**. Content became a machine that needs new inputs when time shrinks; it started to feel like a job, not play. [Card: Meet The AI Creator Who Doesn't Write With AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SUHktZveTJo)

- **Ship through cringe mountain** (Greg Eisenberg / Yoni Yar): the start-stop self-censorship of “people I know will judge a 0-follower account.” Cadence: make it, let it rip, multiple times per day. [Card: Meet The AI Creator Who Doesn't Write With AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SUHktZveTJo)

- **Default video until 50k subs:** 1-minute “here’s what you will see” → do the thing on screen → “subscribe.” Hook recipe: giant product logo (Claude Code) + stick-figure now→after + “you can build an app in 20 minutes” + name the tools + dive. Record in **Tella** (or Descript) in chunks. After 50k, optimize. [Card: Meet The AI Creator Who Doesn't Write With AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SUHktZveTJo)

Product: vibe-coding for mobile, web, and agents. Co-founder origin: a Twitter DM (“I can help with the backend”) → Riverside video of no-code + Firebase → 6 months hacking → “we can make a mobile app that builds mobile apps.” [Card: Meet The AI Creator Who Doesn't Write With AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SUHktZveTJo)

## Run the company as an AI OS (founder still sets hygiene)

- The Claude Code “runs my entire business” setup is **data organization with layers**, not a magic agent. “This takes **hundreds of hours** to start, iterate, and refine.” Front-end dashboards are “cute”; **back-of-house file hygiene is the foundation.** If the desktop is chaos, cloning someone else’s carbon-copy OS will not save you. [Card: This Claude Code Setup Runs My Entire Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7aQbN543Mec)

- **Promote skills deliberately.** Global skill (every agent) vs project-level skill. Skills transfer across Claude / Codex / Gemini; the differentiator is CLIs + integrations. 80/20 test: which features drive **business ROI vs novelty**. [Card: This Claude Code Setup Runs My Entire Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7aQbN543Mec)

- **Interface:** Telegram (blast radius of business vs personal life = **zero** in his case; Signal/Discord for others). Evolution: one agent → team → voice “war room” → hive mind of specialist agents. Comms agent owns Gmail / Outlook / LinkedIn. Meta Ads agent returns spend, actions, blind spots, and a winner/loser take with deep links to the ads. [Card: This Claude Code Setup Runs My Entire Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7aQbN543Mec)

- **Mission Control = Kanban of agents + tasks.** Gemini **3 Flash** (cheapest) classifies new tasks against agent descriptions and auto-assigns. Cron/schedule tab for weekday vs weekend jobs (local or VPS). Memory: **5–6 layers** rolling up to **importance / salience / recency**; searchable (“Gmail”) — maintenance, not “dump a vector DB.” List-view table of agent summaries must be live before 2D/3D “Obsidian graph” candy. [Card: This Claude Code Setup Runs My Entire Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7aQbN543Mec)

- **War room slash commands:** `/standup` and `/discuss` ping a hidden prompt: status vs hive-mind entries in the **last 24 hours**. ## Power, reputation, and change (use surgically)

From the 48 Laws animated summary — founder-applicable, not a license to play court:

- **Reputation is a shield and a sword.** Establish it on **one standout quality** early; once damaged you are an easy target. Perception precedes knowledge of you (Borgia example in the source). [Card: The 48 Laws of Power by Robert Greene | Complete Animated Book Summary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Ai3WkzeZEc)

- **Plan to the end.** “Hope is not a strategy; improvisation will only get you through the next crisis.” Define the end-state (power / wealth / influence — **specific**), map obstacles, write contingencies. Vague vision → vague results. [Card: The 48 Laws of Power by Robert Greene | Complete Animated Book Summary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Ai3WkzeZEc)

- **Never reform too much at once.** Alexander II frees the serfs (1861) → unprepared peasants, furious landowners, assassination. French Revolution → Terror. Fast radical change rarely lets the reformer survive to see the upside. [Card: The 48 Laws of Power by Robert Greene | Complete Animated Book Summary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Ai3WkzeZEc)

- **Formlessness / anti-rigidity.** IBM on mainframes while PCs rose → Microsoft and Apple; “businesses that refuse to innovate collapse.” Take nothing personally; be willing to abandon a system that no longer serves; formlessness is control, not passivity. [Card: The 48 Laws of Power by Robert Greene | Complete Animated Book Summary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Ai3WkzeZEc)

- **Strike the shepherd (Law 42).** Capone taken on **tax evasion**, network collapses. In a toxic org, one person is usually the glue — don’t fight every symptom. [Card: The 48 Laws of Power by Robert Greene | Complete Animated Book Summary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Ai3WkzeZEc)

- **Brand as cult structure (Law 27).** Rituals, hierarchy of progression, exclusivity, keep stimulating emotion so followers don’t pause to analyze. “Every powerful movement from ancient religions to modern brands.” Use for community design, not manipulation theater. [Card: The 48 Laws of Power by Robert Greene | Complete Animated Book Summary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Ai3WkzeZEc)
