# How probability influences ourr daily decisions | Application of Math Probability

The video explains how probability, measured on a scale from 0 to 1, serves as a practical tool for predicting outcomes and making informed decisions in everyday life.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define probability as the measure of how likely an event is to occur, expressed as a number between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain).
- Map simple chance events to exact probabilities: a fair coin has a 1/2 chance of heads, a six-sided die has a 1/6 chance per number, and a 52-card deck has a 1/52 chance per card.
- Interpret weather forecasts as expected frequencies over similar conditions, e.g., a 70% rain chance means roughly 7 out of 10 comparable patterns produce rain.
- Use probability to make binary daily choices under uncertainty, such as deciding whether to carry an umbrella.
- Apply odds-based reasoning to games of chance to understand likelihoods and inform strategy.
- Use actuarial-style risk modeling: compare the likelihood of an event across different groups to set costs, e.g., younger or higher-risk drivers pay higher insurance premiums.
- Estimate investment outcomes by analyzing historical data and current market trends to judge the probability of a stock rising or falling.
- Frame financial and business decisions as risk/reward balances guided by probability.
- Scale probability reasoning from small daily decisions to high-stakes choices like investing, launching a business, or buying insurance.
- Treat probability as a mental compass for navigating uncertainty with greater confidence.

## Failure modes named
- Believing that probability is only a textbook concept rather than a daily-life decision tool.
- Assuming you do not use math or probability in everyday decisions.
- Treating forecasts as certainty instead of understanding them as likelihoods over similar cases.
- Failing to apply probability thinking to high-stakes financial or business decisions.
