# Charlie Munger Mental Models and Psychology of Success

Charlie Munger explains how to live and decide well by treating rationality as a moral duty, avoiding obvious folly and ideology, thinking in multiple orders of consequence, accepting uncertainty and luck, and choosing the right relationships and ambitions.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat becoming unignorant and unstupid as a moral duty; work continuously to keep your own brain from misleading you.
- Update your judgments as new facts arrive; lifelong revision of priors is a core habit.
- Use inversion: list the things that will not work for you and avoid them like the plague.
- Distinguish vivid extreme evidence from deeper reality; do not let dramatic anecdotes override systemic truth.
- Think in first-, second- and higher-order consequences when evaluating trade, technology, policy, and strategy.
- Treat unavoidable multi-party systems as a tragedy-of-the-commons problem; you cannot unilaterally escape a trend that the rest of the world will pursue.
- Hold strong opinions provisionally; admit the limits of your knowledge and keep policy disagreements civil.
- Guard against anger and ideological groupthink, which drive out reason and turn your brain into cabbage.
- Select life partners and domains with extreme care; marriage is the highest-leverage decision most people make.
- Aim for independence and steady competence rather than low-probability status outcomes such as billionaire or president.
- Show up with discipline every day; keep learning, keep plugging, and let compounding do its work.
- Acknowledge luck and variance; do not over-attribute extraordinary outcomes to merit alone.

## Failure modes named
- Letting vivid but extreme evidence distort your model of reality.
- Optimism in roles that require pessimism, such as banking.
- Adopting a victim mentality.
- Chasing speculative manias like Bitcoin.
- Assuming certainty in domains where no one truly knows the answer.
- Allowing hatred, anger, or partisan groupthink to replace reasoning.
- Aiming for status goals with odds stacked overwhelmingly against you.
- Blaming political opponents for inevitable systemic consequences, such as the rise of China through trade.
