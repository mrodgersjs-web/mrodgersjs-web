# In A World of Systems

An animated tour of everyday systems—shower, traffic, fishery—teaching that systems are goal-oriented networks of nested parts whose behavior emerges from stocks, flows, delays, balancing feedback, and reinforcing feedback.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define a system as a set of connected parts organized to achieve a specific goal.
- Recognize that humans are themselves parts of systems, not only external observers.
- Identify stocks as accumulations that grow through inflows and shrink through outflows.
- Map feedback loops as the circular mechanism by which parts of a system influence one another.
- Distinguish balancing feedback loops as self-correcting processes that close the gap between a current state and a desired state.
- Distinguish reinforcing feedback loops as self-amplifying processes that push a system toward extremes.
- Treat delays as structural forces in systems that shape response time, oscillation, and frustration.
- See systems as nested: every system sits inside larger systems and contains smaller ones.
- Notice when social or emotional systems couple with physical systems and change overall behavior.
- Diagnose policy failure when rules are written with imperfect information and short-term returns in mind.
- Recognize depletion traps where outflows exceed inflows and stocks shrink over time.
- Trace any everyday outcome back to the multiple nested systems that make it possible.

## Failure modes named
- Delay-induced oscillation or prolonged hunting for the right state.
- Reinforcing escalation that drives a social system into frenzy or extreme states.
- Blocked outflow causing accumulation and gridlock in a stock-and-flow system.
- Imperfect information producing inadequate or counterproductive intervention rules.
- Short-term optimization undermining long-term stock health and sustainability.
- Systems designed to deplete themselves when extraction outpaces replenishment.
- Invisibility of nested systems leading people to participate without awareness of consequences.
