# Agent-Based Modeling: What is Agent-Based Modeling?

Agent-based modeling is introduced as a purposeful abstraction of reality, where autonomous agents interact with each other and their environment in a computer simulation to study complex systems and general behavioral patterns.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define a model as an abstract description of a process, object, or event rather than a perfect copy of reality.
- Build models by exaggerating the aspects most relevant to your question while suppressing less relevant details.
- Treat models as useful caricatures that highlight key features and push distractions into the background.
- Recognize that "all models are wrong, but some are useful"—usefulness comes from fitness for purpose, not fidelity.
- Construct an agent-based model from three core components: autonomous agents, an environment, and agent-agent or agent-environment interactions.
- Represent an agent as an autonomous, individual element with its own properties and actions inside a computer simulation.
- Select agent granularity to match the research question, such as humans, firms, viruses, or governments.
- Understand that changing the resolution of agents changes the questions a model can answer.
- Use ABM to model general behavioral patterns and emergent dynamics in complex systems.
- Avoid ABM for highly idiosyncratic, erratic individuals whose actions are hard to predict or generalize.
- Prefer ethnographic or in-depth psychological methods for questions focused on unique individual behavior.
- Apply ABM to general roles or categories (e.g., dictators, firms, consumers) rather than to one-of-a-kind persons.

## Failure modes named
- Believing a model must perfectly represent reality to be useful.
- Choosing agent resolution that is too fine or too coarse for the question at hand.
- Assuming agents must always represent individual humans rather than organizations, organisms, or other units.
- Using ABM to predict or explain the behavior of a specific, erratic individual.
- Applying ABM to questions better suited for ethnographic or deep psychological study.
