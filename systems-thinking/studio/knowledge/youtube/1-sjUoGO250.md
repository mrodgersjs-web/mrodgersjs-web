# Deliberate Practice: Achieve Mastery in Anything

Deliberate practice is a mindful, structured, and iterative process of isolating sub-skills, rehearsing them slowly and correctly in short, focused sessions, tracking fine-grained progress, and building routines (often with a coach) until a skill becomes automatic.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Decompose the target skill into distinct elements and define what success looks like for each one.
- Drill one element at a time in sequence rather than practicing everything at once.
- Keep a practice notebook: plan the session beforehand, then reflect afterward on what worked and what didn’t.
- Treat each session as an experiment to find ever-better ways to reach the goal.
- Practice slowly and correctly at first to build accurate muscle memory, then gradually raise speed.
- Cap session length at the duration that lets you stay fully focused.
- Switch practice style or activity every few minutes when long sessions are necessary to sustain attention.
- Eliminate or compress non-practice activities to maximize time on the actual skill.
- Track the smallest meaningful units of improvement to make progress visible and motivating.
- Study how experts practice behind the scenes, not just their final polished performances.
- Use repetition of the same deliberate motions to drive automaticity and peak performance over months and years.
- Anchor practice in a fixed daily routine so it happens regardless of motivation.
- Find a coach, teacher, or mentor who can reveal your potential and guide your direction.

## Failure modes named
- Moving too fast before the skill is correctly learned can internalize wrong movements with serious long-term consequences.
- Watching performance instead of studying practice routines misleads you about what creates expertise.
- Believing that mere practice is enough, while ignoring focused repetition of the same precise actions.
