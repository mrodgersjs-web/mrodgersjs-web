# System Archetypes

Catalog of recurring system structures and the outcomes they predict — pattern-matching for systems mapping.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Archetype = regularly seen arrangement of causal relationships and feedback loops that produce similar observable behavior over time (0:06–0:19).
- Use: recognizing an archetype yields fast understanding of an unfamiliar system and points to powerful intervention design (1:31–1:48, 5:24–5:33).
- **Limits to growth**: reinforcing growth engine peaks when a constraint binds; pushing the old action harder produces decline; the fix is removing/weakening the constraining cause, not more effort (1:51–2:23).
- **Shifting the burden**: two balancing loops attack the same symptom — a fast symptomatic fix and a delayed fundamental solution; quick relief erodes pressure for the fundamental fix and symptoms recur (2:31–3:16).
- **Tragedy of the commons**: shared unowned resource + actors whose profit loops drive escalation (two fishing companies on an open lake) → harvest exceeds regeneration → collapse of the stock and of everyone's profits (3:24–4:45).
- **Success to the successful / resource competition**: two activities draw from one limited pool; early success redirects resources, starving the other and "proving" the choice right; fix by decoupling or balancing the allocation (4:45–5:24).
- **Balancing process with delay**: delayed response to corrective action causes overshoot/undershoot; counter with patience or by speeding the system's reaction/feedback (1:08–1:29).
- Unsustainability pattern: internal negative synergies export externalities (entropy) into the environment; externalities accumulate until the environment can no longer support the system — climate, toxic-asset crises (0:21–1:05).

## Failure modes named
- Applying previous actions "more and more assertively" after growth stalls — produces the opposite of the desired state (2:08–2:20).
- Quick fixes that make symptoms vanish "at least for a while" while eroding the fundamental response (2:31–3:16).
- Open-access escalation where each actor's locally-rational loop destroys the shared stock (3:24–4:45).
- Acting on delayed feedback without perceiving the delay — overshooting the required correction (1:15–1:23).
