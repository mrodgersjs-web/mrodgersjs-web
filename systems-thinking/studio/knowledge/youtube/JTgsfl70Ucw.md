# If Light Has No Mass, Why Does Gravity Still Bend It?

Gravity bends light because gravity is not a force between masses but the curvature of spacetime itself, which determines the available paths that all moving things—including massless photons—must follow.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Model gravity as a Newtonian force: describe attraction as proportional to the product of two masses and inversely proportional to the square of distance, reliable for planets, tides, and the Moon.
- Stress-test an established framework with edge cases: ask how the model handles a massless photon passing near a massive object like the Sun.
- Treat light as a particle stream inside Newtonian mechanics: calculate an effective mass and predict a deflection, as Soldner did in 1801 to estimate ~0.84 arcseconds.
- Identify hidden geometric assumptions: recognize that Newtonian mechanics assumes flat Euclidean space where massive objects exert forces without altering the underlying geometry itself.
- Reframe gravity as spacetime curvature: replace the force-between-masses picture with the idea that mass-energy curves the geometry of spacetime.
- Trace geodesics: model light as following the straightest available path through curved spacetime, producing a deflection of ~1.75 arcseconds near the Sun.
- Use discrepancies to reveal missing structure: compare Soldner’s half-value with Einstein’s doubled value to see that the extra bending comes from the curvature of space itself, not from a new force.
- Apply bending as a measurement instrument: use gravitational lensing, Einstein rings, and dark-matter maps to study invisible mass and distant galaxies.
- Map theory regimes clearly: keep general relativity and quantum mechanics in their respective domains and mark the boundary where both must apply but currently conflict.
- Evaluate successor theories by testability: ask whether proposals like string theory or loop quantum gravity make distinguishable, observable predictions.
- Treat confirmed theories as provisional: read a theory’s singularities and incompatibilities as honest markers of the edge of its validity rather than as final answers.

## Failure modes named
- Treating a long-useful framework as complete “furniture” rather than as an approximation.
- Assuming “no rest mass” means gravity cannot affect an object.
- Mistaking internal consistency and correct derivation for truth when hidden assumptions remain unexamined.
- Confusing mathematical success with absence of foundational gaps.
- Reading untestable predictions from candidate theories as settled progress.
