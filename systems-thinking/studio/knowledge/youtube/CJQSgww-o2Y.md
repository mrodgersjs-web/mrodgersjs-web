# Stoicism for Difficult Decisions: How to Choose Without Regret | Stoicism

The video teaches a four-part Stoic framework for making difficult decisions by separating what is within our control, using the four cardinal virtues as criteria, and applying specific mental exercises to reduce anxiety and regret.

## Procedure / models distilled
- **Dichotomy of Control:** Before deciding, list what aspects of the situation are actually within your power and release attachment to the rest.
- **Preferred Indifferents:** Prefer outcomes like health, stability, and success without making your happiness depend on achieving them.
- **Four Cardinal Virtues as Filters:** Evaluate options against wisdom, courage, justice, and temperance.
- **Morning Reflection / Premeditatio Malorum:** Spend a few minutes each morning anticipating obstacles for each option and rehearsing a virtuous response.
- **View from Above:** Mentally zoom out from your city to the planet to cosmic space/time to right-size the decision and reveal what truly matters.
- **Negative Visualization:** Vividly imagine the worst realistic outcome of each option to discover your resilience and lower anxiety.
- **Inner Council:** Ask what respected role models would do, applying the four virtues to access wisdom beyond your immediate thinking.
- **Evening Review:** Before sleep, review the day’s decisions, note where virtue was shown or lacking, and set one improvement for tomorrow.
- **Needs vs. Desires / Voluntary Discomfort:** For financial and lifestyle choices, distinguish genuine needs from socially driven desires and rehearse living with less.
- **Career Virtue Check:** For work decisions, ask whether the choice comes from fear or wisdom and whether it expresses your virtues without tying self-worth to external success.
- **Relationship Response Check:** In relationship dilemmas, focus on your own just and wise response rather than trying to control the other person.
- **Bias Interrogation:** Pause to question whether you are seeing the situation clearly or reacting through sunk-cost thinking and emotion.
- **Decision Paralysis Release:** If stuck, imagine external outcomes removed and choose the option that better reflects your values.
- **Regret Reframe:** Treat the past as outside your control, accept you did your best with available information, extract the lesson, and refocus on the present.
- **Resilience Practice:** Build decision-making strength by regularly making small, value-aligned difficult choices and reviewing past decisions for lessons.

## Failure modes named
- **Anxiety from outcome attachment:** staking happiness on results outside your control.
- **Decision paralysis:** freezing between options because of fear of wrong outcomes.
- **Cognitive biases:** especially sunk-cost fallacy and emotionally reactive thinking.
- **Mistaking fear/anger for wisdom:** making major choices from defensive emotion rather than virtue.
- **Regret fixation:** dwelling on past decisions instead of acting on what you can control now.
- **Need-desire confusion:** confusing social pressure and wants with genuine needs, especially in financial and lifestyle choices.
- **Trying to control others:** attempting to manage other people’s feelings or reactions in relationship decisions.
