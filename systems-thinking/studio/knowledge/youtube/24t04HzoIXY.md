# How to build a future-proof AI thinking partner

Nick Milo argues that a future-proof AI thinking partner is built by keeping your knowledge in plain-text Markdown, feeding AI curated context files, and using the Imagine–Discern–Integrate guardrails so AI amplifies rather than replaces your own thinking.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Locate yourself on the AI-use continuum (avoiding, reluctantly, practically, enthusiastically) and aim for practical use—neither avoidance nor over-enthusiasm.
- Diagnose friction with the "big three" AI problems: AI thinking for you, AI overwhelming you, and AI failing you through generic responses or context rot.
- Watch for the three cognitive traps—cognitive debt, cognitive isolation, and cognitive surrender—and counter them with integrate and discern.
- Apply the IDI loop: Imagine what you want, Discern accuracy and value, and Integrate only what belongs in your system.
- Keep notes as owned plain-text Markdown files so they remain readable and portable across future apps and models.
- Curate small, high-quality context files/dossiers from your vault so AI works with your meaning rather than generic training data.
- Insert a "translation layer" between raw AI output and your notes to shrink volume, convert output into your own words, and preserve your voice.
- Use links and folders inside your vault to give AI a web of meaning and clean partitions to reason over.
- Consider running models locally (e.g., LM Studio) for privacy, accepting trade-offs such as slower speed and difficulty pointing AI at an entire folder.
- Use open adapters like MCP and CLI tools cautiously to connect AI to task managers, cloud code, or note apps, auditing trust and extra abstraction layers.
- Build lightweight agentic summaries such as a "morning briefing" that scans tasks and notes and returns only the view you need.
- Store binary attachments (PDFs, Word docs, images) in a separate folder so the linked-notes vault stays lean, portable, and AI-readable.

## Failure modes named
- AI-generated answers quietly replacing your critical thinking.
- The 1:100 word-ratio overwhelm from long, low-signal replies.
- Generic AI responses and context rot in long or unfocused chats.
- Cognitive debt from accumulating unprocessed AI outputs.
- Cognitive isolation from substituting AI for human challenge, surprise, and collaboration.
- Cognitive surrender: accepting AI answers when tired without verification.
- Vendor/app lock-in from opaque formats that break future access to your knowledge.
- Vault bloat and backup friction from mixing binary attachments into the note system.
