# Cindy Kelly Introduces the Atomic Heritage Foundation

Cindy Kelly describes the founding and work of the Atomic Heritage Foundation, a nonprofit created to preserve Manhattan Project sites, documents, and oral histories before they are lost.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Found a mission-specific nonprofit to advocate for preserving a dispersed national heritage.
- Locate an advocacy organization near key government decision-makers to shape policy.
- Use insider institutional knowledge to identify threats to historical sites before they disappear.
- Treat environmental-remediation programs as potential erasure of historically significant structures.
- Start preservation with a flagship site, then expand into a national network of related places.
- Conserve both iconic industrial facilities and the residential spaces of leaders and workers.
- Commission authoritative feasibility studies before asking for formal legislative designation.
- Advance policy through staged milestones: expert report → agency study → congressional action.
- Publish accessible anthologies and site guides to build public awareness and visitation.
- Partner across federal agencies, national laboratories, state/local governments, and community nonprofits.
- Record oral histories from aging participants before firsthand knowledge is lost.
- Diversify funding across private donations, government grants, and private foundation support.

## Failure modes named
- The "cleanup-as-demolition" trap: contamination remediation can level structures that carry historical significance.
- Heritage loss through delay: waiting until sites are already scheduled for destruction before acting.
- Vanishing participant memory: first-person historical knowledge disappears as witnesses age and die.
