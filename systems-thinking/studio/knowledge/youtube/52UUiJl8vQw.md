# Immune system - Introduction, Innate Immunity & Inflammation: Medical-Surgical | @LevelUpRN

A foundational nursing overview of immune defense, contrasting non-specific innate immunity with specific acquired immunity and detailing the three stages of inflammation.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Innate immunity is the body's inborn, immediate defense against all antigens, not just one specific pathogen.
- Physical barriers such as intact skin prevent microorganisms from entering the body.
- Chemical defenses such as stomach acid destroy pathogens that are ingested.
- Mucus traps pathogens so they cannot infect tissues.
- Phagocytic cells engulf and eliminate invading organisms as part of innate immunity.
- Acquired immunity develops when B and T cells direct antibody production against specific antigens.
- Inflammation is the body's protective reaction to injury, disease, or tissue damage.
- Stage 1 of inflammation presents as warmth, erythema, edema, decreased function, and/or pain at the injury site.
- Stage 2 of inflammation is marked by white blood cells killing microorganisms and exudate accumulating with dead tissue cells.
- Stage 3 of inflammation ends with damaged tissue being replaced by scar tissue.

## Failure modes named
- Treating innate immunity as antigen-specific rather than general and immediate.
- Viewing inflammation purely as harmful rather than as a protective three-stage healing process.
- Confusing innate immunity, which uses barriers and phagocytes, with acquired immunity, which relies on B and T cell antibodies.
