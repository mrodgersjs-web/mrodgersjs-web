# Brutally Honest Advice About Web Design in 9 mins

A veteran web designer distills 15 counterintuitive principles for building business websites that convert by prioritizing clarity, trust, speed, and customer-centricity over beauty and self-expression.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat every homepage as a scanning surface: bold key points, use short paragraphs, and make the next step impossible to miss.
- Apply the halo effect: a professional-looking first half-second automatically elevates perceived product/service quality; a cheap look creates lasting skepticism.
- Replace generic stock imagery with real, imperfect photos and human touches to signal authenticity and build trust.
- Optimize for clarity first: the site must communicate what you do and how to buy within seconds, because clarity is good design.
- Radically edit copy: cut it in half, then cut it in half again; every word must earn its place.
- Publish pricing or a clear starting-from number to filter prospects and attract buyers in the right budget range.
- Differentiate visually and tonally from industry templates; a distinct point of view prevents commoditization.
- Keep load times under three seconds by removing bloated plugins, uncompressed images, and unnecessary animations.
- Use animation as subtle reinforcement (hover states, natural fade-ins), not as the main attraction; over-the-top motion distracts and slows conversions.
- Design for the customer, not the owner: let visitor data—clicks, drop-offs, page reads—drive decisions.
- Consolidate pages into fewer, higher-impact paths; every extra page adds friction and decision fatigue.
- Reframe the About page as a credibility-and-transformation proof point: metrics, real faces, and customer outcomes, not a resume.
- Build in repulsion deliberately: clearly state who you work with, what you charge, and who you don't serve.
- Treat launch as the starting line, not the finish: commit to ongoing testing, optimization, and traffic growth.
- Sell the result, not the deliverable: show the post-purchase transformation for the customer's life or business.

## Failure modes named
- Walls of text that assume visitors will read instead of skim.
- Cheap or amateur visuals that create a negative halo coloring every later judgment.
- Generic stock photos that trigger skepticism about what else on the site is fake.
- "Award-winning" design that sacrifices clarity and conversion.
- Bloated, essay-style copy that hides the core value proposition.
- Hidden pricing that wastes time with unqualified leads.
- Clone-like industry sites that force price-based competition.
- Designing for the owner's taste, friends, or brand guidelines rather than the customer's problem.
- Page creep: adding more pages that confuse instead of guide.
- About pages written as self-congratulating resumes instead of trust-building proof.
- Trying to appeal to everyone and thereby resonating with no one.
- Launch-and-abandon: treating the website as finished instead of a living system.
- Feature lists instead of outcome-oriented messaging.
