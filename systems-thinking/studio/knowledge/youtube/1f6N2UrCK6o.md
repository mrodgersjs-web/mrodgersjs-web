# Unlocking Your Intuition: How to Solve Hard Problems Easily

The video frames intuition as fast, unconscious pattern recognition built from prior knowledge, and gives a strategy for training it by mapping problem components to solution components while keeping slow reasoning in the loop.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat intuition as a sense: it instantly categorizes a new problem by matching it to patterns you have already absorbed (e.g., "smells like greedy" or "feels like binary search").
- Understand the two-system model: the fast brain supplies intuition; the slow brain supplies reasoning, proof, and detail-filling; use both together.
- Improve intuition by increasing the quantity and quality of prior knowledge you have seen, since intuition can only offer "what worked before."
- Use the mental-mapping strategy: break problems and their solutions into small, memorable components and create associations between problem cues and solution ideas.
- When building the mapping, include solution meta-information such as how the solver thought of it, useful questions, simplifications, common thought processes, and proof sketches.
- Try to understand why each association works; this makes future matches more accurate and exposes missing associations.
- Practice in two modes: deep problem solving strengthens reasoning and explains the "why," while rapid exposure to problems plus solutions strengthens intuition faster.
- For every problem you do, explicitly create or review the mental mapping so your intuition has more patterns to recognize later.
- Test your intuition by attempting to guess only high-level ideas for a new problem, then verifying with justification, examples, or a full solution.
- Work on problems above your current level to create new patterns; easier work mainly consolidates speed, which comes naturally with exposure.
- Justify intuitive guesses, even informally, so they do not become semi-blind guesses and so you can catch misleading directions.
- Build separate logical/proof skills so you can ignore wrong intuitions when they strongly pull you away from a valid but unintuitive path.
- Recognize that intuition and insight are different: intuition retrieves old patterns, while insight generates genuinely new ideas.
- Motivation and passion strongly affect learning efficiency; if you do not care about the domain, find a way to create genuine interest because a motivated brain absorbs far more.

## Failure modes named
- Relying on intuition as a full solution rather than as a shallow first step that still needs slow-brain verification.
- Trusting intuition without justification or proof, leading to wrong but confident guesses.
- Getting trapped by a wrong intuition because it is hard to tune out a strong gut feeling.
- Letting intuition outpace reasoning ability so you feel lost on the hardest problems.
- Applying intuition to completely novel situations where no prior pattern exists; use logic or insight instead.
- Blaming fixed traits like IQ or genetics for lack of progress, which removes the motivation to practice.
- Using only the "read solutions without solving" shortcut and neglecting normal practice, proof practice, and deep understanding.
