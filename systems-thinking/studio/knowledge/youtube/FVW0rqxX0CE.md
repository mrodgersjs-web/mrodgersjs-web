# 152. Prediction Markets & Superforecasting | THUNK

Prediction markets and superforecasting turn uncalibrated opinions into stake-weighted probability estimates, then refine crowd forecasts with de-biasing and track-record scoring.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat every prediction as a probability distribution, not a binary claim or rhetorical certainty.
- Attach real stakes to forecasts to expose the gap between stated confidence and genuine belief.
- Use prediction markets as exchanges that aggregate dispersed beliefs into a live probability index for an event.
- Let market equilibrium adjust contract prices as participants bet against perceived mispricing.
- Define contracts with precise, verifiable resolution criteria so outcomes can be judged cleanly.
- Run internal prediction markets to harvest frontline employee knowledge for business decisions.
- Identify superforecasters by tracking historical accuracy rather than relying on credentials or access.
- Weight individual forecasts by proven track record to improve aggregate predictions.
- Apply de-biasing techniques so estimates follow evidence instead of wishful thinking or overconfidence.
- Combine market aggregation with algorithmic recalibration and individual scoring for hybrid forecasting engines.
- Practice forecasting with play-money platforms to build skill without financial risk.

## Failure modes named
- Selection bias among market participants can skew probabilities away from the broader population.
- Ambiguous or hard-to-verify contract definitions make resolution contentious and unreliable.
- Prediction markets can be manipulated for profit or to influence real-world events.
- Blind trust in the “wisdom of the crowd” ignores imperfect information and biased participation.
- Conversational overconfidence is mistaken for literal calibrated probability.
- Confusing experts with access to data for accurate forecasters ignores the separate skill of probability estimation.
