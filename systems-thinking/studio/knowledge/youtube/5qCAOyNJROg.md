# simple evolution strategy(ES) and covariance adaptation

An intuitive walkthrough of a simple evolution strategy that samples from a multivariate normal distribution, updates its mean via elite selection, and attempts to adapt its covariance matrix—revealing why naive adaptation prematurely collapses exploration.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Evolution strategy follows a survival-of-the-fittest loop: generate a population, rank individuals by fitness, select elites, and use those elites to produce the next generation.
- λ (lambda) denotes the number of offspring generated per generation; μ (mu) denotes the number of elites/parents retained.
- In (μ+λ)-ES, offspring join the existing population so parents can compete against their children.
- In (μ,λ)-ES, parents are discarded regardless of fitness, mimicking natural death and helping the search escape local optima.
- New individuals are generated by sampling from a multivariate normal distribution parameterized by a mean vector and a covariance matrix.
- The diagonal entries of the covariance matrix are the variances of individual variables; the off-diagonal entries are the covariances between pairs of variables.
- Variance controls how spread out the search distribution is along one variable's axis; covariance controls shearing, i.e., how strongly two variables tend to increase or decrease together.
- A simple ES samples λ individuals, evaluates their fitness, selects the top μ elites, and sets the new distribution mean to the average of those elites.
- In the simplest ES, the covariance matrix is held fixed, producing a round, isotropic search distribution that may not match the elongated shape of a useful search region.
- To adapt the covariance, treat each elite as a step away from the previous generation's mean and compute the covariance of those step vectors.
- The covariance update is conceptually the average of (elite − previous_mean)(elite − previous_mean)^T over the elites, so the distribution shape aligns with directions that produced high fitness.
- The mean update is the average of the current elites' positions, shifting the search distribution toward better-performing regions.

## Failure modes named
- Premature collapse: the adapted covariance matrix becomes extremely thin, leaving almost no exploration in any direction.
- Self-reinforcing shrinkage: once the distribution shrinks, sampled elites cluster closer together, so the estimated covariance shrinks even further in a positive feedback loop.
