# Transfer of Learning!

Transfer of learning is the act of recognizing that a new situation is related to prior experience and then applying what you already know, and it can appear as positive, negative, near, or far transfer.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Recognize when a new situation resembles something already experienced.
- Apply existing knowledge or skills to the new situation.
- Positive transfer: use prior learning that is related to the new task to make the new task easier.
- Identify shared structure between contexts to accelerate learning a related task.
- Negative transfer: notice when prior learning interferes with or hinders a new task.
- Audit for interference when the new context has conflicting rules, sounds, or procedures.
- Near (specific) transfer: solve new problems that are very similar or nearly identical to previously practiced ones.
- Use repeated, similar exercises so learners can recognize surface-level patterns.
- Far (general) transfer: take an abstract lesson from one context and apply it to a very different context.
- Extract general principles from stories, fables, or cases so they can be reused in unrelated domains.

## Failure modes named
- Negative transfer: prior learning makes a new task harder instead of easier.
- Interference from mismatched prior knowledge, such as a Visayan speaker struggling with English vowel sounds and pronouncing words awkwardly.
- Assuming similarity where it does not exist, leading to misapplied near transfer.
