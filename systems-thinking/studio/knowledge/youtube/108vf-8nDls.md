# How Can We Harness the Power of Nature to Enhance Ecosystem Resilience?︱Adriana de Palma

Adriana de Palma argues that biodiversity loss and climate change are interconnected crises driven largely by agriculture and global inequality, and that addressing them requires nature-based solutions combined with holistic, cross-disciplinary collaboration and science communication.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat biodiversity loss and climate change as coupled systems rather than separate problems because they share drivers and solutions.
- Map agriculture—especially livestock farming—as a primary driver of biodiversity loss through land-use change, deforestation, pasture expansion, soil damage, and displacement of invertebrates and other species.
- Account for agriculture as a dual polluter: a major source of greenhouse gas emissions and water pollution.
- Recognize nature as an active climate regulator at multiple scales, from rainforests to local urban trees and soil ecosystems.
- Use nature-based solutions such as tree cover for urban heat mitigation and earthworm-rich soils for drainage and flood protection.
- Reframe food systems as a leverage point where biodiversity, climate, diet, health, economics, and social equity intersect.
- Bring together diverse disciplines—biodiversity science, climate science, health, social sciences, economics, and labor studies—to design systemic interventions.
- Set the goal as a "healthy and happy Earth" for all life, not a narrow single-metric target.
- Collaborate with artists, communicators, and educators to translate data and evidence into actionable knowledge for individuals and decision-makers.
- Identify decision-makers at every scale, from individuals to political leaders, as audiences for evidence-based action.

## Failure modes named
- Siloing biodiversity loss and climate change as independent problems.
- Relying on single-discipline scientists to solve complex, systemic issues like food systems.
- Pursuing solutions that optimize only for biodiversity or only for climate at the expense of the other.
- Failing to communicate scientific evidence to the decision-makers and communities who can act on it.
