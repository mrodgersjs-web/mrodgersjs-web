# 6  Powerful Psychological tricks that should be illegal //Robert Cialdini - PRE - suasion

Robert Cialdini's Pre-Suasion shows that the moments before a message—visual, verbal, and identity primes—can shift attention and perceived identity so powerfully that people change behavior while believing they made a conscious, independent choice.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Prime the recipient's attention before the request by exposing them to symbols associated with your goal (flowers for romance, guitar case for creativity/culture, clouds for comfort, coins for price).
- Anchor price or cost magnitude with an extreme, joking reference point just before stating the real price so the real amount feels smaller.
- Invite the recipient to adopt a desirable identity label immediately before the ask so they act consistently with that label.
- Establish unity and shared identity—especially family-like bonds or in-group membership—before delivering the message to increase trust and compliance.
- Use family-framed benefits to motivate action, even when the actual benefit is trivial.
- Ask for "advice" rather than "opinion" or "feedback" to create a sense of partnership and forward movement toward your goal.
- Time important requests for right after the recipient has received praise, achieved something great, or is otherwise in a positive, capable mindset.
- Prime your own environment with images, phrases, music, or ambient media that put you in the mental state required for your task.
- Before a difficult ask, remind the other person of a past cooperative act or shared project goal to shift them from adversarial to cooperative mindset.
- Treat the message as the seed and the pre-message context as the soil: even a great seed fails if the soil is not prepared.
- Delay decisions when you notice someone has just primed you with a small favor, so you don't reciprocate with an oversized yes.

## Failure modes named
- Believing you are immune to subconscious priming while freely attributing decisions to conscious logic.
- Using influence tactics unethically, which tends to backfire as people around you copy the manipulative behavior.
- Saying yes immediately after receiving a small favor, leading to regret when the requested favor is far larger.
- Dismissing environmental primes (wall phrases, background images, music) as silly while they silently shape behavior.
- Asking for feedback or opinion when seeking cooperation, because those words create psychological distance instead of unity.
- Failing to prepare the "soil" of attention and trust before delivering an important message.
