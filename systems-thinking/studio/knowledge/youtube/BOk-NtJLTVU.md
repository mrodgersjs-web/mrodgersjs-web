# Travis Luther | The Strength of Weak Ties - Using Online Social Networking to Pocket Social Capital

A sociologist-turned-legal-marketer argues that lawyers should use online social networks to cultivate weak ties, accumulate social capital, and gain visibility without relying solely on paid advertising.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat sociology as the foundation of marketing: understand why groups behave, not just how to persuade.
- Build social capital by accumulating durable relationships with people, institutions, and knowledge that can be drawn on later.
- Recognize that interpersonal ties are either strong, weak, or absent, and that strength depends on time spent, emotional intensity, intimacy/mutual confiding, and reciprocity.
- Cultivate the ideal weak tie for online networking: low time, low emotion, low intimacy, but high reciprocity.
- Use weak ties to reduce dependence on primary groups for new information and to feed novel information back into your core network.
- Create a Facebook business page separate from your personal page, and lock down privacy settings on the personal side.
- Publish valuable, reciprocity-driven content consistently across platforms rather than treating them as pure advertising channels.
- Cross-post links back to your website or blog to improve organic search engine rankings.
- Claim a custom Facebook URL once you reach 25 likes so your page matches other marketing materials.
- Target peer professionals and similar-but-unknown contacts on Twitter and LinkedIn to expand weak-tie reach.
- Use syndication tools to broadcast one piece of content to multiple platforms without extra labor.
- Decide in advance on posting frequency, owner, and schedule so consistency becomes a system rather than a mood.
- Monitor the search ecosystem: Google dominates, but Bing/Yahoo share an algorithm and increasingly surface fresh social content.

## Failure modes named
- Assuming money alone produces success, while ignoring the social capital that actually enables mobility.
- Over-relying on strong ties and primary groups, which rarely provide novel information.
- Mixing personal and business Facebook identities without privacy controls, exposing personal life to potential clients.
- Posting in bursts and then going silent, which collapses discoverability and wastes setup effort.
- Pouring budget into pay-per-click legal ads instead of building organic SEO and social authority.
- Treating social platforms as one-way advertising channels rather than reciprocity networks.
