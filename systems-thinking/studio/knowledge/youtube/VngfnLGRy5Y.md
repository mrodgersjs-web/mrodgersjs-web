# Social Networks, Bridging Gaps and Your Reputation at Work

Managerial value and reputation in organizations are shaped by social network position: brokers who bridge structural holes between specialized silos create disproportionate value, while reputations are built and sustained through gossip networks rather than raw competence alone.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Map structural holes: identify gaps in communication between specialized departments or roles where no one naturally connects.
- Broker across holes: position yourself as the link between disconnected groups to capture and create value.
- Recognize specialization creates ignorance: experts often become unaware of the implications of their work for other areas of the firm or market.
- Leverage gossip as network-building: treat informal talk as the human equivalent of primate grooming that builds and maintains relationships.
- Cultivate being interesting, not just competent: reputations spread when people want to discuss you.
- Use the "bright, brief, gone" principle: deliver strong work concisely and leave, so stories about you exaggerate positively in your absence.
- Build a talkable reputation: ensure multiple people discuss you, since reputations that persist require ongoing conversation.
- Avoid the invisibility trap: doing good work alone is insufficient if no network carries stories about you.

## Failure modes named
- Structural holes in communication between specialized departments or roles.
- Experts becoming "idiot savants" who do not see cross-functional implications.
- Capable people being overlooked because they are boring rather than interesting.
- Mediocre people being promoted simply because they are interesting to talk about.
- Reputation decay when too few people talk about you.
- The "dead at the desk" trap: excellent work goes unnoticed because no one notices or discusses you.
