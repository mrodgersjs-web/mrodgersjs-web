# Functional Organisational Structure - A-Z of business terminology

A functional organizational structure divides employees into specialized departments with their own resources and targets, operating as a centralized system where information flows up to senior management for decisions and back down through middle management for implementation.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Segment the organization into distinct functions or departments based on specialization.
- Assign each employee to one specific department to clarify responsibility and accountability.
- Equip each function with its own people, resources, budgets, and performance targets.
- Model the functional structure as a centralized hierarchy rather than a decentralized one.
- Pass operational data upward from the functional level to top management.
- Have top management collate and analyze incoming data to inform planning and control decisions.
- Feed plans and decisions downward from top management to middle management.
- Task middle management with implementing the decisions handed down from above.
- Employ and train specialist staff within each function to build deep expertise.
- Standardize systems and procedures across specialist teams to make control easier.
- Use economies of scale and senior-management experience to drive efficiency across the organization.

## Failure modes named
- Specialized functions reduce job variety and limit job enrichment for employees.
- Rigid procedures constrain employee flair, creativity, and ingenuity.
- Centralization increases bureaucracy and form filling.
- Equipment purchases and procedures become compromises between competing functional requirements.
