# Why 90% of AI Startups Will Be Dead by December

Ash Maurya walks through the ten most common Lean Canvas mistakes that kill startups—especially AI startups in 2026—and pairs each with a concrete fix.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define your **minimum success criteria** before filling out the canvas: the smallest 3-year outcome that would make the project worthwhile, then align every box to that destination.
- Make metrics actionable by pairing a number with a time frame (e.g., "$10,000 MRR by month six") rather than using generic labels like "revenue" or "engagement."
- Replace broad customer segments with a specific **early adopter** you could locate and call next week; if you cannot name where to find ten of them, the segment is too broad.
- Avoid the **local maxima trap** by exploring multiple business-model variations early instead of committing to the first internally consistent canvas.
- Run **problem-discovery interviews** and use root-cause analysis to move from surface symptoms (slow, expensive) to specific, picture-able problems people will pay to solve.
- Be honest in the **unfair advantage** box: write "Not yet" if needed, then state what you are building toward (network effects, proprietary data, deep domain expertise).
- Anchor the **unique value proposition** to a specific customer and a specific problem; it must be both different from and significantly better than existing alternatives on something that matters.
- Stress-test the canvas against **actual runway** by working backward from minimum success criteria through customers, price, and channel to see if the path is realistic before time or money runs out.
- List two channels: one non-scalable channel to land the first 10–20 customers, and one scalable-channel hypothesis to grow beyond that; avoid the "channel buffet."
- Validate **monetizable pain** by stress-testing the existing-alternatives box first; current spend in dollars, time, or frustration is your real competition and demand signal.
- Use the **VIBE framework** to counter innovator's bias: Validate the problem, Interview five people for evidence, Build a demo (not an MVP), then Experiment and measure before committing to code.

## Failure modes named
- No way to measure success / building without a destination
- Marketing to everyone / customer segment is "anyone"
- Stuck on a local maximum / premature narrowness
- Problems that aren't specific enough / surface-level symptoms
- No real unfair advantage story / mistaking passion, service, or tech for defensibility
- Weak unique value proposition / different but not meaningfully better
- Not enough runway / model takes longer to validate than resources allow
- No scalable path to customers / "channel buffet"
- No monetizable pain / blank or weak existing-alternatives box
- Innovator's bias / falling in love with the solution
- Solution-first or build-first canvas
- Zombie startup: looks alive but is going nowhere
- AI wrapper / building on rented land
- Vibe-coding trap: shipping a polished app for a problem no one will pay to solve
