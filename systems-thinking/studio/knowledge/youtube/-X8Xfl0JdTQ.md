# Karl Popper, Science, & Pseudoscience: Crash Course Philosophy #8

Karl Popper argues that science advances by making risky, falsifiable predictions and seeking disconfirmation, whereas pseudoscience protects itself by interpreting any evidence as confirmation.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Frame a theory as a conjecture that makes risky predictions capable of being proven false.
- Treat attempted falsification, not confirmation, as the genuine test of a scientific theory.
- Check whether a theory rules out possible observations; the more prohibitive the theory, the stronger it is.
- Distinguish forward-looking predictions from backward-looking accommodation of existing data.
- Ask of any claim: what evidence could refute it? If none exists, it is not scientific.
- Deliberately seek disconfirming evidence rather than collecting confirming instances.
- Accept a theory as provisionally supported only after rigorous attempts to disprove it have failed.
- Abandon or revise a theory once it has been falsified instead of immunizing it.
- Hold beliefs as probable and contingent on current data, never as certain or closed.
- Maintain an open, critical stance toward existing beliefs to move closer to truth.
- Use falsifiability as a demarcation criterion between science and pseudoscience.
- Evaluate explanations by whether they risk refutation or can accommodate any outcome.
- Treat every falsified belief as useful progress toward a more accurate understanding.

## Failure modes named
- Confirmation bias: searching only for evidence that supports a pre-existing theory.
- Accommodation trap: explaining past data in multiple contradictory ways to preserve a theory.
- Immunization: reinterpreting evidence so a theory becomes unfalsifiable.
- Irrefutable theories masquerading as science because they cannot be tested.
- Certainty-induced closed-mindedness that blocks belief revision.
- The Santa Claus problem: easy confirmation makes almost any belief seem supported.
- Mistaking plentiful confirmation for strong evidence when predictions carry no risk.
