# Re-Broadcast - Using PreMortem Analysis to Make Better Decisions with Gary A. Klein

Gary Klein explains how experienced decision-makers use pattern recognition and mental simulation under uncertainty, and how premortem analysis surfaces hidden failure modes before a plan is launched.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Naturalistic decision making: study how people actually decide in real, high-stakes, time-pressured environments rather than idealized laboratory tasks.
- Recognition-primed decision model: experts typically generate one plausible course of action first, not a set of options to be compared.
- Pattern matching: years of experience create a library of prototypes that direct attention, set expectancies, define goals, and suggest the most likely workable action.
- Mental simulation: evaluate the single generated course of action by imagining how it will play out, checking for problems rather than weighing alternatives.
- Anomaly detection: violated expectancies act as a wake-up call that the situation may be different from the matched pattern and needs re-diagnosis.
- Premortem core move: tell participants the plan has already failed, then ask them to identify what caused the failure.
- Reframe meeting dynamics: shifting from "any concerns?" to "the plan failed" reverses social pressure and licenses people to raise unpopular risks.
- Private generation first: give participants individual time to write failure causes before group sharing to reduce conformity and anchoring.
- Leader models candor: the leader volunteers a legitimate, non-frivolous concern first to lower the cost for others to speak up.
- Stretch failure scenarios: allow exaggerated or provocative future failure states; the backward causal chain often exposes real vulnerabilities.
- Premortem inaction too: apply the method to the decision to do nothing, not only to active plans, to reveal hidden costs of delay.
- Build richer mental models: treat mistakes as learning opportunities by becoming curious instead of critical, which encourages disclosure and growth.
- Avoid punitive error policies: "zero tolerance" cultures drive errors underground and make systems less safe over time.

## Failure modes named
- Pregnancy with the plan: emotional attachment to a chosen course makes critique feel impossible once the decision is conceived.
- Demand characteristics in normal meetings: social pressure suppresses dissent when people are asked generically for problems or reservations.
- "Nobody could have seen this coming": so-called black swan events are often imagined by someone beforehand but ignored, sidelined, or dismissed.
- Monday morning quarterbacking dismissal: foresight exercises are wrongly accused of being hindsight, discouraging proactive risk work.
- Zero-tolerance error regimes: punitive responses to mistakes cause concealment, blame deflection, and loss of organizational learning.
