# Welcome - Dynamical Systems | Intro Lecture

An introductory overview of a dynamical systems course that treats ordinary differential equations geometrically, emphasizing qualitative phase-space behavior over exact solutions and progressing from one-dimensional systems to chaos and discrete maps.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Adopt a geometric, qualitative approach to ordinary differential equations: ask how solutions evolve, where they originate, and where they end up in phase space.
- Map the macroscopic structures of phase space before chasing exact solutions.
- Progress from simple one-dimensional systems, to two-dimensional systems, then to three- and higher-dimensional systems where chaos appears.
- After continuous chaotic systems, study discrete-time iterative processes as an alternative window into dynamics.
- Use bifurcation analysis to track how qualitative behavior changes as parameters are varied.
- Recognize periodic and quasi-periodic motion as fundamental qualitative regimes.
- Anchor theory in physical examples such as the pendulum, chemical reactions, and problems from physics and engineering.
- Combine calculus tools (differentiation, integration, Taylor expansions) with linear algebra and geometric reasoning.
- Treat this course as a geometric continuation of ODE fundamentals: existence/uniqueness, linear equations, and series solutions.
- Read alongside Steven Strogatz's textbook as the primary reference, using lectures to add motivation and extra perspective.

## Failure modes named
- Treating dynamical systems as "just differential equations" and neglecting the geometric spaces-and-flows perspective.
- Prioritizing exact solutions over qualitative, macroscopic phase-space properties.
- Beginning the course without basic calculus skills or the ODE fundamentals covered in a prerequisite series.
- Dismissing physical applications and trying to learn the theory in pure abstraction.

02 frame - establishes the scope, geometric lens, prerequisite path, and dimensional progression map for projects involving nonlinear dynamics and differential-equation models.
