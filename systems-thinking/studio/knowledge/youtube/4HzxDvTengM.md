# Culture, Risk Management, and SMS: Don Arendt on The FAA's Experience with Safety Management Systems

Don Arendt argues that a Safety Management System only works when an organization treats safety as the deliberate, ongoing management of risk and cultivates a tangible, learning-oriented safety culture rather than a checklist program.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define safety operationally as “how well risk is managed,” not as freedom from harm; deliberately understand, control, and accept residual risk before approving an operation.
- Treat SMS as an ongoing practice and way of thinking, not as a “thing,” manual on a shelf, or set of checkboxes to complete.
- Use Safety Risk Management (SRM) as a predictive design phase: describe the system, identify hazards, assess severity and likelihood, decide whether the risk is acceptable, and either change the design or formally accept it before operation.
- Use Safety Assurance (SA) as a continuous monitoring loop: process controls, audits, employee reports, investigations, and data sense-making to confirm that risk controls remain effective.
- Build safety culture as a tangible environment, not an abstract ideal, by cultivating reporting, just, flexible, informed, and learning subcultures so safe behavior becomes automatic.
- Place safety accountability with line leadership and the CEO; the safety office facilitates, but every manager’s performance must be judged partly by safety performance.
- Practice high-reliability habits: be preoccupied with failure, sensitive to operations, reluctant to simplify, listen to small signs, and defer to expertise.
- Treat audits and employee reporting as learning tools, not score-keeping games; a no-defect audit is acceptable only when it reflects genuine confidence from monitoring.
- Use investigations to learn about system gaps and control failures, not merely to assign blame or confirm regulatory compliance.
- Recognize that SMS maturity ends when the system is no longer needed separately—when risk management and safety thinking become simply “the way we do work.”

## Failure modes named
- “SMS as a thing” / checklist mentality: inspecting the manual instead of the thinking behind it.
- The “palliative approach” of telling organizations they already do this under another name, which accepts the current accident-rate plateau.
- Treating “safety culture” as an abstract dumping ground or touchy-feely excuse for anything hard to measure.
- Using “risk management,” “data-driven,” and “safety culture” as buzzwords on documents that lack substance.
- Defining safety as risk-free or “freedom from harm,” which ignores that non-participants and bystanders can still be harmed.
- Turning audits into gaming or performance theater instead of learning opportunities.
- “Ticket and flick it” compliance: ticking boxes and then discarding the risk thinking.
- Oversimplifying and missing small warning signs because the organization is “used to success.”
- Believing the safety department is “in charge of safety,” instead of line management and leadership.
- Claiming a “whiz-bang SMS” or “great safety culture” while the underlying behaviors and processes are missing.
