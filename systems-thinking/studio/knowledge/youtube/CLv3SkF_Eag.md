# Positive and Negative Feedback Loops

Mr. Andersen explains that negative feedback loops stabilize a system around a target set point, while positive feedback loops amplify a change and move the system away from that point, using examples from body temperature, fruit ripening, childbirth, and blood glucose regulation.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define homeostasis as a stable internal environment that organisms maintain using feedback loops.
- In a negative feedback loop, the system moves above or below a target set point and mechanisms push it back toward that point.
- In temperature regulation, the hypothalamus senses changes and triggers sweating and vasodilation to cool the body, or goosebumps and vasoconstriction to warm it.
- Distinguish ectotherms, whose internal temperature matches the environment, from endotherms, which maintain a constant internal temperature at a higher energy cost.
- In a positive feedback loop, a change is amplified and the system moves farther away from the target set point.
- Fruit ripening spreads through ethylene gas: ripe fruit releases ethylene, nearby fruit detect it and ripen, and they release more ethylene in turn.
- Childbirth relies on positive feedback: pressure of the baby's head on the cervix causes contractions, which increase pressure and cause stronger contractions.
- Blood glucose is regulated by pancreatic beta cells secreting insulin when glucose is high.
- Insulin lowers blood glucose by triggering cells to take it in and the liver to store it as glycogen.
- When blood glucose drops too low, pancreatic alpha cells secrete glucagon.
- Glucagon raises blood glucose by triggering the liver to break down glycogen and release glucose.
- Type I diabetes is a failure of beta cells, so no insulin is produced and blood glucose keeps rising.
- Type II diabetes is insulin resistance: cells stop responding to insulin after chronic high glucose, often tied to diet and inactivity.
- Diabetes management replaces or augments the failed loop through insulin injections or insulin pumps.

## Failure modes named
- Type I diabetes: autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells eliminates insulin secretion, causing blood glucose to rise unchecked and potentially leading to coma or death.
- Type II diabetes: chronic high glucose, obesity, and inactivity cause cells to become insulin resistant, so glucose stays in the blood.
- Any alteration or mistake in a feedback loop can break homeostasis and produce serious pathology.
