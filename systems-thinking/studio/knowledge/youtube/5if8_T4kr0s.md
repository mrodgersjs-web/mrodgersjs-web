# Learning & Transfer of Training Defined - Training & Development Lecture 4 Part 1 (Full Version)

The lecture defines learning as an experience-driven change in KSAOs, behavior, or performance, and transfer of training as the post-training application of that learning to the job, shaped by internal learner conditions and external environmental conditions.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Training effectiveness requires both **learning** (during training) and **transfer of training** (post-training application).
- Define **learning** as a measurable change in KSAOs, behaviors, or performance brought about by experience or intense practice.
- Use three cognitive learning strategies: **rehearsal** (repetitive review), **organizing** (finding similarities and trends), and **elaboration** (relating new content to prior knowledge).
- Define **transfer of training** as trainees applying what they learned to their actual jobs after the training event.
- Track **learning** within the training program; evaluate **transfer** by collecting performance data from the work environment.
- Aim for two transfer outcomes: **generalization** (applying training to a similar but non-identical work context) and **maintenance** (continued use of the skill or knowledge over time).
- Ensure **internal conditions** for learning: prerequisites, meaningful content, active processing, observation of correct behaviors, and opportunities to practice.
- Support memory by attending to how information is perceived, stored, and later recalled.
- Ensure **external conditions** in both training and work environments: ample practice opportunities, constructive feedback, and social support from managers and colleagues.
- Recognize that manager and peer support includes speaking positively about training, encouraging attendance, and asking what others learned.
- Design the work environment to demand and reinforce use of new KSAOs; without workplace support, training investment is lost.

## Failure modes named
- Training is wasted when the work environment never asks trainees to use the new skill or knowledge.
- Transfer fails when the workplace does not support, facilitate, or encourage learning and application.
- Skills and knowledge decay through lack of use, undermining maintenance ("use it or lose it").
- Generalization fails when trainees cannot bridge the gap between the training context and the different on-the-job context.
