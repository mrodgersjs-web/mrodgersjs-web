# Superforescasting: How to be good at predicting the future?

A review of Philip Tetlock’s *Superforecasting* arguing that while most experts are no better than chance, some forecasters consistently outperform by breaking problems apart, examining biases, and updating beliefs continuously.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat forecasting as a skill that can be improved, not an innate gift.
- Break complex questions into their constituent parts to make them tractable.
- Examine your own biases before forming a forecast.
- Identify “superforecasters” by their long-term track records, not by credentials alone.
- Use the famous Tetlock finding that average expert predictions in politics and economics are roughly as accurate as a dart-throwing chimpanzee as a baseline for skepticism.
- Update beliefs and forecasts constantly as new evidence arrives.
- Replace political prejudices and fixed assumptions with evidence-based estimates.
- Distinguish consistently better forecasters from average experts by measuring performance.
- Hold forecasts loosely so they can be revised rather than defended.
- Ask “what would make me change my mind?” before committing to a prediction.
- Learn from forecasting failures by diagnosing where the reasoning went wrong.

## Failure modes named
- Assuming credentials or expert status guarantee accurate predictions.
- Treating a complex political or economic question as a single undifferentiated guess.
- Letting prior political prejudices override new evidence.
- Failing to update beliefs after disconfirming events.
- Overconfidence after a few lucky predictions.
