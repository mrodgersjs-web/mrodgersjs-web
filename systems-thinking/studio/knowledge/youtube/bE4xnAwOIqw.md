# Synthetic Truths: HBR KPMG and AI

A critical teardown of an HBR/KPMG article showing how consulting-market content uses authority signals, scale metrics, and managed anxiety to manufacture demand for proprietary AI “sophistication” frameworks.

## Procedure / models distilled
- **Academic halo deployment**: bolt a university partnership onto a consulting narrative so it reads as research rather than sales material.
- **Scale-as-truth aesthetic**: use large numbers (1.4 million prompts, 2,500 employees, eight months) as intimidating substitutes for validity, causal inference, or bias checks.
- **Black-box mystification**: invoke “thinking models,” 50 variables, and heavy compute to create knowledge asymmetry and make readers feel unable to judge the claim.
- **Category wipeout**: first frame the buyer’s current KPIs as naive, misleading, and obsolete so existing evaluation systems lose legitimacy.
- **Invisible problem construction**: introduce an abstract, unmeasurable problem (“quality of AI collaboration”) that can only be handled by expert intermediaries.
- **Calibrated performance panic**: cite uneven gains and a tiny elite of “sophisticated users” to make leadership feel they are underperforming without knowing where.
- **Proprietary lens insertion**: present the author’s framework as the only valid interpretive layer for the newly constructed problem.
- **Soft-sell dependency**: mask the real product—implementation, system integration, and advisory services—behind “low-cost,” easy-to-use indicators.
- **Audience ego validation**: reassure managers and executives that seniority, intuition, and control remain central, neutralizing displacement fears.
- **Competitive repositioning**: shift identity from auditor/compliance provider to architect of cognitive performance, expanding scope and billing authority.
- **Shadow boxing strategy**: position against Big Tech by claiming ownership of the “correct usage layer” rather than the tools themselves.
- **Market-making document model**: use a publication not to report findings but to invent a new performance category, install the firm as its translator, and broaden the mandate from finance to organizational cognition design.
- **Paywall/AI-summary trap**: note that paywalled corporate thought leadership is increasingly consumed through AI summaries, which can themselves amplify framing without original-source scrutiny.

## Failure modes named
- Confusing university co-branding with methodological rigor.
- Treating big-data scale as evidence of truth or causal validity.
- Accepting black-box complexity as a reason to defer judgment.
- Falling for problem-solution funnels that first invalidate your metrics, then sell the fix.
- Mistaking flattery of seniority for empirical findings.
- Outsourcing the definition of “good AI use” to a vendor’s proprietary framework.
- Reading consulting/HBR market-making pieces as independent research.
- Letting AI summaries of paywalled articles stand in for direct source verification.
