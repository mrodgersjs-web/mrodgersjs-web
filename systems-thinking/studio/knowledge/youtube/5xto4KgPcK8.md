# CCSS Societal Discussion #12: Foundations of Complexity Economics

Brian Arthur argues that complexity economics is not a niche heterodoxy but a more general, realistic way of doing economics that replaces equilibrium assumptions with recursive agent interactions, process, and computational experimentation.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Model the economy as a recursive feedback loop where agents react to aggregate patterns their own actions create, similar to cars forming and reacting to traffic.
- Shift the analytical frame from equilibrium, stasis, and optimality toward process, evolution, structural change, and open-endedness.
- Treat agent-based and computational models as a "computational laboratory" for running controlled experiments and variations on stripped-down problems.
- Use computational experiments to test how changes in assumptions, parameters, or variables alter emergent outcomes rather than relying solely on deductive mathematics.
- Build interactive visualizations or "flight simulators for the mind" so policymakers and stakeholders can develop intuition by manipulating scenarios.
- Include history, path dependence, small contingent events, and innovation as drivers of economic outcomes rather than treating them as noise around equilibrium.
- Replace perfectly rational, identical agents with bounded rationality, heterogeneous agents, and realistic behavioral rules.
- Stress-test systems for robustness and resilience under extreme or "perfect storm" conditions instead of optimizing for a single equilibrium.
- Bring policymakers into the modeling process so models are co-constructed and explored rather than handed over as black-box outputs.
- Recognize economies as organic, evolving arrangements rather than machine-like structures humming at optimal equilibria.
- Combine insights from mathematics, computer science, history, geography, sociology, and political economy rather than siloing economics.
- Use visualization and computation to make dynamic phenomena such as exponential growth intuitively graspable for decision-makers.
- Define rigor as respecting reality and deducing what follows, rather than as mathematical convenience or analytical tractability.

## Failure modes named
- The equilibrium assumption acts as a perceptual filter that hides structural adjustment, innovation, transitory phenomena, and the role of history.
- Assuming perfectly rational, identical agents with common knowledge produces elegant but "dead" models that lack authenticity and aliveness.
- Hyper-formalization and mathematization can become ends in themselves, leading theorists to prefer convenient assumptions over realistic ones.
- Building sophisticated technical or theoretical models without attending to the surrounding social and policy process prevents insight from translating into action.
- Treating economic systems as optimal machines rather than robust or resilient systems blinds analysis to adaptation under disruption.
- Confusing mathematical sophistication with scientific superiority, regardless of whether the model engages actual economic reality.
- Policy failures can arise when leaders lack intuitive understanding of dynamic processes such as exponential growth because they rely on static mental models.
