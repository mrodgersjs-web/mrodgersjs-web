# Your Brain: Perception Deception | Full Documentary | NOVA | PBS

The human brain does not passively receive reality; it constructs a functional, edited simulation of the world from sparse sensory samples, then mistakes that construction for objective truth.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Perception as active construction: the brain transforms incoming sensory signals rather than transparently transmitting external reality.
- Neural competition: sensory neurons fire in proportion to signal strength, but weaker signals are suppressed as they travel toward the brain, sharpening diffuse input into usable percepts.
- Predictive filling-in: the brain uses assumptions, memory, and expectations to fill gaps where sensory information is missing or impoverished.
- Central high-acuity bottleneck: only about 1% of the visual field is processed with 20/20 detail at any moment; the rest is inferred.
- Saccadic sampling: the eyes move roughly three times per second to place the high-resolution fovea on behaviorally relevant targets, making vision an action-dependent process.
- Illusion as diagnostic: discrepancies between objective reality and subjective perception expose the brain's normally invisible interpretive shortcuts.
- Light-source normalization: the brain subtracts inferred illumination from a scene to stabilize object color, which produces color-constancy illusions like the dress phenomenon.
- Memory reconsolidation: retrieving a memory returns it to an unstable state; it must be rebuilt for long-term storage, so each recollection can introduce change and noise.
- Global ignition model of consciousness: conscious perception is associated with a sudden surge and broadcast of activity across distributed regions including parietal, anterior cingulate, and prefrontal cortices.
- TMS-EEG complexity marker: transcranial magnetic stimulation produces an electrical echo whose spread and complexity indicate whether an apparently unresponsive brain is conscious.
- Developmental pruning trade-off: early brains are densely connected and exploratory; later brains prune unused connections to become efficient but less flexible.
- Narrative self-construction: the sense of self is a continuous, dynamic story woven from perception and memory to serve present functioning rather than to preserve objective truth.

## Failure modes named
- Naive realism: assuming that what we see, hear, and feel is the external world as it actually is.
- Memory fidelity illusion: believing memories are stable recordings rather than reconstructed narratives that degrade with each recall.
- Self-essence error: treating the self as a fixed, accurate core rather than a constructed, updated story.
- Rich-field illusion: assuming we see the entire visual world in high detail when only a tiny fraction is ever in focus.
- Consciousness-by-behavior assumption: concluding that an unresponsive patient is unconscious based only on bedside behavioral exams, missing hidden awareness.
- Interpretive rigidity: becoming trapped in one perceptual interpretation of an ambiguous stimulus and treating disagreement as moral failure.
