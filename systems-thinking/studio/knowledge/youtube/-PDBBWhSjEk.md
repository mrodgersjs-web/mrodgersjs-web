# What is Asymmetrical warfare???

Asymmetrical warfare is the weaker side's strategic choice to avoid direct, force-on-force confrontation and instead use unconventional, adaptive tactics to exploit a stronger enemy's weaknesses until it is disrupted, destabilized, and demoralized.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define conventional warfare as a symmetric contest between comparable forces using similar weapons, tactics, and strategies, where victory depends primarily on manpower, technology, and resources.
- Identify asymmetrical warfare as the default strategic option for a smaller or weaker force confronting a larger, more powerful foe.
- Refuse to meet superior force with equal force; instead exploit the enemy's weaknesses while amplifying your own strengths.
- Select unconventional actors and methods—guerrillas, insurgents, cyber operators—rather than uniformed armies in open battle.
- Shift success metrics from territory gained or enemies slain to disruption, destabilization, and demoralization of the opponent.
- Use unpredictability and adaptability to force the larger, conventional force onto unfamiliar terrain, both literal and figurative.
- Operate as a nimble boxer against a heavyweight: jab, weave, and stay one step ahead rather than trading blows.
- Invest heavily in understanding the enemy, accept elevated risk, and commit to the long game rather than seeking a quick victory.
- Recognize conventional warfare as a "bigger stick" head-on clash and asymmetrical warfare as a chess game in which the underdog checkmates the king through indirect moves.
- Remember that both forms share the same ultimate objective—achieving strategic objectives and securing victory—even if their paths differ.

## Failure modes named
- Attempting symmetric, head-on confrontation from a position of weakness.
- Treating asymmetrical warfare as a quick fix or shortcut to victory.
- Underestimating the deep enemy knowledge, risk tolerance, and patience required.
- Letting the stronger force fight on its familiar terrain and terms.
- Assuming the path to victory is a straight line.
