# The Art of Negotiation (Tim Castle, audiobook summary)

Negotiation as prepared value exchange: the 90% rule checklist, the irresistible ask, objection handling, anchoring/framing, and walk-away discipline.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Mindset base: you are always negotiating; not negotiating compounds silent losses (first salary offers accepted, unwanted projects accepted) (1:01–2:11).
- Inner game: self-belief stacked from small kept commitments; confidence = clarity grounded in quantified evidence of value; "no" is the start of the real conversation, not rejection (2:42–4:45).
- **The 90% rule — 7-step preparation checklist**: (1) goal/anchor + reservation point (the line where you walk), (2) strategic empathy — their motives, deadlines, internal politics, fears, and what "success" means to them, (3) build leverage inventory (alternatives, expertise, time pressure on their side), (4) script the opening + the justification for your high anchor, (5) pre-script responses to every objection, (6) define your BATNA explicitly, (7) rehearse mentally and control logistics (5:18–8:29).
- The ask: specific unambiguous numbers, not "hoping for better"; ownership framing ("based on the value I bring…"); anchor high then justify immediately with facts; then **the silence rule** — stop talking after the ask (8:34–10:31).
- Frame every ask as mutual value ("a higher rate ensures 100% focus on scaling these results"); rehearse aloud until natural — "the first time you say your ask should never be in the room" (10:31–11:21).
- Listener's advantage: listen to connect, not to reply; strategic pause invites them to fill the void (often with concessions); open-ended questions move parties from positions to interests; read tone/hesitation as signal (11:24–13:31).
- Objection protocol: welcome → acknowledge ("fair question") → clarify, don't defend (the stated objection usually masks the real one) → feel-felt-found → **isolate** ("is this the only thing stopping us?") to prevent objection creep → bring external benchmarks to depersonalize (14:04–16:19).
- Anchoring: set the first number (primacy bias pulls the whole range); if their anchor is too low, counter-anchor immediately (16:28–17:21). Frames: loss aversion, fairness/legitimacy via standards and precedent, opportunity cost of delay (17:21–18:23).
- **Walk-away strategy**: BATNA strength is real leverage; be mentally ready to end the conversation at the reservation point; exit gracefully with the door open — detachment often improves the offer (18:25–20:14).
- Relational vs transactional deals: relational negotiations maximize total negotiating value (TNV) over the partnership's life; concessions are earned and always traded ("if you… then we…"), never given free; expand the pie rather than divide it (20:18–22:14).
- Training loop: small daily low-stakes asks + honest post-negotiation debrief (what worked, where did I settle too fast, what did they value) (22:19–23:44).

## Failure modes named
- Improvising into the room unprepared — "you've already compromised your position" (5:31–5:36).
- Soft differentiating language ("would it be okay if…") that signals insecurity (9:12–9:31).
- Talking after the ask — conceding against yourself to relieve silence (10:06–10:31).
- Arguing against objections instead of clarifying to root need (14:43–15:03).
- Settling below the reservation point out of fear of walking (19:13–19:19).
- Free concessions that teach the other side to expect more for nothing (21:39–21:49).
