# Building Your Lattice Work of Mental Models

Complex decisions are better understood when you weave together mental models from multiple disciplines into an interconnected lattice rather than relying on any single field.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Build a lattice of mental models by collecting key ideas from diverse disciplines instead of hoarding isolated facts.
- Treat each discipline as a distinct lens: psychology for human behavior and decision-making, economics for incentives and market dynamics, biology for evolution and instincts, physics for system principles.
- When facing a complex decision, deliberately draw on a range of models rather than defaulting to one favorite discipline.
- Map the intersections between models so that each new lens reduces blind spots and sharpens judgment.
- Apply the lattice to real situations by asking what each relevant discipline reveals about the problem.
- In business or systemic decisions, combine financial viability with human reactions, organizational adaptability, and systemic risk.
- Remember Munger’s rule: knowing isolated facts is not enough; understanding comes from how facts connect within a lattice.
- Develop multidisciplinary fluency without trying to become a deep expert in every field.

## Failure modes named
- Relying on a single model (e.g., economics alone) to explain complex events or make decisions.
- Memorizing isolated facts and trying to “bang them back” without connecting them to a broader framework.
- Ignoring psychological and biological drivers like fear, greed, and survival instincts when analyzing systemic behavior.
- Evaluating a business expansion only on financial viability while neglecting employee reactions, organizational adaptability, and systemic risks.
- Believing you must master every discipline before you can use a multidisciplinary approach.
