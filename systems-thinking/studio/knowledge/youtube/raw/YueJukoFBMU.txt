let's assume Sarah promises to give Matt
$100 if he runs a mile in less than 7
Minutes in this case everything depends
on Matt he can train until he
consistently succeeds and when he's
ready he can show Sarah and get his $100
Game Theory doesn't study situations
like this one instead it studies
situations involving more than one
participant and in which not everything
depends on one person for example in the
game of chess the other player's moves
continuously influence your strategy
that would be one example of a situation
studied by Game Theory but don't make
the mistake of assuming only actual
games are studied game theory is also
used to for example analyze situations
in which multiple companies are
competing against one another just like
in a game of chess the actions of one
company are influenced by the actions of
the other participants for example if a
competitor lowers the price the other
companies might be forced to do the same
as you can see game theory basically
analyzes the behavior of two or more or
participants in situations involving
Rewards or punishments the rules of the
so-called game are known by everyone and
it is assumed that all players are
rational now of course Game Theory will
never be able to predict human behavior
with 100% accuracy in one situation or
another you might very well do the exact
opposite of what game theory suggests
but still it offers some truly
fascinating human behavior insight
