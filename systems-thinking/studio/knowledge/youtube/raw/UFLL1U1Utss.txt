Redundancy &amp; Fault Tolerance
One of the strengths of the internet’s&nbsp;
design is the inherent redundancy within a&nbsp;
complex network.
Redundancy is the inclusion of extra&nbsp;
components that can be used to mitigate the&nbsp;
failure of a system if other components fail.
A path between two computing devices&nbsp;
on a computer network (a sender and a&nbsp;
receiver) is a sequence of&nbsp;
directly connected devices that&nbsp;&nbsp;
begins at the sender and
ends at the receiver.
Routing is the process of finding&nbsp;
a path from sender to receiver.&nbsp;
Routing on the internet is usually&nbsp;
dynamic; it is not specified in advance.&nbsp;
In most cases, there is more than one&nbsp;
pathway through which a transmission can be&nbsp;
sent in order to reach its destination.
This allows the network to not only be fast&nbsp;&nbsp;
and efficient by finding the most optimal route
but also robust enough to continue functioning&nbsp;&nbsp;
even if part of the network fails and a
pathway is cut off.&nbsp;
Redundancy within a system often requires&nbsp;
additional resources but can provide the&nbsp;
benefit of fault tolerance.
A fault-tolerant system is one that can support&nbsp;
failures and still continue to function.
This is important because elements of complex&nbsp;
systems fail at unexpected times, often&nbsp;
in groups, and fault tolerance allows&nbsp;
users to continue to use the network.&nbsp;
The redundancy of routing options between&nbsp;
two points increases the reliability of the&nbsp;
internet and helps it scale to&nbsp;
more devices and more people.
Just as scalability is an important feature&nbsp;
of algorithms, it was also one of the core&nbsp;
design features of the internet.&nbsp;
The designers of the internet built the system&nbsp;
to be scalable, meaning the ability to add&nbsp;
more devices easily without any negative&nbsp;
consequence to time performance.
To recap redundancy and fault tolerance:
• Redundancy is the inclusion of extra&nbsp;&nbsp;
components that can be used to mitigate
the failure of a system if other components fail.&nbsp;
• Fault-tolerant is when a system can support&nbsp;
failures and still continue to function.&nbsp;
• The designers of the internet built&nbsp;
the system to be scalable so more devices&nbsp;
could be added without&nbsp;
sacrificing time performance.
