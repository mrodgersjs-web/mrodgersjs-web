Okay, let's do this. Today, I'll be
teaching you about the ant colony
optimization algorithm. In an
internet-based society, online trade is
consistently becoming more important.
However, an increasing number of parcels
for delivery also means an increase in
logistical complexity. Let's assume a
German parcel delivery service has to
deliver 40 parcels to different
addresses all over Germany. To save
time, the fastest route between the
cities has to be found. If a computer
were to calculate each individual route
from Frankfurt to Dusseldorf to Berlin
as well as from Berlin to Frankfurt to
Dusseldorf and so on, we would end up
with 815 quad dicilian possibilities.
That's an 8 with 47 zeros. And should
even one of the cities change the next
day, the roots have to be computed a new
all over again. Even modern
supercomputers cannot calculate such
amounts. The ant colony optimization
algorithm helps to find a solution to
this with a simple mathematical
procedure. It simulates the roots in a
way that is also used by ant colonies to
find the best route while foraging for
food. An ant that has discovered a short
and efficient route will walk to and
from the food source more frequently
than another that walks a longer route.
The colony notices this by the
pherommones emitted by the faster ant
and so can identify the quickest route
without having to try each one. With the
ant colony optimization algorithm, the
computer learns how to think like an ant
colony and can calculate the fastest
route much quicker. This helps the
parcel delivery service to save on both
time and fuel.
