This particular fact sheet does not contain the word China.
However, analysts believe it is imbued with messages countering China.
This report is from Kang Young-gu in Washington.
When President Lee Jae-myung made his announcement, the White House simultaneously released a joint fact sheet.
On the surface, it does not explicitly mention China. However, on closer inspection, the Trump administration's strategy to counter China becomes evident.
The most striking phrase states that the U.S. will strengthen its deterrence posture not only against North Korea but also against regional forces pressuring its allies — effectively targeting China.
Another key point is the strategic flexibility of U.S. forces in Korea.
In 2006, the joint U.S.–Korea statement noted that U.S. forces would remain in Korea and could also respond to security situations in Northeast Asia beyond the peninsula if needed.
We also reaffirmed the U.S.’s firm commitment to the continued stationing of U.S. forces in Korea and extended deterrence.
Furthermore, the fact sheet emphasizes peace and stability in the Taiwan Strait and expresses clear opposition to any unilateral changes to the status quo, signaling opposition to China's pressure on Taiwan.
China reacted sensitively.
The Chinese Ambassador to South Korea, Dai Bing, expressed dissatisfaction over the Taiwan issue, warning against playing with fire, and stated he hopes plans to introduce nuclear-powered submarines in South Korea will be handled cautiously, considering all parties’ concerns.
Despite China's opposition, analysts predict the U.S.-South Korea alliance will grow even stronger.
The U.S.-South Korea collaboration is bearing fruit step by step. Expectations are growing that the alliance will enter a new phase in the Trump era.
Kang Young-gu, MBN News, Washington.
