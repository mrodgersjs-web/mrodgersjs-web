fault tree analysis is a top-down
deductive approach to understanding how
a series of lower level events can
combine to result in an undesired event
such as an accident or equipment failure
by creating a diagram using a set of
symbols quantifying risk and following
formal logic a fault tree analysis helps
us to understand how specific systems
can fail identify the best ways to
reduce risk and assign a risk rate or
probability to a failure the fault tree
is a visual model of the logical
relationship of the unwanted event to
the more basic events that contribute to
it
we use the fall tree analysis to
methodically investigate what happens to
a system when there is a change to the
status of one or more elements of that
system
we want to know how likely it is for
specific combinations of these changes
to result in an accident defect or
breakdown
full trees are structured with symbols
for events
gates and transfers
the unwanted event is located at the top
of the fault tree now
examples of unwanted events include
accidents defects in product or process
an equipment failure or it may be a risk
or potential hazard
events in the middle of the tree are
intermediate events these are caused by
other events
at the bottom of the tree are the basic
events the most elemental factors
causing the fault
the logical relationship between events
is shown by symbols for various types of
gates
transfer symbols may be used to connect
the inputs and outputs of related fault
trees such as when a system has several
subsystems
whenever possible we assign values for a
probability of occurrence to the lowest
level events in the tree
the quantitative data may be for
reliability safety or quality
this data is used to evaluate the
probability of the top event occurring
as we can see the fall tree analysis is
a top-down deductive approach that
begins with a general conclusion then
works out its specific causes by
constructing a logic diagram
the most common purpose of a fault tree
analysis is to identify potential causes
of failures before they occur
the fall tree analysis is used widely in
the fields of aerospace safety
engineering and reliability engineering
additionally they're used to evaluate
the statistical probability that the top
level event will occur
and provide more in-depth analysis to
other tools and problem prevention
practices
the added degree of detail that fault
tree analysis provides allows us to
focus efforts on improving elements
critical to reliability or safety for
example
during an fmea or failure modes and
effects analysis we take a bottom-up
approach to examine potential failure
component by component rather than at a
system level
this may identify certain failure modes
as being high severity frequent or hard
to detect
in such situations the fault tree
analysis is useful to evaluate these
failures in more depth and with specific
metrics
fault tree analysis is used widely
during tpm efforts it serves as a
foundational tool used extensively
during focused improvement activities
understanding the causes and risks of
various types of equipment failures is
also a key step in establishing a
planned maintenance system
in the mature stage of autonomous

improvement and skill development

other pillars of tpm such as early

a completed fault tree analysis helps us

quantify the factors that lead to
