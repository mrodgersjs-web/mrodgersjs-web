The loss of biodiversity
and climate change
are not separate problems.
They are connected to each other through
drivers and solutions.
One of the biggest drivers of
biodiversity loss
is agriculture.
Especially livestock farming.
Livestock causes an enormous
loss of biodiversity,
both through
changes in land use,
i.e., deforestation to
gain pastureland,
and also because of the
density of livestock,
which damages the soil and the invertebrates living in the soil
and
displaces biodiversity from these areas.
Agriculture is also a
huge polluter, both in terms of
greenhouse gases and
water pollution.
Climate change and the
loss of biodiversity
often share the same causes,
and the solutions are similar as well.
Nature offers many
solutions to combat climate change.
It regulates our climate
naturally, and
people who
are more familiar with this area
often think of the Amazon rainforest,
which is absolutely true.
Large rainforests, large
forest areas like these,
play
an important role in regulating the climate.
But even on a much smaller scale,
nature is truly very important.
When you think of earthworms,
some of them
really burrow deep into the ground.  In this way,
they support water drainage
and protect us from flooding.
If you live in a city,
you've probably noticed
that the more trees there are,
the more protected you are from
the heat.
As climate change
becomes an increasingly serious problem,
nature can not only be a solution,
it can also help us to survive
under the changing
conditions.
For example, when we
think about food systems,
a huge driver of
biodiversity loss is
climate change, but also
inequality in the world.
Correcting food systems
will have very important effects.
But as a
biodiversity scientist,
I cannot do this alone
, and a climate scientist
cannot do this alone,
because the food system needs to consider
diet and
health, as well as
social sciences,
jobs and the economy.
Simply put, so
many people are needed to participate
in the search for
solutions
that have a holistic impact on the Earth
.
It can't just be about
biodiversity or climate.  The goal must be to create
a
healthy and happy Earth
for everyone and everything that
lives here.
So we need to work together
to find the solutions.
Then we also need to
think about collaborating with people
who
can impart this knowledge.
As a scientist,
collaborating with artists was
essential to put data
and evidence into the hands
of people
who have the power
to make decisions.
This can range from an individual to a
political
decision-maker.
