The goals of this course are--
we want you to learn about legal
theories and leading thoughts
about legal negotiation.
We'd like you to acquire
practical negotiation skills.
And in this sense,
this negotiation course
is a little different from,
maybe, from other courses
you've taken.
There is more live content,
and there's much more doing,
and I suspect less
lecturing because we
think that's the best
way for you to learn
how to be a good negotiator.
You learn how to
negotiate in teams
with difficult adversaries
and how to counsel clients
about settlement.
You'll explore issues that arise
in cross-cultural negotiation,
multi-issue negotiation, and
when there are more than two
parties--
as many as six or more.
2,500 years ago, the philosopher
Confucius said some words
that we take really to heart.
He said, "I hear, and I forget.
I see, and I remember.
I do, and I understand."
And we're going to try to apply
that philosophy to this course.
To deepen your understanding,
to develop your skills,
we want you to do--
to negotiate before,
during class,
to see and analyze the video
of experienced bargainers,
and to discuss what's happened.
You'll do readings.
You'll listen to
lectures, fairly short
lectures to learn about
the theories, techniques,
challenges of the process.
But we want you to take
the role of a lawyer
in role-played negotiations to
build your practical skills.
As you'll hear me
say other times,
if you want to learn
your favorite sport--
if it's tennis, basketball,
or something else--
you can read about it, you can
watch people play the sport ,
but until you get out
on the court yourself,

learn how to do it.
And so we want you
to get on the court,
practice, talk about
what's happened,
watch people, and become
better and more comfortable
in the role.
We'd like you to
analyze videos, and we'd
like you to participate in class
discussions-- all of which,
we think will make you
a better negotiator.
Your progress and
learning in the course
is going to be assessed
as we go along--
first, in contributions you
make to class discussions,
the documents you create

and perhaps papers in which you

observed and learned,
and finally, a
take-home final exam,

of a surprise at that point.
So that's the plan.
We look forward to
working with you
to make you a better and
more effective negotiator.
