What are evolutionarily stable strategies?
In many cases, it is quite
clear what is most beneficial for the individual.
The most advantageous option to
ensure survival and reproduction.
However, there are cases where
the most advantageous option depends on
how the rest of the population behaves, or
more precisely, how the
majority of individuals in the population behave.  We
can show this
with the example of fishermen and pirates.
Let's say we have a certain type of bird
that has two options for obtaining food.
It is a seabird.
He can either go fishing,
dive under the water and catch the fish,
or he can fly around
and rob the birds that caught the fish.  We'll
call them pirates.
These are two options for
getting food.
So what is better,
to be a fisherman or a pirate?
The answer depends on
how the rest of this population behaves.
If the entire population is fishing, an
individual may appear and
become a pirate due to a mutation.
Since many birds are fishing nearby, it
is easy for him to steal a fish.
So you could say
that natural selection favors pirates,
that the pirate gene
will spread in the population.
A few generations later,
everyone would be pirates,
and there would be no one to
fish.
Piracy is therefore not sustainable,
it is evolutionarily unstable.
But fishing
can also be unstable.
When the entire population is fishing, a
mutated pirate may appear.  A
stable strategy is one
that, when applied by the entire population,
no mutant can emerge
that would have an advantage over the others.
Therefore, neither the fishermen nor the pirates in my example
are an evolutionarily stable population.
However,
some ratio between them could be evolutionarily stable.  It
could be, for example,
80% fishermen and 20% pirates.
Equilibrium occurs when both
types of birds thrive equally.  A
well-known example
is the sex ratio in a population.
Any deviation
from the equilibrium ratio
is evolutionarily disadvantageous.
When there are more males in a population, it
is more advantageous to have daughters.
When there are more women in the population, it
is more advantageous to have sons.
Therefore, the equilibrium
ratio is 50 to 50.
More precisely, it is 50% economic
investment in daughters and 50% in sons.  An
evolutionarily stable strategy is one
that cannot be improved by anything.
No strategy is favored
if everyone adheres to this balance.
