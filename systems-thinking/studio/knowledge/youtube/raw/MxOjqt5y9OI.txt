in this scenario we have two parts to
our Network each one with its own path
to our ISP and the internet
packets originating from pc1 are
eventually forwarded to router a and on
to the ISP
while packets originating from PC2 are
eventually forwarded to router B and
then on to the same ISP
but what happens when there is a problem
with router a or with a link used along
the path to router a
packets requiring router a to forward
them onto the internet will get dropped
and can no longer reach their final
destination
a fault tolerant network is one that can
continue operations without an eruption
when one or more components of the
network fail
we have now installed additional links
in our Network giving us multiple paths
to reach our ISP and the internet
having multiple paths to a destination
is known as redundancy one of the ways
to help make the network fault tolerant
if one path fails the packets are
automatically forwarded using a
different link
the more redundancy in a network the
more fault tolerant the network becomes
