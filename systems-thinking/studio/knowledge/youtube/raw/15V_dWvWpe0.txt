at safety meetings do you?
Then there are four things you should be talking
about at your meetings.

Safety meetings are a legal requirement.
You have to have them.
But nowhere does it state that you can’t
use those meetings to talk about things that
make your workplace better.
When you focus on only rules, procedures, and inspections, you lose the engagement of your people.
Coaches use their pre-game meetings to get buy-in to the game plan.
Not to cover rules and procedures.
Your meetings create the team's mindset so meetings are very important.
So instead of rules, there are four things you should be talking about in safety meetings.
First, you need to make sure in every meeting
you talk about accountability.
Every member of your team has an obligation
to be responsible for his/her actions.
Getting your people to move from just following
rules to an “I got this” ownership mindset
doesn’t happen without accountability.
Second, teamwork.
You’ve got to talk about how to work together better as a team.
If you’re not talking about caring for the
people you work with, and standing up for
and protecting them, then you’re not actually
talking about teamwork.
Would your people openly speak-up if one of
their fellow team members didn’t have their
head in the game?
They would if they were a tight team that
cared about each other.
Third, you need to be talking about respect.
Not just expecting it but openly discussing
it.
Respect for your teammates is as important
as respect for yourself.
You can’t give that which you don’t own.
It is so much easier to demonstrate respect
for people you care about than people you
don’t.
That’s why solid-performing teams have a
healthy respect for each other.
And lastly, you’ve got to talk about courtesy.
Yes, in safety meetings.
Courtesy is a major contributor to any good
safety program.
Courtesy is the preventative measure - the
thing you do without being told.
Seriously, think about this.
When has courtesy ever been the reason that
someone got hurt?
You can’t build a solid workplace culture
without accountability, teamwork, respect
and courtesy.
They don’t just magically happen.
You have to talk about them.
No better time than when you’ve got the
whole team assembled together.
So, get busy on creating your discussion points
on these four subjects for your next safety
meeting.
