Your app runs on GPT-4.0.
One provider, one model, one point of
failure. OpenAI goes down and your
entire application dies, or you hit your
rate limit and 40% of your requests just
vanish. This is the same reason you
don't serve your website from one
machine. You put multiple servers behind
Nginx. So, why are you serving AI from a
single provider? Today, I'll show you
five load balancing strategies that keep
your AI alive. Strategy one, round
robin.
The simplest approach, you cycle through
providers evenly, OpenAI Anthropic,
repeat. It's dead simple and gives you
even distribution across all your
providers. But, here's the catch, round
robin treats all providers equally when
they aren't. It ignores cost, latency,
and capability differences. That's where
cost-based routing comes in.
Strategy two, cost-based routing. Route
to the cheapest provider that can handle
the request.
Look at this, GPT-4.0 mini costs 15
cents per million input tokens. Gemini
Flash costs 7 cents. Compare that to
GPT-4.0 at 2.50. Most requests don't
need the expensive model. This alone can
save you 50 to 80% on your API bill.
Simple requests go to cheap models. Only
complex tasks hit the expensive ones.
Strategy three, latency-based routing.
Track the rolling average response time
for each provider and route to the
fastest one available.
You keep the last 100 measurements and
pick the provider with the lowest
average latency.
Strategy four, capability-based routing.
Different models are better at different
things. Excels at code generation.
GPT-4.0 mini is plenty for data
extraction.
Why pay premium prices for tasks a
cheaper model handles just as well?
Strategy five, weighted distribution.
Give more traffic to your preferred
providers. Maybe OpenAI gets 50% and
Anthropic gets 30 and gets 20. You
control exactly how traffic flows. Now,
the most important pattern of all,
fallback chains. When your primary
provider fails, you automatically fall
back to the next one in line. Primary,
secondary, tertiary. This is
non-negotiable for production, but
fallbacks alone aren't enough. You need
health checking with circuit breakers.
Track error counts for each provider.
After three consecutive failures, trip
the circuit breaker and stop sending
traffic there.
Check again after a period. Now, let's
put it all together into a complete load
balancer.
The balancer class combines health
tracking, latency monitoring, and budget
management. It picks a routing strategy,
balanced, cheapest, or fastest, and
filters to only healthy providers. And
you don't have to build all of this from
scratch.
Tools like LiteLLM and OpenRouter handle
multi-provider routing out of the box.
But understanding these patterns is
crucial so you know what's happening
under the hood. Let's recap. Never rely
on a single provider. Use cost-based
routing to save 50 to 80%. Fallback
chains are non-negotiable.
And health checks with circuit breakers
prevent cascading failures. Implement
even one of these strategies today and
your production AI will thank you.
