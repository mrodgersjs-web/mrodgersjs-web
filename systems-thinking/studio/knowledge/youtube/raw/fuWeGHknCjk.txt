The big question about the Industrial Revolution&nbsp;
is: Why did it happen in England? Why did it&nbsp;&nbsp;
happen in the 18th century? The Industrial&nbsp;
Revolution meant the replacement of the&nbsp;&nbsp;
cottage mode of production with a factory mode&nbsp;
of production. By the end of the 18th century&nbsp;&nbsp;
there was a big growth in cotton production and&nbsp;
initially by women spinning in their cottages.&nbsp;&nbsp;
So, in the cottage with the spinning wheel,&nbsp;
there was a woman who was the spinner, there was&nbsp;&nbsp;
the apparatus – the spinning wheel. On the right&nbsp;
side of the spinning wheel there was a big wheel,&nbsp;&nbsp;
that she spun with her right hand. If she did&nbsp;
this for 12 hours a day, she could produce one&nbsp;&nbsp;
pound or half a kilo of cotton yarn. In the middle&nbsp;
of the 18th century there were quite a few people&nbsp;&nbsp;
who were trying to invent machines to mechanise&nbsp;
that production process. James Hargreaves was the&nbsp;&nbsp;
first to succeed. His invention was the spinning&nbsp;
jenny. It was a kind of scaled up version of the&nbsp;&nbsp;
spinning wheel that spinners had used previously&nbsp;
in their cottages. With the spinning jenny, the&nbsp;&nbsp;
woman turned the big wheel, but it was connected&nbsp;
with strings to ten, twenty, thirty spindles, so&nbsp;&nbsp;
she could do much more work. So, the question is:&nbsp;
why did Hargreave go through five years of effort&nbsp;&nbsp;
and hundreds of pounds of expenses from the 1760s&nbsp;
to make this machine work? The situation in the&nbsp;&nbsp;
18th century was new. If we compared the earnings&nbsp;
of women in spinning back to the end of the 16th&nbsp;&nbsp;
century with those of men doing building work,&nbsp;
what we'd find is that in the late 16th to early&nbsp;&nbsp;
17th century a woman earned about a third of what&nbsp;
a man earned. By the time you get to the middle of&nbsp;&nbsp;
the 18th century, the woman is earning two thirds&nbsp;
of what the man is earning, so there has been&nbsp;&nbsp;
a big rise in her earnings, and that means that&nbsp;
labour that is saved by the spinning jenny is more&nbsp;&nbsp;
valuable and it can offset the extra cost of the&nbsp;
machinery. So the rise in women's wages had a big&nbsp;&nbsp;
impact on the rate of return to using equipment.&nbsp;
I tried to estimate what the rate of return would&nbsp;&nbsp;
have been throughout the period from the end&nbsp;
of the 16th century to the middle of the 18th&nbsp;&nbsp;
century. It goes from something like 2/3% per year&nbsp;
in the early 17th century – which wasn't enough to&nbsp;&nbsp;
attract anybody to invest in something like that&nbsp;
– to 30/40% in the middle of the 18th century,&nbsp;&nbsp;
which was a very profitable investment&nbsp;
project. The reason for that was because&nbsp;&nbsp;
women's wages, which monetised the gains from&nbsp;
mechanisation, kept rising and rising. The high&nbsp;&nbsp;
wages were the result of Britain's success in&nbsp;
international trade. That was rooted in the&nbsp;&nbsp;
Empire. The Empire had expanded a lot in the 17th&nbsp;
and 18th century. There were very valuable sugar&nbsp;&nbsp;
colonies that had been started in the 1660s. You&nbsp;
had the thirteenth colonies that became the United&nbsp;&nbsp;
States on the East Coast of North America, Canada&nbsp;
was added to that in 1763, and then in 1757 the&nbsp;&nbsp;
East India Company conquered Bengal and added that&nbsp;
part of India to the Empire. So, all of these were&nbsp;&nbsp;
captive markets for Britain, and all of them were&nbsp;
very prosperous in generating high income. So,&nbsp;&nbsp;
they were big in growing markets for British&nbsp;
manufacture exports and cloth was an important&nbsp;&nbsp;
manufactured export. Production of that cloth&nbsp;
required more and more employees. Labour markets&nbsp;&nbsp;
got tighter and tighter and wages went up, and&nbsp;
that made the use of mechanised spinning equipment&nbsp;&nbsp;
profitable. This happened in Britain but it didn't&nbsp;
happen in other countries, were the rate of return&nbsp;&nbsp;
to using spinning jennies was still very low in&nbsp;
the 18th century. So, the Industrial Revolution&nbsp;&nbsp;
was the knock-on effect of British imperialism.&nbsp;
The spinning jenny was invented because it&nbsp;&nbsp;
was profitable to use and that profitability&nbsp;
covered the costs of Hargreave's R&amp;D project.
