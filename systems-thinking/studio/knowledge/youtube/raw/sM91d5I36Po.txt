A 1995 study of Olympic medal winners
found that bronze medal winners seemed
on average far happier than silver medal
winners. The reason has to do with
perceived gains or losses around a
reference point. The silver medalist
focus on almost winning gold with any
other outcome considered a loss. The
bronze medalist focus downwards towards
fourth place and not winning a medal at
all. Therefore, any outcome above not
winning a medal is a gain. That's why
some silver medalists have this
irrational reaction. They should be
happy at being second best in the world
rather than being first loser. Prospect
theory explains that we process
information in an illogical way by
valuing gains and losses differently.
Expressed in financial terms, the amount
of joy gained from receiving $50 should
be equal to a situation in which you
gained $100 and then lost 50 as both
results in a net gain of $50.
But most people view the single gain
more favorably than the gain and loss
scenario because the feeling of pain due
to a loss is two to three times greater
than the joy felt from an equivalent
gain. Loss does hurt, but get some
perspective. Enjoy the wins, too.
