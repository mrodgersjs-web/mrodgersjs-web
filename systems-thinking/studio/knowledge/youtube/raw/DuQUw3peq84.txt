hire more developers. But, adding more

This week, we are discovering the method
behind Amazon's mad team strategies.
Amazon's famous two-pizza team
principle, was it really just about head
count? More people on a team means more
communication links between them, and
Amazon treated that as friction, not
culture.
So, it was really about reducing
communication. Smaller, autonomous teams
made decisions faster because they spent
less time coordinating and more time
building.
So, Amazon built two structural pieces
to make that true.
One, the API mandate in 2002.
Bezos issued a directive that every team
had to expose its data and functionality
through its service interfaces,
communicate with other teams only
through those interfaces, and use no
other form of inter-process
communication. The goal was reducing
dependency and inter-team communication
needs.
Number two, single-threaded leadership.
Paired with team size, Amazon gave each
initiative one leader with one job. No
splits across three priorities or
waiting on sign-offs from other
departments.
So, how can you flip this framework and
implement it in your own business? Here
are my three tips.
Number one, run the two-pizza test, but
audit dependencies, not just head count.
Take your last shipped project, count
how many teams or approvals you needed
before it could go out, not just how
many people were on the team. If that
number is high, even with a small team,
you don't have a two-pizza team. Replace
the approvals with documented handoffs
or interfaces, so the next team acts on
a spec instead of a meeting.
Number two, run the name one person
test. For every initiative, write down
who can say yes to a change without
checking with anyone else. If you can't
write down one name, you don't have a
single-threaded ownership.
Pick the name today. If two people
currently share the call, name one lead
and move the other to support, and do
this in writing before your next team
meeting.
Number three, sort the blocker before
you open a requisition.
Pull the last three blockers that
stalled the team. Waiting on another
team's sign-off is a dependency problem.
No clarity on who owns the call is an
ownership problem. Only if neither one
of those are true, and the work itself
was simply more than the hands
available, do you have a headcount
problem?
Fix dependency and ownership first. Only
open a requisition if the same blocker
still shows up after both of those are
cleared.
