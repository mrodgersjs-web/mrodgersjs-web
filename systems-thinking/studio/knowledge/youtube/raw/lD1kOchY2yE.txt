We're very pleased to introduce our new series of deliberate practice training books
published by the American Psychological Association.
Deliberate practice is a term coined by K. Anders Ericsson.
He's a psychologist in the science of expertise.
Deliberate practice refers to training activities professionals use across a wide range of fields to achieve expertise.
Practice is how professional athletes develop extra physical strength and endurance,
or how professional chess players develop extra mental capacity,
or how professional musicians develop their ear for music.
Traditionally, psychotherapy trainees have been expected to develop most of their therapy skills while working face-to-face with clients
However, this is not ideal!
It's kind of like asking a pilot to learn to fly when there are actual passengers in the plane.
So we've created a series of deliberate practice exercises to help expand your psychotherapy skills
before you sit face-to-face with a real client.
In my own training,
I personally noticed that while my ability to write and talk about therapy advanced very quickly,
my effectiveness at actually helping my clients progressed quite slowly.
In other words, there was a growing disconnect between my knowledge about therapy and my ability to perform it,
especially with clients I found challenging.
To help address this gap, we created a series of deliberate practice training books
tailored specifically for therapists.
Each book focuses on a major model of psychotherapy.
We asked leading researchers and practitioners a simple question:
What are the essential skills for your model of therapy that trainees have the most difficulty acquiring?
We then created deliberate practice exercises specifically for those skills,
and tested the exercises at training sites around the world.
The books provide instructions to grade the difficulty of the exercises from beginning to advanced,
so the exercises should benefit all therapists.
Thank you for your interest in our new book series.
