Franklin gave his days a similar
structure through two direct questions
asking in the morning, "What good shall
I do this day?" and in the evening,
"What good have I done today?" Those
questions placed virtue inside the
schedule, making character part of the
day's preparation and part of its final
review.
Franklin admitted that his tracking
system revealed far more faults than he
had expected, which gave the practice
its human weight and kept
self-examination grounded in reality.
