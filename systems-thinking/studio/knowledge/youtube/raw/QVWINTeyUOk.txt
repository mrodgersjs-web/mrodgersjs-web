THE DOPAMINE PROBLEM&nbsp;
Let's start with why studying feels so painful.
Every time you check Instagram, watch a YouTube&nbsp;&nbsp;
short, or play a game, your brain gets&nbsp;
a massive dopamine spike. It's instant,&nbsp;&nbsp;
it's easy, and it feels good.
Now, when you try to study, your brain&nbsp;&nbsp;
compares it to these activities. And studying? It&nbsp;

"This is boring! Go back to the fun stuff!"
You're not lazy. You're not broken. Your brain&nbsp;&nbsp;
is just doing what it's designed to do, seek the&nbsp;

THE DOPAMINE LOADING METHOD
The dopamine loading method works in three phases:&nbsp;&nbsp;
deprivation, priming, and amplification.
Phase one: Deprivation.&nbsp;
Before you study, you need to lower your&nbsp;
baseline dopamine. This means 30 minutes&nbsp;&nbsp;
of absolutely nothing stimulating. No phone, no&nbsp;
music, no talking, just silence or a simple walk.&nbsp;
Your brain will hate this. It'll&nbsp;
feel uncomfortable. But that's the&nbsp;&nbsp;
point. You're creating a dopamine deficit,&nbsp;
making your brain hungry for stimulation.&nbsp;
Phase two: Priming.
Now, before you open your books,&nbsp;&nbsp;
spend 2 minutes visualizing yourself studying&nbsp;
successfully. Picture yourself understanding&nbsp;&nbsp;
concepts, solving problems, feeling satisfied.
This creates anticipation. Your brain starts&nbsp;&nbsp;
releasing dopamine before you even begin,&nbsp;
because it predicts a reward is coming.&nbsp;
Phase three: Amplification.
As you study, you're going to manually&nbsp;&nbsp;
inject dopamine rewards. Every 25 minutes, take a&nbsp;
2-minute break and do something mildly enjoyable,&nbsp;&nbsp;
stretch, walk, drink water mindfully.
Not scrolling. Not high-dopamine&nbsp;&nbsp;
activities. Just enough to tell your brain:&nbsp;
"Hey, this studying thing comes with rewards."&nbsp;
THE DAILY DOPAMINE ROUTINE
Let me give you a practical&nbsp;&nbsp;
routine you can start today.
Morning routine.&nbsp;
Wake up and do NOT touch your phone. This is&nbsp;
critical. Instead, drink water, make your bed,&nbsp;&nbsp;

dopamine low, so studying later will feel more&nbsp;&nbsp;

Pre-study phase.&nbsp;
30 minutes of dopamine deprivation.&nbsp;
Sit in silence, go for a slow walk,&nbsp;&nbsp;
or just stare out the window. Let your&nbsp;
brain get bored. Let it crave stimulation.&nbsp;
First study block.
Before starting,&nbsp;&nbsp;
spend 2 minutes with your eyes closed. Visualize&nbsp;

Use the Pomodoro technique: 25 minutes focus,&nbsp;
5 minutes low-dopamine break. During breaks,&nbsp;&nbsp;

After every successful study block, give yourself&nbsp;
a small celebration. Literally say "yes!" or&nbsp;&nbsp;
pump your fist. It sounds silly, but it trains&nbsp;
your brain to associate studying with winning.&nbsp;
Mid-morning break.
Now you can have a moderate&nbsp;&nbsp;
reward. A good breakfast, some sunlight, maybe&nbsp;
10 minutes of light exercise. Still no phone.&nbsp;
Second study block.
Repeat the same process.&nbsp;&nbsp;
The key here is consistency. Your brain&nbsp;
is learning a pattern: low stimulation,&nbsp;&nbsp;

Lunch break.&nbsp;
Eat mindfully without screens. Notice&nbsp;
the taste, the texture. This keeps your&nbsp;&nbsp;
dopamine receptors sensitive.
Afternoon study session.&nbsp;
By now, your brain is actually starting to&nbsp;
associate studying with dopamine release.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Keep the pattern going.
Evening wind down.&nbsp;
This is when you can enjoy higher-dopamine&nbsp;
activities, but strategically. Watch something,&nbsp;&nbsp;
play a game, but set a time limit. You've&nbsp;
earned it, but you're staying in control.&nbsp;
