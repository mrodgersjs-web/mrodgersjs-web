MIT researchers have recently created
cells programmed with synthetic analog
Gene circuits that are able to respond
to a stimulus in a graded manner a
typical synthetic Gene circuit is
digital meaning that it acts like a
switch gene expression is either on or
off analog Gene circuits act more like
dimmer switches and are capable of a
broad range of
outputs say that we wanted to make a
cell that responded to the presence of a
certain small molecule a cell with a
digital circuit would turn on expression
of the output in this case a fluorescent
protein in an All or Nothing manner in
contrast a cell with an analog circuit
could respond dynamically to continuous
concentrations of the small molecule
changing its output depending on the
concentration of the small
molecule here is a simplified view of
how these circuits work in the digital
circuit gene expression is turned on by
transcription factors that bind to the
small molecule the digital circuit turns
fully on as the threshold concentration
of the small molecule in the analog
circuit additional Gene modules regulate
the availability of the transcription
Factor based on the levels of the
stimulus allowing for a graded
response analog circuits can carry out
mathematical functions to do addition
two circuits can be used Each of which
respond to a different stimulus but lead
to the production of the same output
molecule synthetic analog circuit are
uniquely suited to interface engineered
cells with a natural world where
continuous information is predominant
for this reason there are many potential
medical applications for synthetic
analog circuits for example we could
engineer probiotic bacteria in the human

Sentinels when tissues become inflamed
the probiotic Sentinels respond to

graded way and provide just the right
