What is an agent-based model?
An agent is an autonomous, individual element with properties and actions in a computer simulation.
Agent-based modeling (ABM) is based on the idea that the world can best be modeled through agents, an environment, and agent-agent or agent-environment interactions.
An agent-based model in our context is a model that consists of agents, as you might have expected.
In most of our cases, we're dealing with computer simulations.
Agent-based modeling is based on the idea that the world is best represented by agents and their environment, and by the interactions between agents or between agent and environment.
And [we believe?] that this is one of the best ways to model the complex systems we know from reality.
Here are some pictures of different types of things that one might imagine an agent to have.
Most people think of agents as human individuals.  But there are other options.
For example, a firm could be an agent in a model that examines the interactions between firms.
It could also be a human being, of course.  This image is a screenshot from a model in NetLogo about the spread of HIV, where the agents actually represent people who can transmit HIV to each other.
The spread of HIV does not need to be modeled at the human level.  But this model was probably appropriate for the purpose for which it was created.
One could also create a model using viruses as agents.  However, it would be a completely different model, with a different resolution and for answering different questions.

Many agent-based models attempt to characterize general behavioral patterns.
An individual who is erratic, who behaves differently from the majority in many ways, is very difficult to understand or whose actions are difficult to predict.
Other models are better suited for this, such as ethnographic or thorough psychological studies.
I recommend that you do not use agent-based models for such questions.  On
the other hand, if you want to create a model of the general role of dictators and despots, not for a specific individual, then agent-based modeling may very well be the appropriate method.
