# Become a Master Persuader

Robert Greene argues that the key to persuasion is reversing attention away from yourself and toward the other person’s needs, knowledge level, interests, and changing problems.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Accept that you want influence and persuasion as a necessary life skill rather than denying or resenting it.
- Reverse the lens: before making any pitch, study the target—their past work, values, pressures, and current interests.
- Research the audience before crafting the message; frame the same core idea to match their mental model.
- Assume the other person does not share your knowledge or interests; translate your idea into their language.
- Think inside the reader, client, or customer by simplifying, clarifying, and structuring information for their absorption.
- Treat people’s needs as dynamic, not static; continuously track changing tastes, trends, and problems.
- Practice radical outer-orientation as a daily habit across every domain—business, writing, family, and work.
- Shift from inward complaint about not being heard to outward observation of what the other person is going through.
- Recognize that people sense self-interest instantly; genuine attention to their needs builds trust and impact.

## Failure modes named
- Approaching persuasion self-first by focusing on your brilliance, needs, and “perfect pitch.”
- Assuming the audience shares your knowledge base and interests.
- Treating customer or audience needs as fixed rather than evolving.
- Building an elaborate pitch without first listening to or researching the target.
- Turning inward to whine about not being heard instead of observing the other person.
