# Systems Thinking!

The video explains systems thinking as a way for senior managers to zoom out from isolated programs, map interacting multi-level influences, identify leverage points, and frame evaluations that improve strategic action on complex issues like obesity.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define a system as an entity made of interacting parts whose interconnections create a whole that produces some result.
- Adopt a systems perspective to understand what helps or hinders the success of health interventions.
- Map multi-level factors — physiological, psychological, social, environmental, and economic — and how they interact.
- Recognize that complex problems like obesity emerge from many interacting influences rather than a single cause.
- Practice “zooming out” to consider influences inside and outside the organization that affect a program.
- Identify leverage points — places in the system where small changes can amplify overall impact.
- Distinguish leverage points you can directly change from those beyond your control but still relevant to planning.
- Expand the boundaries of evaluation to capture diverse perspectives, values, interrelationships, and key system boundaries.
- Commission evaluations that deliver strategic direction, including program improvements, partnership opportunities, and policy-advocacy targets.
- Set realistic program expectations by understanding the external factors that shape its success.

## Failure modes named
- Treating a program as isolated from the larger system it operates in.
- Expecting simple linear cause-and-effect solutions to work in a complex system.
- Program-level staff thinking only within their program’s traditional boundaries.
- Ignoring the interactions among physiological, psychological, social, environmental, and economic factors.
- Failing to account for the perspectives and values of actors outside the program.
