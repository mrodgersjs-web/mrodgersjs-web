# What is Organizational Design? How Does It Support a Digital Strategy and Change Management Plan?

Eric Kimberling explains organizational design as a future-state discipline that translates business strategy, processes, and technology into role structures, performance measures, competencies, and a change plan that unlocks transformation value and reinforces culture.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Anchor organizational design on a clear future-state business strategy, processes, and technologies before defining roles.
- Define the future-state organizational structure as an org chart that maps roles, responsibilities, reporting relationships, span of control, and cross-functional collaboration.
- Treat the org chart as a foundational blueprint for work design rather than a cosmetic exercise.
- Establish performance metrics and success measures for each role or box in the future-state org structure.
- Tie those performance measures directly to compensation and pay levels for each role.
- Perform a current-state versus future-state competency gap analysis to identify skills that must change.
- Close competency gaps through hiring, learning and development, and targeted upskilling.
- Translate the future-state org design into concrete activities and work streams within the organizational change plan.
- Rewrite job descriptions to reflect new roles, responsibilities, and expectations.
- Deploy human capital management and HR technologies to enable and accelerate the workforce transition.
- Communicate future role clarity so employees understand how their jobs will evolve and why.
- Align the org design with the desired target culture to reinforce new behaviors and ways of working.

## Failure modes named
- Treating org design as a description of the current organization rather than a future-state blueprint.
- Failing to clearly articulate how jobs, roles, and responsibilities will change, which creates confusion and resistance.
- Leaving employees to assume the worst about their roles, such as believing their jobs are in jeopardy.
- Deploying best-in-class technology or process redesign without a clear org design strategy, leaving transformation value unrealized.
- Defining future-state roles without closing the underlying competency gap through hiring or development.
