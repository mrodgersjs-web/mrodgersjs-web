# 💡 metalearning | a framework on learning how to learn

A framework for self-directed learning that allocates ~10% of effort to clarifying why you want to learn, what to learn, and how to find and filter resources before diving in.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Reserve ~10% of total learning time for metalearning: planning the why, what, and how before consuming content.
- Clarify the "why" by distinguishing instrumental goals (practical outcomes) from intrinsic goals (curiosity, joy).
- Define the "what" by listing the concepts you must understand, facts you must memorize, and procedures you must practice.
- Build a knowledge tree: anchor new learning to first principles / trunk concepts, then hang details on branches.
- Extract transferable frameworks from past learning and apply them to structurally similar but content-different domains.
- Benchmark the path to your outcome by studying standard curricula, syllabi, popular courses, books, or expert roadmaps.
- Emphasize resources that match your outcome and learning style; actively exclude or deprioritize those that do not.
- Use mental models / frameworks as memory shortcuts rather than trying to memorize isolated facts.
- Self-direct your route: piece together your own resource sequence instead of blindly following a preset textbook order.
- Accept iterative learning as learn–unlearn–relearn; it is okay to branch out and circle back later.
- Treat learning as a treasure hunt where the path is built through exploration and experimentation.

## Failure modes named
- Following a textbook or standard curriculum without questioning efficiency or relevance, leading to demotivation.
- Creating mismatched expectations by not clarifying whether the goal is instrumental or intrinsic.
- Choosing a graded / exam-oriented curriculum when the real goal is practical skill or enjoyment.
- Memorizing surface facts instead of understanding the underlying frameworks that generate them.
- Refusing to exclude resources that are poorly matched to your outcome or learning style.
- Assuming you must learn everything now rather than branching from first principles and returning later.
