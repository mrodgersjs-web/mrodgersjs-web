# Jeff Bezos: All Successful Teams Follow The 2 Pizza Rule

Jeff Bezos explains Amazon's two-pizza team rule and its memo-based meeting culture, where teams stay small and every meeting begins with silent reading of a six-page narrative memo to ensure real engagement before discussion.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Two-pizza team rule: cap team size at the number of people who can be fed with two pizzas.
- Replace PowerPoint decks with a six-page narratively structured memo as the default meeting artifact.
- Write memos with real sentences, verbs, nouns, and topic sentences rather than bullet points.
- Use the memo to create shared context before a discussion, not as a presentation to be delivered.
- Start the meeting with silent reading of the memo, treating the room like a study hall.
- Budget roughly the first half hour of the meeting for everyone to read the memo in the room.
- Only begin discussion after the full document has actually been consumed by everyone present.
- Hold reading time synchronously in the meeting because busy people will otherwise bluff that they pre-read.
- Protect the first portion of the meeting as dedicated consumption time so participants cannot pretend to have read.

## Failure modes named
- PowerPoint presentations as a weaker medium for complex discussion than written narrative.
- Executives bluffing their way through meetings as if they read the pre-work.
- Bullet-point documents that lack sentences, verbs, and real contextual structure.
- Pretending to have read the memo instead of actually doing the work.
- Culture shock for new executives encountering Amazon's memo-and-silent-reading meeting norm.
