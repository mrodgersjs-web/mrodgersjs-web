# Game Theory Dominant Strategy Practice: Econ Concepts in 60 Seconds

Jacob Clifford walks through two payoff-matrix exercises to practice identifying whether each firm has a dominant strategy.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Use a payoff matrix where each cell lists two payoffs for two competing decision-makers.
- Read the left-hand number in each cell as the payoff for the firm on the left axis and the right-hand number as the payoff for the firm on the top axis.
- Box or highlight the payoffs of the firm you are analyzing to avoid confusing whose outcomes you are comparing.
- Test for a dominant strategy by holding the opponent's move constant and asking which of your own options gives the higher payoff.
- Repeat that comparison for every possible move the opponent could make.
- A dominant strategy exists only if the same choice is best no matter what the opponent does.
- If the best choice changes depending on the opponent's move, the firm does not have a dominant strategy.
- Run the same test symmetrically for each player in the matrix.
- In the advertising matrix, Firm A should advertise whether Firm B advertises or not, so advertising is A's dominant strategy.
- In the advertising matrix, Firm B's best move depends on Firm A's choice, so B has no dominant strategy.
- In the price matrix, Firm X's dominant strategy is to lower price.
- In the price matrix, Firm Y's dominant strategy is also to lower price.

## Failure modes named
- Confusing which payoff belongs to which firm inside a single cell.
- Treating a strategy as dominant when it is only best under one opponent move.
- Assuming that every player in a game must have a dominant strategy.
