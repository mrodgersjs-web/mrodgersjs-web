# 24 Understand risk vs  uncertainty

Distinguish quantifiable risk from genuine uncertainty, then cheaply and early convert assumptions, questions, and surprises into facts so you neither commit prematurely nor reject a viable opportunity.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Adopt a "kill the project" posture for transformational initiatives: actively seek evidence that disproves the idea rather than confirming it.
- Separate risk from uncertainty: risk applies when you know enough to assign probabilities; uncertainty applies when the situation is too unknown to probability.
- Use the chasm metaphor: crossing an unfamiliar chasm is not inherently risky—it depends on whether you find a bridge or a tightrope.
- Map every factor across the certainty range: facts (known knowns), assumptions (things you think you know), questions (known unknowns), and surprises (unknown unknowns).
- Treat the primary goal as converting assumptions, questions, and surprises into facts as cheaply and early as possible.
- Distinguish factoids from facts: reject unreliable information that has been repeated until it is accepted as truth.
- Rank risk factors by impact—low, moderate, high—and focus attention on the high-impact items to avoid paralysis by analysis.
- Recognize that familiar markets contain more facts and resemble portfolio risk models, while unfamiliar markets begin with mostly uncertainty and require a different mindset.
- Delay resource commitments until the nature of the chasm is discovered; avoid both reckless commitment and unwarranted avoidance.

## Failure modes named
- Mislabeling uncertainty as risk and acting as if probabilities exist when they do not.
- Committing to cross the chasm before discovering whether the crossing is a bridge or a tightrope.
- Rejecting a viable opportunity because it feels risky when it was actually uncertain and crossable.
- Treating factoids as validated facts.
- Paralysis by analysis from spreading attention across too many risk factors instead of prioritizing high-impact ones.
