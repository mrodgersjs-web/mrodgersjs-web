# Stop Doing Spaced Repetition  Wrong

The video argues that repetition is essential for long-term memory, but passive rote repetition is inefficient; effective review uses active recall, self-testing, and error-focused practice.

## Procedure / models distilled
- **Active recall instead of passive review**: look away from the material and recite the information from memory rather than re-reading it.
- **Audio flash cards**: record prompts with pauses, then play them back during passive time and say the answer aloud during each pause.
- **Handwritten full retrieval**: write out the entire body of material from memory to surface gaps.
- **Error-focused review**: after a full retrieval, identify every mistake and concentrate subsequent review only on the errors.
- **Iterative error reduction**: repeat error-focused reviews in cycles, driving the error rate down from large percentages toward near zero.
- **Pre-performance self-test**: before the real event, reconstruct the material from memory and then walk through the answers to find remaining mistakes.
- **Review for long-term retention**: without review, memorized information fades within about a week; spaced active review moves it into long-term memory.

## Failure modes named
- **Rote memorization trap**: trying to learn by reading, writing, or repeating the same information over and over as the sole strategy.
- **Passive review illusion**: looking at the material again tricks the brain into thinking it is known.
- **Reviewing everything equally**: spending time on already-known items instead of focusing on errors.
- **Skipping review**: expecting long-term retention without any spaced review.
- **No pre-event self-test**: failing to do a full from-memory reconstruction before a high-stakes recall.
