# Using Agent Based Modelling to make better decisions

Agent-based modelling simulates individual travelers as autonomous agents with distinct goals and constraints, letting decision-makers test how transport policies, technologies, and shocks affect different communities and emergent system outcomes.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat each traveler as an autonomous agent with unique characteristics, goals, and travel-behavior factors.
- Model individual decisions about whether, when, and how to travel rather than averaging behavior across groups.
- Simulate how each agent's choices interact across an area or city to produce system-level patterns.
- Combine new datasets and cloud computing to build and run models faster and at larger scales.
- Start with existing datasets and models for quick deployment, or build targeted small-area models and scale up to national populations.
- Test the likely impacts of transport policies on different communities, such as rural versus urban effects of road tolls.
- Map complex outcomes such as neighborhood walking or cycling schemes' effects on air quality.
- Estimate medium- and long-term impacts of new transport interventions and emerging technologies like autonomous vehicles.
- Capture the demands and preferences of distinct groups to create a holistic picture for decision-making.
- Shift decision support from predicting average behavior across groups to understanding individual reactions to changing circumstances.
- Use agent-based models to build confidence about which transport changes will achieve desired equitable, lower-emission, accessible outcomes.

## Failure modes named
- Relying on aggregate or average-behavior assumptions that mask real individual travel patterns.
- Assuming rigid daily routines (e.g., nursery drop-off then direct commute) when real days involve chained trips, errands, home working, and off-site meetings.
- Using broad assumptions about people's movements and behaviors that are no longer accurate enough for future transport investment decisions.
- Expecting travel behavior to follow regular, predictable patterns despite constant evolution and unexpected shocks.
