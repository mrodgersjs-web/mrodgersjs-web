# How to Find Product-Market-Fit as Fast as Possible (CEO Explains)

A practical walkthrough of the Superhuman-style PMF survey loop: measure disappointment, segment users, double down on what power users love, and iteratively convert the "somewhat disappointed" into fanatics until you cross the 40% threshold.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Quantify PMF with the Rahul Vora "very disappointed" test: ask users "How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?" and target ≥40% answering "very disappointed."
- Send the four-question PMF survey: disappointment level, who would benefit most, the #1 benefit received, and how to improve.
- Expect low early scores (e.g., 10% very disappointed) and treat them as normal diagnostic data, not a verdict.
- Segment responses into three cohorts: very disappointed (power users), somewhat disappointed (casual fans), and not disappointed (poor fit).
- Temporarily ignore the "not disappointed" cohort; they are not your customer and their feedback will dilute your focus.
- Mine the very-disappointed cohort for patterns in user persona, use case, and highest-valued benefits.
- Double down on the #1 benefit power users love by shipping more features that amplify that exact outcome.
- Study the somewhat-disappointed cohort to identify the one or two blockers preventing casual fans from becoming fanatics.
- Iterate in tight loops: ship improvements, rerun the survey, and repeat until the "very disappointed" score climbs past 40%.
- Reframe PMF from an abstract concept to a repeatable, measurable feeling: users "ripping the product off the shelves."

## Failure modes named
- Treating PMF as a vague concept instead of a quantifiable metric.
- Listening to users who are not disappointed; they pull the product away from its true market.
- Aiming to please everyone rather than serving the narrow persona that already loves the product.
- Assuming the first version should score well, then abandoning the loop because initial scores are low.
- Building features based on aggregate feedback instead of segmenting by disappointment level.
- Ignoring the "somewhat disappointed" middle cohort, leaving growth on the table after the power-user base is locked in.
