# How A True Polymath Learns.

Benjamin Franklin became a self-made polymath not through formal schooling or innate genius, but through a set of repeatable learning habits: spaced reconstruction, Socratic inquiry, pragmatic experimentation, cross-field principle transfer, deliberate community, relentless reading, and interest-driven craft obsession.

## Procedure / models distilled
- **Spaced reconstruction (anti-forgetting curve):** Read a source, set it aside for a few days, then reconstruct it from memory in your own words; compare with the original and note gaps. This combines spaced repetition with self-teaching/Feynman-style explanation.
- **Socratic/soft inquiry:** Replace confrontational debate with a sequence of gentle, innocent questions that surface the other person's assumptions and refine both parties' thinking.
- **Pragmatic experimentation over book learning:** Test ideas by interacting with the world; prioritize knowing how nature acts and what is useful over abstract theoretical explanations.
- **Cross-field principle transfer:** Treat disciplines as connected; extract underlying principles (e.g., exponential growth) that can be applied across science, business, self-improvement, and politics.
- **Junto-style sharpening circle:** Form a regular discussion group of mixed backgrounds where ideas are debated without fondness for dispute or desire of victory, so ideas and people are refined.
- **Learn from everyone regardless of status:** Use the Socratic method to seek insight from any person, not only elites, keeping ego from blocking learning.
- **Relentless, opportunistic reading:** Read voraciously even when access is limited; treat reading as focused mental training that improves conversation and independent thinking.
- **Follow interests into craft obsession:** Choose work aligned with genuine fascination, then experiment playfully within it, as interest sustains deep practice and invention.
- **Strengthen all sides before deciding:** When exploring a topic, think deeply and examine multiple sides of an argument rather than rushing to a conclusion.

## Failure modes named
- **Passive consumption without retrieval:** Reading or watching without later reconstructing the material from memory, letting the forgetting curve win.
- **Ego-driven argument:** Debating to win rather than to learn, which offends others and blocks new information.
- **Over-reliance on theory without experiment:** Staying in abstractions and never testing ideas against reality, stalling innovation.
- **Siloed thinking:** Treating fields as unrelated, missing cross-disciplinary principles and solutions.
- **Outsourcing thinking:** Relying on employees, AI, or short-form media to do your thinking, eroding focus and depth.
- **Trend-chasing over interest:** Choosing paths based on market growth or money rather than genuine curiosity, reducing persistence and creativity.
