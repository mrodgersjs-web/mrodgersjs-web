# Growth Loops vs Funnels: Real World Applications

Growth loops are self-reinforcing cycles where inputs use a product to create value, and that value is reinvested to generate new inputs, making growth more scalable and efficient than linear funnels.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define a growth loop as input → product use creates value → value reinvested to generate new inputs, with the goal of running as long as possible with minimal new input.
- Map the customer journey first: understand how customers find the product, why they start using it, and what triggers them to pay or upgrade.
- Identify the primary growth loop by asking where most users already come from and whether that channel is repeatable rather than one-off.
- Sketch the loop qualitatively before quantifying it: list each step and the hand-off from one step to the next.
- Quantify each step with dashboard-style metrics rather than obsessing over precise attribution; track inputs, throughputs, and outputs.
- Optimize loops by asking three questions: how to maximize the absolute impact of each step, minimize friction between steps, and increase the speed of the loop.
- Recognize four main acquisition loop archetypes: viral loops, content loops, paid loops, and sales loops.
- Use viral loops when users naturally share artifacts that expose new users to the product.
- Use content loops when product actions generate indexable or shareable material that attracts organic traffic.
- Use paid loops when revenue from users can be reinvested into paid marketing channels at increasing scale.
- Use sales loops when revenue can be reinvested into sales team capacity, tooling, and process to drive more closures.
- Extend a primary loop by adding reinforcing paths rather than building entirely separate loops.
- Align on the primary growth loop cross-functionally with sales, marketing, and product to avoid siloed ownership of acquisition, retention, and monetization.

## Failure modes named
- Treating funnels and loops as fundamentally different rather than complementary models for understanding growth channels.
- Building funnels where outputs never feed back into inputs, creating optimization ceilings but no scalability.
- Letting functional silos split ownership of the growth system across marketing, product, sales, and operations.
- Relying on one-off channels like Product Hunt as a primary growth mechanism because they cannot be repeated.
- Overvaluing K factor as a decision metric when it is hard to measure, rarely stays above one for long, and can still be valuable below one.
- Creating separate loops instead of reinforcing an already working primary loop.
- Deciding the primary growth loop in isolation within the product team instead of transparently with marketing and sales.
