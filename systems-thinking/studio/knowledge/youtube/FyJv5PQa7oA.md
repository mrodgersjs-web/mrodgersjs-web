# Burnout Blockers #5: Plan Lessons Better with Premortems

Use a premortem—imagining a planned decision has already failed and working backward to identify likely causes—to make lesson plans, classroom management choices, and curriculum changes more realistic and robust.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define the decision or plan you are considering (e.g., a week of lessons, a classroom change, or a curriculum shift).
- Imagine the decision has already been implemented and the outcome was a failure.
- Envision the failure in concrete detail: what exactly went wrong in the classroom?
- Work backward from that imagined failure to identify the likely causes of the problems.
- Use those causes to generate more questions and targeted research before finalizing the plan.
- Make small tweaks to the plan to prevent the identified problems.
- Be willing to abandon the option entirely if the premortem reveals a better alternative.
- Apply premortems to lesson planning by considering students’ cognitive development and prior knowledge.
- Apply premortems to classroom setup and social dynamics, not just content.
- Recognize that imagining failure is preparation, not pessimism.

## Failure modes named
- Treating “what could go wrong?” as an afterthought instead of a built-in planning step.
- Picking a living situation, lesson plan, or curriculum change based only on surface positives.
- Ignoring mismatched schedules, social dynamics, and resource-sharing issues.
- Failing to envision concrete failure details, which limits the ability to find causes.
- Confusing negative scenario thinking with pessimism and avoiding it.
