# Emergent Complexity

Emergence is the phenomenon where simple building blocks following local rules produce complex collective behaviors and properties that none of the parts possess alone.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define emergence as simple parts plus local rules producing complex wholes with novel properties, as seen in water molecules forming snowflakes and surface tension, neurons forming brains, and ants forming colonies.
- Identify the two primitives of any emergent system: building blocks that can be combined, and rules that govern how they interact.
- Exploit combinatorial explosion, where the number of possible arrangements grows exponentially with the number of parts, creating vast possibility spaces.
- Build hierarchical layers of emergence by combining lower-level blocks into higher-level blocks, such as atoms forming molecules, cells, organs, and organisms.
- Use cellular automata as a model: apply simple local update rules to a grid of cells repeatedly and observe global patterns evolve.
- Accept computational irreducibility, the property that some rule systems cannot be shortcut by formula and must be run to know their outcomes.
- Recognize chaos and the butterfly effect, where tiny changes in initial conditions compound into drastically different long-term outcomes.
- Distinguish pockets of reducibility, like planetary orbits, from computationally irreducible domains like weather and many complex systems.
- Practice design by emergence: invent building blocks and rules, then discover consequences rather than scripting every detail by hand.
- Iterate experimentally by running, observing, tinkering, and repeating to find rule sets that produce interesting behavior.
- Apply an evolutionary approach by generating small variations of a working system, selecting the most promising, and repeating.
- Accept the control problem: controlling emergent behavior without killing it is difficult, so expect unintended consequences and exploits.
- Respond to emergent misbehavior by adjusting rules after the fact, such as nerfing or buffing mechanics, rather than trying to pre-design every outcome.

## Failure modes named
- Assuming simple rules guarantee simple outcomes and failing to experiment.
- Trying to design every detail of a complex system directly instead of designing blocks and rules and letting consequences emerge.
- Over-controlling an emergent system so tightly that the interesting self-organization dies.
- Ignoring unintended consequences, exploits, and nasty side effects until they become entrenched.
- Treating computationally irreducible systems as if they can be predicted with closed-form equations.
- Clinging to pre-existing ideas and refusing to throw out prototypes that are not producing interesting emergence.
