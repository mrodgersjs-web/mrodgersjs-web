# Introduction to Stock Flow Diagrams

Three-minute primer: what goes in a stock-flow diagram and why building one changes the conversation.

## Procedure / models distilled
- An SFD shows the parts of a system and how they affect one another — a visual representation of how the system works (0:04–0:12).
- **Stocks** = accumulations, drawn as boxes; **flows** = rates of change that raise or lower them (0:12–0:24).
- The tool's real effect: shifts focus from the individuals in the system to the structure they form together (0:26–0:39).
- Accumulations are everywhere and usually invisible because they're obvious: animal populations, a person's happiness level, snow on the ground, water in a lake, grass on rangeland, trees in a forest (0:39–1:11).
- Combinatorics: inflow-only ⇒ stock rises; outflow-only ⇒ falls; both ⇒ can move either way (1:12–1:27).
- Build procedure: pick anything that accumulates → box it as a stock → add elements that affect the flows (1:30–1:48).
- Use the diagram as a **conversation artifact**: two people diagram the same target (e.g., a literary character's happiness) differently, and comparing the two diagrams exposes and refines each party's mental model (1:53–2:23).

## Failure modes named
- Designing around individual actors instead of the accumulation structure they produce (0:26–0:30).
- Debating mental models in words when a shared diagram would localize the disagreement (2:06–2:23).
