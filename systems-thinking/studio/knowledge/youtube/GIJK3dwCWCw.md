# Immune System, Part 1: Crash Course Anatomy & Physiology #45

The immune system enforces a layered defense policy—starting with innate physical and chemical barriers, then internal nonspecific cells and inflammation, and finally calling in the adaptive, target-specific response when needed.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Layered defense policy: innate external barriers → innate internal defenses → adaptive specific defenses, with each layer escalating only when the prior one is breached.
- Physical barrier model: skin (keratinized epithelium) and mucous membranes lining respiratory, digestive, urinary, and reproductive tracts block pathogen entry.
- Chemical barrier model: stomach acid, sticky mucus, antimicrobial enzymes in saliva and tears, and defensin peptides in skin/membranes kill or trap microbes.
- Phagocyte mechanism: "eating cells" engulf invaders; neutrophils are abundant, short-lived, and form pus when they die, while macrophages (differentiated from monocytes) patrol or anchor in tissues and can eat repeatedly.
- Natural killer cell mechanism: NK cells patrol blood and lymph, detect host cells that have lost MHC1 surface proteins, and trigger apoptosis in infected or cancerous cells.
- Inflammatory response model: injured tissue mast cells release histamine, causing vasodilation (redness/heat), capillary permeability (swelling), and recruitment of phagocytes and lymphocytes to the site.
- Neutrophil recruitment model: injured cells signal leukocytosis from bone marrow, capillary endothelial cells release homing chemicals, and neutrophils adhere to vessel walls, flatten, and squeeze through to reach the wound.
- Macrophage cleanup wave: monocytes arrive at inflamed tissue, transform into macrophages, replace dead neutrophils, and consume lingering pathogens plus cellular debris.
- Fever mechanism: overwhelmed phagocytes release pyrogens that raise the hypothalamic thermostat, increasing cellular metabolism for faster healing and causing liver/spleen to sequester iron/zinc away from bacteria.
- Adaptive immune system as specialist backup: slower to mobilize but target-specific and capable of immunological memory for faster future responses.

## Failure modes named
- Misreading healing signs as illness: redness, heat, swelling, pain, and fever are defensive responses, not purely symptoms of disease.
- Breached physical barriers: cuts, scrapes, or mucosal damage let pathogens bypass the first line of defense.
- Local immune overload: major injury or severe infection can overwhelm neutrophils and macrophages before adaptive defenses arrive.
- Assuming simple means weak: brute-force innate defenses (barriers, acid, fever) are evolutionarily effective even if not subtle.
