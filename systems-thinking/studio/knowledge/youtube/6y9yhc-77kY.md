# Acting From Strength: Achieving Deterrence In Foreign Policy | Intellections

Deterrence is the practice of preventing an unwanted military action by convincing an adversary that the costs of acting will outweigh the benefits, achieved through capability, credible commitment, and clear communication.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define deterrence as preventing an unwanted action by threatening unacceptable consequences.
- Build the capability to act from strength across military, economic, and political domains.
- Recognize that strength expands the menu of options for compelling acceptable behavior.
- Pair capability with a visible willingness to follow through on threats.
- Treat credibility as a strategic asset: adversaries must believe consequences are real.
- Never make empty threats; doing so undermines future deterrence.
- Communicate clearly and in advance what behavior is unacceptable and what punishment will follow.
- Ensure that when red lines are crossed, consequences are delivered without ambiguity or surprise.
- Shape the adversary’s decision calculus so that restraint becomes its most attractive option.

## Failure modes named
- Empty threats that signal weakness and erode credibility.
- Adversaries concluding there will be no consequences for defiance.
- Over-reliance on military capability without the political will to use it.
- Ambiguous communication that leaves adversaries unsure where red lines lie.
- Failure to impose promised consequences after a violation.
