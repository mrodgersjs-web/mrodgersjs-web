# Analogical Reasoning- The Closest Thing To Pure Intelligence

Analogical reasoning—the deliberate identification of shared relational structure across different domains—is presented as the core mechanism of fluid intelligence and the strongest lever for generating novel knowledge.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define analogical reasoning as identifying structural similarity (not surface similarity) between two domains and using it to solve a problem or generate new knowledge.
- Use Gentner's structural mapping theory as the foundational framework: an analogy maps relational structure from a known source domain onto a novel target domain.
- Distinguish three types of similarity: mere appearance (same objects, no analogy), analogical similarity (different objects, same relational structure), and mere relational similarity (shared abstract structure only).
- Apply the systematicity principle: the deeper and more interconnected the relational correspondence, the stronger and more valid the analogy.
- Follow the four-stage analogical process: retrieval of a source analog, mapping of relational structure, evaluation of systematicity, and inference/abstraction to generate new knowledge or schemas.
- Enforce two strict mapping rules: one-to-one correspondence (each element maps to exactly one counterpart) and parallel connectivity (map a relation together with its arguments, not selectively).
- Treat semantic distance as a variable: near analogies are low demand and low creative yield, while far analogies require more cognitive resources and produce stronger insights.
- Build far analogies deliberately by pairing domains with almost zero surface overlap but a shared relational skeleton.
- Study analogous cases simultaneously (comparative exposure) rather than sequentially, because comparison forces relational encoding instead of surface encoding.
- First build deep domain knowledge, then cross-pollinate that structure into other fields; you can only map structures you already possess.
- Train working memory as the bottleneck for holding two structural systems in mind during mapping.
- Write analogies down, classify them as surface or structural, and refactor surface matches into deeper relational mappings to increase meta-awareness.

## Failure modes named
- Retrieval defaults to surface similarity, causing novices to retrieve objects that merely look alike rather than systems that share relational structure.
- Isolated surface matches produce weak analogies with little explanatory or predictive power.
- Attribute matches (e.g., "both are wise") are mistaken for true analogical reasoning, which requires matching relationships between pairs, not attributes of individual entities.
- Cherry-picking arguments or relations violates one-to-one correspondence and parallel connectivity, breaking the logical coherence of the mapping.
- Accepting inferences that lack relational support in the source domain produces invalid conclusions.
- Staying with near analogies limits cognitive demand and creative output, preventing the representational updates that far analogies provide.
