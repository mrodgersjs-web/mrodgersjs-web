# Explaining the viral coefficient formula: Lesson 95

The video teaches the viral coefficient formula as a measurable way to judge whether shared content will sustain itself or die off by comparing newly engaged people to the original audience reached.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define virality as second-order sharing producing equal or greater reach than the original post.
- Start with the original number of people reached by a post.
- Multiply original reach by the number of shares generated from that audience.
- Estimate total potential secondary reach by multiplying shares by each sharer's average network size.
- Apply an engagement rate to the potential secondary reach to find actual newly engaged people.
- Compute the viral coefficient by dividing newly engaged people by the original audience reached.
- Interpret a coefficient of 1 or greater as self-sustaining viral growth.
- Interpret a coefficient below 1 as inevitable decay of the sharing loop.
- Increase the coefficient by improving headlines, images, and overall content interest to lift sharing.
- Treat the coefficient as a barometer rather than a precise prediction, especially when exact reach is unknown.
- Build intuitive judgment by tracking engagement numbers, comments, and post longevity over time.
- Use a calculator tool to operationalize the formula without doing manual math.

## Failure modes named
- Treating virality as pure luck or myth instead of a plannable, measurable mechanism.
- Waiting for exact reach data before applying the framework.
- Expecting viral posts immediately rather than iterating with practice.
- Ignoring small content optimizations that can push the coefficient above the critical 1 threshold.
- Confusing total potential secondary reach with actual newly engaged people.
