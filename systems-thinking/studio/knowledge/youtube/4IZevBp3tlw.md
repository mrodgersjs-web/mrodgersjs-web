# Think More Clearly with Bayesian Reasoning

Bayesian reasoning is the practice of holding provisional degrees of belief (credences), assigning probabilities to hypotheses, and revising those probabilities as new evidence accumulates.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Think in degrees of belief rather than binary true/false judgments.
- Treat hypotheses as supportable or falsifiable, but never finally provable to 100% certainty.
- Assign prior credences to propositions before evaluating new evidence.
- Update credences continuously as fresh information and data arrive.
- Rely on converging evidence from many independent angles to increase confidence.
- Hold even strongly supported beliefs as provisional and revisable.
- Apply a simplicity rule of thumb: give simpler theories higher prior likelihoods than more complicated ones.
- Remain aware that any analysis may have overlooked evidence or may be falsified by future data.
- Distinguish "we are uncertain" from "all possibilities are equally likely."

## Failure modes named
- Binary or black-and-white thinking that forces claims into simply true or false categories.
- Believing a hypothesis can be definitively proved rather than only supported or disproven.
- Throwing up hands and treating every possibility as equally likely just because uncertainty exists.
- Overlooking a critical data point that could disprove a currently held belief.
- Practicing Bayesian updating inconsistently or less effectively.
