# North Star Metric for Customer Support Dept

The video argues that most customer support metrics measure process speed or satisfaction rather than real problem resolution, and proposes replacing them with a single binary post-interaction question—"Is your problem solved? Yes/No"—that becomes the department's North Star metric.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Reframe the purpose of customer support as solving the customer's actual problem rather than handling tickets.
- Audit existing support metrics against whether they genuinely prove the customer's problem was resolved.
- Distinguish process metrics (first response time, tickets closed, ticket resolution time) from outcome metrics (problem solved).
- Recognize that NPS and conversation ratings conflate the quality of the agent interaction with whether the issue was fixed.
- Deploy a binary post-interaction question to the customer: "Is your problem solved? Yes / No."
- Track the proportion of "Yes" responses as the primary outcome indicator for the support department.
- Treat every "No" response as a signal to open a follow-up conversation rather than as a final outcome.
- Follow up with customers who answer "No," resolve the remaining issue, then re-survey them.
- Only close a ticket once the customer has shifted their answer from "No" to "Yes."
- Interpret clusters of "No" responses as evidence of systemic gaps in training, data access, or empowerment rather than as individual agent failure.
- Use the binary outcome metric to locate where the support process breaks down upstream.
- Set the department's core goal as converting "No" outcomes into "Yes" outcomes.

## Failure modes named
- Optimizing first response time while the customer's underlying issue remains unresolved.
- Counting auto-closed tickets as evidence of successful resolution.
- Using ticket resolution time as a proxy for whether the customer problem was actually solved.
- Treating high NPS or conversation ratings as proof of problem resolution.
- Confusing a pleasant, friendly support interaction with a successful support interaction.
- Closing tickets before confirming the customer no longer has the problem.
- Blaming support agents for unresolved issues that stem from missing data, tools, or organizational empowerment.
