# Characteristics of Healthcare Organizations Seeking High Reliability: A Learning Culture

High-reliability healthcare organizations operate as learning cultures that convert reports of good catches, unsafe conditions, and system failures into process improvements and safer patient care.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat a learning culture as the operational lifeblood of a high-reliability organization.
- Learn from failures, near misses, and unsafe conditions rather than ignore or punish them.
- Translate learnings into improved processes that restore normal operations quickly.
- Frame reporting as a contribution to organizational learning, not as blame or tattling.
- Ask questions openly: no question is a “dumb question,” and everyone is entitled to be more informed about operations.
- Capture learning opportunities through three channels: good catches, unsafe conditions, and system failures.
- Feed every report into a continuous improvement loop that improves the care provided.
- Reinforce that reporting patient-safety concerns supports the highest quality and safest care for patients.
- Provide a clear, accessible channel for staff to submit learning opportunities.
- Build psychological safety so staff feel safe to speak up, question, and report.

## Failure modes named
- Treating questions as dumb or unwelcome, which suppresses inquiry.
- Failing to report good catches, unsafe conditions, or system failures.
- Losing learning opportunities because there is no clear reporting channel.
- Not applying reported learnings back into improved processes.
- Delaying return to normal operations after a failure because lessons are never implemented.
- Allowing a culture where staff fear blame and stop speaking up.
