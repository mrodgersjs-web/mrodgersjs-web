# Thinking in Systems, Ch. 6: Leverage Points in Systems (Donella Meadows)

Chapter walkthrough of Meadows' leverage-points essay, presented most-effective to least-effective.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Leverage points = places to intervene in a system; Meadows ranks them least to most effective, and the walkthrough reverses that order (0:02–0:31).
- Most powerful: **paradigm shifts** — the shared beliefs about how the world works (tax fairness 0:43, land ownership 1:32, "growth is good" 2:04, "paid less = worth less" 2:13).
- Paradigm-shift method: name the current paradigm's failure over and over (2:45), ignore reactionaries who benefit from the old paradigm (2:52), put new-paradigm people in influencer positions (3:09), target the persuadable middle not the resisters (3:30).
- "Transcending paradigms" is a warning not to get locked into even your own improved paradigm (3:41–4:04).
- **System goals**: the apparent goal is often not the real one — profit is a requirement to stay in the game; the perceived altruistic goal actually structures people's effort and incentives (4:08–5:45).
- **Self-organization**: a system's ability to change itself; the designer's job is to set the rules and goals for reorganization so the system evolves in the better direction from within (5:48–6:32).
- **Rules and information flows**: "to understand the deepest malfunctions of systems, pay attention to the rules and who has power over them" (6:35–7:14). Adding a missing information flow (e.g., visible fish-stock level for fishers) changes behavior without coercion (7:24–8:14).
- **Feedback loops**: slow down runaway reinforcing loops (rich-get-richer; counter with universal education, progressive taxation) (8:29–9:13); protect balancing loops that actors quietly remove — rest/vacation, antitrust, truth-in-advertising, whistleblower and FOI laws are all balancing loops under attack (9:15–11:12).
- **Buffers and delays**: a stabilizing stock relative to its flows creates stability (lakes flood less than rivers); buffers work but are expensive and slow to change (11:15–12:46).
- **Physical stock-and-flow structure** (infrastructure): transformational but costly and hard, so ranked low as a first move (13:11–13:31).
- Least effective: **numbers/parameters** (tax rates, budgets, caps) — tweaking them rarely changes system behavior because the action is in the structure of flows (13:34–14:10); they matter only when a parameter touches something higher on this list (14:40).

## Failure modes named
- Spending political/organizational energy on parameter tweaks while structure, rules, goals, and paradigms run untouched (13:36–14:00).
- Deleting balancing loops whose purpose is not obvious (rest, antitrust) and wondering why problems escalate (9:26–10:15).
- Assuming the stated goal is the operative goal; incentives follow the real one (4:44–5:22).
- Wasting effort converting resisters instead of moving the middle (3:30–3:38).
