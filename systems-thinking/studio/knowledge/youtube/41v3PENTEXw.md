# What is Organization Design?

Organization design is the practice of aligning strategy, capabilities, structure, processes, metrics/rewards, and people practices through a structured design process so an organization can execute its strategy effectively.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Use Galbraith's Star Model as the core framework: strategy defines the goal and path; capabilities are the differentiators required to execute that strategy; and four levers create alignment.
- Treat structure as a primary but blunt instrument: decide how to organize, define key roles, and allocate power, while recognizing that every structure creates silos and boundaries.
- Design processes to knit the organization across structural boundaries by intentionally mapping how work flows between roles and how decisions are made.
- Use management processes as linkage mechanisms that create the right conversations and connect people in alignment with structure.
- Deploy metrics and rewards as underutilized levers to define business success and shape individual and team behavior toward strategic goals.
- Build people practices around the talent profiles, competencies, and HR processes needed to attract, retain, and develop the right people.
- Treat culture as an outcome, not a direct design lever: identify desired behaviors during strategy development, then recognize that culture emerges from how the organization works.
- Begin any design effort by understanding current strategy, required capabilities, and the current operating model.
- Frame Milestone 1 as business case and discovery: define the problem, organizational implications of strategy, and the gap between current and future state.
- Combine strategic grouping and integration work by choosing a basic structure and simultaneously designing the linkages across boundaries.
- Run the design process participatively, involving people from across the organization to create, iterate, evaluate, and prototype options before finalizing decisions.
- Staff pivotal roles with the right talent, treating organization design as unfinished until leadership and talent choices are made.
- Plan the transition from current to future state as a continuous learning and adjustment process, treating organization design as ongoing leadership work.

## Failure modes named
- Relying on structure alone as a sufficient lever, ignoring the silos and boundaries it creates.
- Underutilizing metrics and rewards to guide behavior and signal success.
- Attempting to design culture directly rather than shaping it through aligned systems and practices.
- Treating organization design as complete without making the right talent choices for pivotal roles.
- Failing to design linkages across structural boundaries, leaving handoffs and decision rights unresolved.
- Skipping discovery and business-case work, designing without clarity on the strategic problem and gap.
- Treating organization design as a one-time project rather than an ongoing process of transition and continuous improvement.
