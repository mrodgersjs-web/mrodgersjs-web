# 1: Introduction to Systems Thinking | Causal Loop Diagrams

An introductory overview of systems thinking for community groups, presenting causal loop diagrams and social network analysis as mapping tools to understand complex, dynamic problems and inform strategic planning.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat complex social problems as **wicked problems**: dynamic, interconnected, with no single certain answer and unintended consequences.
- Apply a **systems thinking lens**: look at the whole system, its interconnections, feedback loops, and changing variables rather than isolating one part.
- Use **causal loop diagrams (CLDs)** to map cause-effect relationships and the feedback loops that amplify or change conditions over time.
- Embrace **non-linear causality** in CLDs: an intervention on one variable can loop back and reinforce or alter the original condition, reflecting real-world complexity.
- Draw CLD data from mixed sources: academic literature, gray literature and policy documents, qualitative interviews, quantitative surveys, stakeholder discussions, and community members’ lived experience.
- Use **social network analysis (SNA)** to visualize how organizations interact, communicate, and collaborate around themes such as information sharing, service coordination, planning, and advocacy.
- Identify **key organizations and collaboration opportunities** through SNA to strengthen multi-actor responses to the problem.
- Shift from a **siloed service model** toward a streamlined, client-centered system where organizations function as a coherent network, coalition, or partnership.
- Combine CLDs and SNA to create shared visuals that support **dialogue among stakeholders** and more informed decisions about where to intervene.
- Follow the training package as a **digital case study**: short videos, stage-by-stage deliverables, and PDF resources guide a community group through applying the tools.

## Failure modes named
- **Cognitive overload**: the human brain can only hold a limited number of variables at once, making mental systems mapping unreliable.
- **Failure to simulate downstream effects**: it is difficult to imagine how changing one part of a system will ripple through other parts.
- **Single-part fixation**: focusing on one element of the problem instead of the whole system misses feedback loops and unintended consequences.
- **Siloed organizations**: services organized around institutions rather than around the client create gaps (“cracks”) that prevent the system from fully functioning.
- **Linear cause-effect assumptions**: expecting straightforward one-way relationships ignores the amplifying feedback loops that drive real-world dynamics.
- **Underserved groups staying under the radar**: populations with high newcomer or minority representation may lack advocacy skills or awareness of services, allowing low-resource conditions to persist.
