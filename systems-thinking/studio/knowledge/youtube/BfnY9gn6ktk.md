# What are complex adaptive systems?

A short introduction defining complex adaptive systems as multi-scale, connected, ever-changing systems that are unpredictable, contagious, and resilient through modularity and redundancy.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Use the three-word lens—**complex**, **adaptive**, **system**—to recognize a CAS: many connected parts, constant change, and dynamics spanning scales.
- Map the working parts and their connections to identify where complexity comes from.
- Treat the system as dynamic, not static: species evolve, behaviors change, and rules update over time.
- Analyze across scales, from individual interactions to population-level patterns.
- Expect nonlinearity: small inputs can produce large, disproportionate outputs, making precise forecasting unreliable.
- Look for contagion pathways; high connectivity lets disturbances, ideas, or diseases spread rapidly.
- Detect modularity by finding clusters that are more connected internally than with the rest of the system.
- Assess redundancy by checking whether similar functions are duplicated across modules.
- Combine modularity and redundancy to evaluate and design resilience—the capacity to absorb damage without losing function.
- Apply the CAS frame to social, ecological, and economic domains to anticipate crises rather than assume stability.

## Failure modes named
- Catastrophic collapse or crisis when CAS dynamics are ignored, as seen in the 2008 financial crisis, pandemics, and global climate change.
- Rapid contagion of disease, misinformation, or shocks through tightly connected networks.
- Loss of resilience when modularity and redundancy are eroded, leaving the system vulnerable to localized failures.
- Misjudging CAS as predictable or linear, leading to interventions that fail because future states cannot be reliably forecast.
