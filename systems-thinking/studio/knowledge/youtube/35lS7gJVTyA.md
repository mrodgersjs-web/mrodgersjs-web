# Improve Your Baseline Dopamine for Motivation & Drive | Dr. Andrew Huberman

Dr. Andrew Huberman explains that healthy baseline dopamine is a prerequisite for motivation, then offers zero-cost daily practices—sleep, non-sleep deep rest, tyrosine-rich nutrition, morning sunlight, and regular movement—that restore and sustain it.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat baseline dopamine as the "water in the wave pool": without enough baseline, you cannot generate motivational peaks or troughs.
- Get sufficient quality sleep every night; sleep literally restores dopamine reserves and lets you meaningfully consider goals.
- Practice non-sleep deep rest (NSDR), similar to yoga nidra, using a body scan and long exhale breathing for 10–30 minutes to replenish dopamine reserves.
- Consume tyrosine-rich foods—Parmesan cheese, certain meats, nuts, and vegetables—to supply the rate-limiting amino acid for dopamine synthesis.
- View morning sunlight as early as possible: 5–10 minutes on a clear day, 10–20 on cloudy, 20–30 on overcast, without sunglasses, facing eastward but not staring at the sun.
- Use morning sunlight to trigger a cortisol and dopamine-related cascade that elevates mood, alertness, and baseline dopamine throughout the day.
- Exercise regularly, at least five days per week, combining cardiovascular and resistance training to maintain elevated baseline dopamine.
- Recognize that movement circuits such as the nigrostriatal pathway support, and interact with, goal-seeking dopamine circuits.
- Re-up sleep, nutrients, sunlight, and movement on a roughly 24-hour cycle; these are daily maintenance practices, not one-time fixes.
- Acknowledge that genetics and circumstance create baseline variation and differences in "activation energy," but foundational practices remain essential for everyone.

## Failure modes named
- Chronic sleep deprivation drains dopamine reserves and makes goal pursuit feel impossible.
- Confusing traditional meditation with a dopamine booster; meditation is a focus exercise with little evidence for raising dopamine.
- Skipping morning sunlight or wearing sunglasses, missing the cortisol-dopamine timing that supports daytime motivation.
- Treating exercise only as a source of euphoric peaks instead of a daily baseline maintenance tool.
- Neglecting tyrosine-containing nutrition, limiting the raw material needed to synthesize dopamine.
- Using genetic or circumstantial differences as a reason to skip the foundational daily practices.
