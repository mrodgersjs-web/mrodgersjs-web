# What is Your Product's North Star by MyFitnessPal Senior PM

A MyFitnessPal Senior PM defines a product North Star as the user-centered, emotional long-term vision that gives direction to every feature, stakeholder decision, and metric, while remaining open to iteration.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define the North Star as the user's emotional "aha moment" rather than a numeric target like user count, revenue, retention, or conversion.
- Separate short-term business metrics (e.g., Airbnb bookings, Spotify streams) from the enduring directional goal (e.g., feeling like a local, music everywhere).
- Validate market fit for the North Star before committing significant engineering or design effort.
- Run user research with explicit guardrails against confirmation bias, leading questions, and hypothesis-proving inquiries.
- Frame the vision as a simple, user-understandable story rather than an internal business abstraction.
- Carve out recurring time for long-term thinking across horizons from quarters to five-plus years, outside the daily feature grind.
- Take informal ownership of the vision even when formal authority has not been granted to product.
- Anchor every function's plans back to the North Star during prioritization and execution.
- Repeat the vision continuously because stakeholders naturally drift toward short-term local goals.
- Translate the qualitative North Star into concrete quantitative proxies such as new users, conversion, retention, or engagement.
- Treat the North Star as a living hypothesis that evolves with company stage, user behavior, competition, and resources.
- Apply speed/quality/cost trade-offs without letting the North Star itself be compromised.

## Failure modes named
- Confusing revenue or output metrics with the North Star.
- Treating the North Star as an immutable slogan once announced.
- Building toward a vision without validating that users actually want it.
- Conducting research that only confirms the PM's existing hypothesis.
- Communicating the vision in internal jargon that users cannot feel or understand.
- Letting daily features, requests, and stakeholder noise crowd out long-term vision work.
- Assuming leadership or someone else owns the vision so the PM stays silent.
- Allowing sales, marketing, and engineering to optimize disconnected local goals.
- Compromising the vision itself under speed, cost, or quality pressure.
- Clinging sentimentally to an outdated vision after market signals shift.
- Keeping the vision purely qualitative without identifiable measurable metrics.
