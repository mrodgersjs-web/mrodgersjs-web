# Micro-Canonical Ensemble - Statistical Mechanics

The micro-canonical ensemble describes an isolated collection of systems where every member has fixed energy, volume, and particle number, and each accessible microstate occurs with equal probability.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define the ensemble as the set of all microstates consistent with fixed total energy E, volume V, and particle number N.
- Treat each member of the ensemble as isolated: impermeable walls prevent particle exchange, rigid walls prevent volume change, and adiabatic walls prevent heat exchange.
- Assign equal a priori probability to every allowed microstate: P_i = 1/Ω, where Ω is the total number of accessible microstates.
- Assign probability zero to any microstate that does not satisfy the fixed E, V, N constraints.
- Normalize probabilities so that the sum over all allowed microstates equals one.
- Compute Boltzmann entropy for the ensemble as S = k_B ln(Ω).
- Recognize that entropy remains constant because E, V, and N are fixed and Ω does not change.

## Failure modes named
- Extending the uniform probability 1/Ω to states that violate the fixed E, V, or N constraints.
- Confusing the micro-canonical ensemble with canonical or grand canonical ensembles, where energy or particles can fluctuate.
- Forgetting that the equal-probability assumption applies only to accessible microstates, not to all conceivable states.
- Treating entropy as variable in an isolated system rather than constant for fixed Ω.
