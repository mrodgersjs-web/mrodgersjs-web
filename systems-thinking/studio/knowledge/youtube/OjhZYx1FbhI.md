# 16. Thermodynamics: Gibbs Free Energy and Entropy

A lecture on how Gibbs free energy (ΔG = ΔH − TΔS) predicts spontaneity, how entropy measures disorder/degrees of freedom, and how to calculate and interpret ΔG and ΔS for reactions.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define a spontaneous reaction at constant temperature and pressure as one that proceeds forward without outside intervention.
- Predict spontaneity using Gibbs free energy: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.
- Interpret ΔG signs: ΔG < 0 spontaneous forward, ΔG > 0 non-spontaneous, ΔG = 0 equilibrium.
- Compute ΔG at room temperature by converting ΔS from J/(K·mol) to kJ/(K·mol); assume T = 298 K when not specified.
- Calculate reaction entropy from standard absolute entropies: ΔS° = ΣS°(products) − ΣS°(reactants), with stoichiometric coefficients.
- Estimate the sign of ΔS from phase changes: gas > liquid > solid in disorder, so forming gas generally increases entropy.
- Treat entropy as a state function: only the change in disorder between initial and final states matters, not the path.
- Use the convention that products-minus-reactants applies to ΔS and ΔG of formation, unlike bond enthalpy which uses reactants-minus-products.
- Interpret ΔG° of formation: negative means the compound is stable relative to its elements; positive means it is thermodynamically unstable and tends toward elements.
- Separate thermodynamics from kinetics: ΔG says whether a process is favorable, not how fast it occurs; many spontaneous reactions are slow.
- Relate entropy to physical disorder and internal degrees of freedom, such as vibrations and freedom of motion in solids, liquids, and gases.
- Use the hydrogen peroxide “elephant toothpaste” demonstration as a concrete model of an entropy-increasing reaction producing gas.

## Failure modes named
- Treating ΔH alone as the determinant of spontaneity and assuming exothermic reactions are always spontaneous.
- Forgetting to convert ΔS from joules to kilojoules before plugging into ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.
- Applying multiplication/division significant-figure rules to addition/subtraction when computing ΔG.
- Confusing absolute entropy S with entropy change ΔS; S = 0 only for a perfect crystal at 0 K.
- Assuming a spontaneous reaction will proceed quickly; thermodynamics gives no information about reaction rate.
- Using reactants-minus-products for entropy or Gibbs free energy of formation instead of products-minus-reactants.
- Interpreting a negative ΔG of formation as implying the reverse reaction is impossible rather than non-spontaneous relative to the elements.
