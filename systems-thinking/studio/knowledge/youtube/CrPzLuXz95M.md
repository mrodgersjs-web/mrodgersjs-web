# The Most Powerful Technique To Learn Everything | Spaced Repetition

Spaced repetition counters the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve by revisiting learned material at expanding intervals, converting fragile short-term memory into durable long-term retention.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Spaced repetition is the practice of reviewing learned material at deliberately increasing time intervals rather than in a single cramming session.
- The Ebbinghaus forgetting curve models how memory drops steeply soon after learning and then continues to decay more slowly over time.
- Without review, roughly 75% of newly learned information is lost within 48 hours.
- Each spaced review interrupts the forgetting curve, resets retention to a higher baseline, and makes the next decay slower until memory plateaus in long-term storage.
- Combine spaced repetition with active recall and practice testing to slow the forgetting curve even further.
- In the Leitner system, sort flashcards into five boxes reviewed at expanding intervals (daily, weekly, every other week, monthly, mastered); move cards forward on success and backward on failure.
- The Leitner system ensures difficult material is reviewed more frequently while well-known material is reviewed less often.
- Build a spreadsheet revision timetable with topics listed in rows and review dates in columns, color-coded red (weak), orange (needs review), and green (learned).
- Prioritize red topics most frequently, then orange, aiming to turn every topic green before the exam.
- Use spaced-repetition software such as Anki to automate scheduling, presenting cards at intervals determined by how well you know them.
- Mobile apps let you exploit small pockets of idle time for micro-reviews instead of requiring long desk sessions.
- Avoid rigid intervals by adjusting box timings or review dates to match your own retention and the difficulty of the material.

## Failure modes named
- Cramming or re-reading a textbook repeatedly in one sitting is time-consuming and ineffective for long-term retention.
- Reviewing all topics equally often wastes time on material already mastered and neglects material that is fading fastest.
- Treating spaced repetition as passive re-exposure misses the added benefit of active recall and practice testing.
- Waiting until you are at a desk or have a large block of time ignores the opportunity for frequent short mobile reviews.
