# Keys to Becoming a High Reliability Organization

Building a high reliability organization requires embedding a culture of mindfulness around patient safety through education, storytelling, inclusive engagement, unit-based huddles, and transparent leadership that fosters trust.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Cultivate a culture of mindfulness where every associate is thoughtful about what might cause harm to patients.
- Build the mindset through education, teaching, and role modeling rather than relying on directives alone.
- Use stories—good catches, near misses, and transparent accounts of devastating errors—to make safety real and resonant for everyone.
- Equip healthcare workers with practical tools to become highly reliable once the rationale and benefits are explained.
- Engage physicians as visible leaders, champions, and role models for the safety culture.
- Place patient safety at the epicenter of care and involve every role, not just clinicians.
- Mobilize nurses, technicians, environmental workers, and all support staff as active contributors to safety.
- Hold daily safety huddles and check-ins; push them down to the unit level so frontline staff participate directly.
- Treat culture change as an organization-wide program requiring every clinical and non-clinical person to have the same investment and belief.
- Articulate a compelling vision of patient safety, quality, and service that is shared both top-down and bottom-up.
- Develop trust between leaders and staff as a prerequisite for influence and change.
- Share information openly and practice clear, transparent communication so people can act together.

## Failure modes named
- Relying on leaders alone while frontline and non-clinical staff remain uninvolved or uninvested.
- Confining safety huddles to higher organizational levels instead of embedding them in daily unit operations.
- Failing to be transparent about devastating errors, which erodes trust and misses learning opportunities.
- Treating culture change as a quick initiative rather than a long-term, organization-wide transformation.
- Attempting to lead without building trust and sharing information across levels.
