# Normal Accidents, High Reliability, Wicked Messes | Systems Thinking Ontario | 2021-08-09

A systems-thinking tour through normal accidents, high-reliability organizations, and wicked messes, arguing that complex tightly coupled systems fail in unavoidable ways, and that safety comes from recovery, frontline partnership, value alignment, and inclusive learning rather than prevention alone.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Use failure as a design lens: success is achieved by anticipating and obviating failure, not by assuming success.
- Distinguish three modes of change before acting: unfolding nature, fixing problems, and making history/disclosing new worlds.
- Do not intervene in a system you do not understand; weigh errors of commission against errors of omission, because doing nothing can be safer than acting.
- Practice wu wei / non-intrusive action: work with a system’s self-organizing tendencies instead of imposing top-down control.
- Accept normal accidents as an inherent property of interactively complex, tightly coupled systems; some failures cannot be eliminated, only managed.
- Invest in recovery, learning, and resilience, not only prevention and redundancy, because redundancy can increase complexity and create new failure paths.
- Build high-reliability practices: anticipate and contain trouble, question simplifications, defer to expertise regardless of rank, and treat near misses as learning events.
- Partner with frontline operators who live with the equipment; treat their tacit, local knowledge as essential rather than subordinate to engineering or management.
- Create psychological safety so anyone close to the work can speak up, challenge authority, and surface anomalies before they cascade.
- Align values across the system so that safety is not subordinated to speed, profit, or schedule pressure.
- Capture failure data with affordances like black boxes or operating-room recorders so that post-hoc learning is possible.
- Increase stakeholder variety and collaborative rationality when facing wicked messes; include broader voices in design, requirements, and analysis.
- Recognize when ambition exceeds current capacity; scale intervention to match the system’s ability to learn and adapt.

## Failure modes named
- Hierarchy silencing frontline knowledge and treating operators as unintelligent.
- Engineers and managers disconnected from the actual work as performed.
- Interactive complexity combined with tight coupling producing unavoidable normal accidents.
- Redundancy and safety devices adding complexity and creating design-induced accidents.
- Errors of commission prioritized over errors of omission, favoring harmful action over beneficial inaction.
- Misaligned values, such as profit or schedule pressure overriding safety.
- Top-of-hierarchy influence suppressing dissent and punishing whistleblowers.
- Wicked messes being abstracted away in boardrooms while frontline complexity is ignored.
- Problems whose complexity exceeds the organization’s grasp, causing paralysis or superficial gestures.
