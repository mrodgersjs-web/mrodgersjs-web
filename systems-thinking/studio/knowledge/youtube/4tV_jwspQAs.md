# Business is hard until you build systems like this

Four installable systems—McDonald’s-style operations, OKRs, a daily metrics dashboard with insight, and a founder 10-80-10 involvement model—let a business scale without the founder doing everything.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Install an operations system by documenting SOPs so every repeatable task is produced the same way every time, like a Big Mac in any McDonald’s.
- Treat every workflow as a versioned process with clear standards, not something invented ad hoc at the moment of execution.
- Assign process ownership to operationally-minded people rather than to the highest individual performer in that function.
- Enforce the defined process even when experienced hires want to “do it their way,” because consistency is the system.
- Install a growth system using OKRs so everyone knows the company North Star and their own measurable contribution to it.
- Cascade OKRs from the company objective down to team and individual key results so alignment exists without constant founder reminders.
- Build a daily manual KPI dashboard that a human calculates, reviews, and shares with insight, not just automated raw data dumps.
- Centralize metrics visibility so the whole team sees the same daily picture instead of allowing information to be concealed.
- Follow the founder 10-80-10 rule: define the first 10% of any major initiative, let the team own the 80% execution, then return for the final 10% review.
- Use the first 10% to set direction, messaging, ownership, objectives, and put work into task/SOP systems before stepping away.
- Resist micromanagement during the middle 80% so team innovation and ownership can emerge.
- Return for the final 10% to review output against original intent, give feedback, and approve going live.

## Failure modes named
- The founder becoming the bottleneck by refusing to delegate.
- Confusing top-performer skill with operational process-ownership skill.
- Experienced hires insisting on doing things “their way” instead of following the SOP.
- Teams saying things are going great while numbers show otherwise.
- Relying on automated reports that nobody actually looks at.
- Lack of centralized data, allowing people to conceal information.
- Stepping away and expecting the team to “just do stuff” without clear founder direction.
