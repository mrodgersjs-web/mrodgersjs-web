# Pre-Mortem

A pre-mortem is a structured risk-identification exercise in which a team imagines a project has already failed, then works backward to surface, prioritize, and address the causes before they happen.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Imagine the idea has been carried out, 12 months have passed, and it was a total disaster.
- Ask every participant to individually and silently write down all the reasons the project failed.
- Generate as many potential causes of failure as possible during silent individual work.
- Create a matrix of problems ranked by how serious and how likely each one is.
- Cluster or note problems that many people identified independently.
- Prioritize problems that are both serious and likely to occur.
- For serious/likely problems that are within your control, decide what to do now to prevent them.
- For serious/likely problems outside your control, create a contingency plan for what to do if they happen.
- Use the exercise to puncture excessive optimism and counter groupthink before committing to the idea.

## Failure modes named
- Optimism bias leading teams to ignore plausible failure modes.
- Groupthink, where people simply agree because they hope the idea will work.
- Failure to plan for serious, likely risks that are outside direct control.
- Missing preventable risks because teams never explicitly imagine failure.
