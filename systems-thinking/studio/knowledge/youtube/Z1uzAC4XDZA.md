# The Purest Form of Intelligence: Analogical Thinking

Analogical thinking is the core cognitive skill of recognizing structural parallels across domains, using known patterns as scaffolding to understand novel situations, and knowing when to release an analogy before it distorts understanding.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat analogy as structural parallel (A is to B as C is to D), not as a surface comparison of similar features.
- Distinguish surface similarity from structural similarity; expert thinking operates on the latter.
- Use a productive analogy as cognitive scaffolding: import the architecture of a known domain to make a new domain thinkable.
- Build a broad analogical library by reading widely across science, history, economics, philosophy, narrative, and other fields.
- Ask the structural question when facing something new: "How does this work, and where else have I seen something that works this way?"
- Map novel problems onto solved versions in other domains to import existing frameworks and approaches.
- Test every analogy for generativity: it must teach or derive insight, not merely decorate a point you already know.
- Push each analogy to its breaking point to discover where the structural parallel stops holding.
- Practice full use and clean release: exploit an analogy fully while it generates insight, then abandon it when evidence contradicts its structure.
- Hold analogies lightly to prevent a useful frame from hardening into ideology.
- Recognize that every concept and act of recognition is itself a form of analogical mapping.
- Reframe learning as the continuous expansion of a cross-domain structural pattern library.

## Failure modes named
- Decorative analogy: using an analogy only to illustrate or persuade rather than to generate genuine insight.
- Premature commitment: locking into an analogy and distorting new evidence to preserve the frame.
- Analogy hardening into ideology: refusing to release a once-generative analogy when it no longer fits reality.
- Surface-similarity trap: mistaking look-alike features for shared underlying logic.
- Pushing past the limit: extending an analogy into regimes where it actively misleads.
