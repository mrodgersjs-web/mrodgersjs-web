# The Science of Habits | Marco Badwal | TEDxFS

Marco Badwal explains that nearly half of daily behavior is automatic habit, shaped by cue-behavior-reward loops and neural myelination, and that awareness plus tiny consistent steps are the levers for reshaping identity.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define a habit as an automated behavior repeated in the same context and running below conscious awareness.
- Model the habit loop: a cue triggers a behavior, the behavior produces a reward, and dopamine release reinforces the loop so it repeats.
- Recognize that repetition + dopamine causes the brain to tag a behavior as important, regardless of whether it helps or harms you.
- Use the energy-efficiency principle: repeated behaviors thicken neural connections and wrap them in myelin, making them cheaper to execute.
- Conduct a one-week behavior audit: write down everything you do honestly to make subconscious habits visible.
- Apply the 1% better / small-steps rule: start tiny and stay consistent so the brain encodes the behavior.
- Expect the three-stage affect arc: initial resistance → tolerance → missing the behavior when skipped; reaching the final stage signals habit formation.
- Design environments and cues deliberately, e.g., put the phone on flight mode and use an app like Forest to make focus the default.
- Cultivate awareness first; use mindfulness to observe the urge-behavior-reward chain, because you can only change what you can see.
- Choose identity-shaping habits: prioritize behaviors that pull you toward the person you want to become and starve those that pull you away.

## Failure modes named
- The brain cannot distinguish good habits from bad habits; it only registers repetition and dopamine.
- Strongly overexpressed habits become addictions.
- Social-media and notification systems are engineered to hijack the dopamine-reward loop.
- Without awareness, people cannot see the subconscious cues driving behavior, leaving them stuck.
- Attempting large, abrupt changes instead of small, consistent steps leads to abandonment.
