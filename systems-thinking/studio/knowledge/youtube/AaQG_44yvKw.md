# Market Dynamics June 2021

Scott Juds argues that different investment results come from refusing to passively hold the broad market: exit dangerous markets with protective rules, then improve bull-market returns through disciplined sector rotation, while staying alert to behavioral volatility and macro liquidity risks.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Use protective bear-market rules ("Stormguard armor") to exit equities when market conditions are unsafe and preserve capital for redeployment later.
- Improve bull-market returns through sector rotation rather than holding a broad index, since sectors exhibit persistent relative trends.
- Run a Spider sector strategy that owns only the current trend leader and exits into a bear-market strategy when the storm-guard signal triggers.
- Treat trend following as a probabilistic friend, not a crystal ball; trends can bend or end abruptly.
- Accept higher short-term volatility in sector funds because short-term bumps are preferable to long-term crashes.
- Compare thematic ETFs against leveraged benchmarks because some appear normal but behave like 3x leveraged products.
- Develop trend-confirmation indicators to transfer momentum leadership among wild thematic ETFs and normal ETFs more reliably.
- Ignore or endure most punctuated market events because they are usually short, random, and reverse quickly.
- Avoid using very short trend-filter windows because rapid exits and rebound re-entries produce whipsaw losses.
- Expect sharp sector drops to recover rather than chase the last rotation; premature switching increases whipsaw risk.
- Build portfolios with low relative risk, high Sortino ratio, controlled tail risk, and consistent rolling returns.
- Monitor margin debt as an indicator of market tops, alongside stimulus flows.
- Prepare for the eventual removal of stimulus ("punch bowl") by holding trend leaders, diversified portfolio leaders, forward-walk progressive tuning, and integrated bear-market protection.

## Failure modes named
- Holding through major bear markets without protection devastates long-term portfolio value.
- Chasing last year's hot thematic or sector fund after a sudden reversal leads to buying near a top.
- Letting loss aversion dominate decisions causes panic selling of volatile leaders during normal drawdowns.
- Treating thematic ETFs as normal index funds ignores their leveraged-like volatility and risk profile.
- Reacting to short punctuated events by shifting strategy produces whipsaw losses.
- Selling a sector on a sharp pullback and buying the outperforming sector usually means buying high and selling low.
- Believing a high "beats benchmark" probability eliminates all underperformance; a 70% beat rate still means 30% of periods trail.
- Assuming continued stimulus will prevent a downturn; the removal of stimulus is a classic trigger for market slides.
