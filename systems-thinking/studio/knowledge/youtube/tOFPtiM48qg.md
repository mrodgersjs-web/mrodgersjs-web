# Train Your Brain to Outsmart Any Situation (critical-thinking audiobook)

Long-form survey of strategic-intelligence training: four pillars, bias catalog, mental models, first principles, decision-under-pressure, decomposition, systems thinking.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Four pillars of strategic intelligence: big-picture/systems thinking, second-order thinking, leverage thinking, metacognitive awareness — combined with cognitive flexibility and bias correction (3:34–10:36).
- Mental autopilot failure triad: pattern-matching old solutions onto new situations, survival circuits hijacking under pressure, cognitive conservatism (11:28–14:30).
- Bias-defense toolkit: ask for base rates ("what actually happens in cases like this?") (16:04), premortem — assume failure and work backward (16:28–16:49), argue the opposite position / intellectual empathy (16:49–17:41).
- Bias catalog with fixes: confirmation bias (22:01), availability heuristic (23:06), anchoring (24:40), planning fallacy — use reference-class statistics (25:20, 30:17–30:38), overconfidence — events judged 5% likely happen 20–30% of the time (26:49), self-serving attribution (27:33), sunk cost (27:55), Dunning-Kruger (28:14), bias blind spot — training alone doesn't remove it, process does (28:36–29:28).
- Mental-model core: reinforcing vs balancing feedback loops (34:02–34:51), leverage points — small inputs, large system effects (35:43–36:10), probabilistic thinking with expected value not binary outcomes (36:55–37:44), optionality — reserves and skills create options (38:11–38:56), incentives — observe what's actually rewarded, not announced (38:56–39:42), social proof as a design lever (40:03–40:29).
- **First-principles thinking**: strip the problem to fundamental truths and rebuild — the rocket example where raw-material cost exposed the assumption that rockets must be expendable (41:23–42:07, 45:07–47:25).
- Decision under pressure: pre-commit decision rules and constraints (59:12–59:58), OODA loop — observe/orient/decide/act to prevent rushing (60:30–60:53), 10/10/10 test (62:04–62:27), separate real from artificial deadlines (62:57–63:18), satisfice against criteria under time limits (63:40–64:02).
- Pattern recognition: watch leading indicators per domain (69:11–70:19), scenario thinking with key variables per scenario (70:45–71:27), transfer patterns cross-domain (epidemiology → virality; anti-fragility) (72:14–73:04).
- **Problem decomposition** — five cuts: functional, by time horizon (separate work streams for urgent vs long term) (78:33–79:19), constraint-based — separate hard vs self-imposed constraints (79:19–80:48), stakeholder-based (80:48–81:33), and root-cause via iterated "why" (81:33–82:20).
- Systems chapter: behavior is generated by structure, not events; emergent properties from interactions; delays between action and effect explain policy failure; archetypes incl. shifting-the-burden; Meadows' leverage ladder from parameters up to paradigms (110:02–117:33).
- Negotiation model: interests not positions, BATNA, objective criteria, listen to formulate shared options (122:11–124:34).
- Maintenance loop: belief audits (95:32), decision journal reviewed regularly (136:50–137:52), robust strategies over predictions, scenario planning with trigger signals (128:44–129:56, 134:13–135:20).

## Failure modes named
- Reasoning by analogy as the default; treating conventions as fundamental requirements (40:53–41:23, 48:12–48:36).
- Overconfidence + planning fallacy: no contingency margin on self-predictions (26:27–27:10).
- Escalation of commitment to failing approaches (94:30–94:49).
- Well-intentioned interventions that fight system structure instead of using leverage points (112:50, 116:17–116:45).
- Ignoring delays: judging interventions before the delayed effect lands (117:07–117:33).
