# Random Matrices in Unexpected Places: Atomic Nuclei, Chaotic Billiards, Riemann Zeta #SoME2

Random matrix theory provides a universal statistical lens through which the spectra of complex deterministic systems—from atomic nuclei and chaotic billiards to the zeros of the Riemann zeta function—exhibit the same eigenvalue-spacing patterns.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define a random matrix ensemble by specifying both a probability distribution for the entries and any structural constraints (symmetry, Hermiticity, etc.).
- Distinguish matrices with independent identically distributed (iid) entries from “physical” matrices whose structure reduces the number of free parameters.
- Use symmetric matrices to model covariance matrices, where symmetry follows from the fact that covariance is independent of argument order.
- Use Hermitian matrices to model quantum Hamiltonians, whose eigenvalues represent physically observable energy levels.
- Compute ensemble averages by integrating a matrix-dependent quantity over all independent entries weighted by the ensemble probability density.
- Prefer rotationally or unitarily invariant quantities (trace, determinant, traces of powers) because they depend only on the spectrum and remain unchanged under coordinate rotations.
- Regard the Gaussian random matrix ensemble as the simplest case because it is the only ensemble that is simultaneously iid and rotationally/unitarily invariant.
- Interpret the Wigner-Dyson spacing distribution as evidence of level repulsion: eigenvalues avoid lying on top of one another.
- Model intractable heavy atomic nuclei by replacing the exact Hamiltonian with a random matrix and predicting statistical spectral properties instead of exact energy levels.
- Analyze classically chaotic billiards by solving the Laplacian and finding that high-energy eigenvalue spacings follow the Wigner-Dyson distribution.
- Recognize quantum chaos operationally as the emergence of random-matrix statistics in a quantum system’s spectrum.
- Treat deterministic sparse Hamiltonians such as 1D spin chains as capable of showing RMT statistics in the appropriate energy window, even though no randomness was put in by hand.
- Compare the spacings of Riemann zeta-function zeros along the critical line to Wigner-Dyson spacings and connect them, via the Hilbert–Pólya conjecture, to a hypothetical quantum chaotic Hamiltonian.

## Failure modes named
- Treating an iid-entry random matrix as representative of all random matrix ensembles, ignoring the structural constraints that define most real-world matrices.
- Assuming random matrix statistics require explicit randomness built into the model, missing emergent randomness in deterministic complex systems.
- Expecting random matrix theory to predict exact eigenvalues or exact matrix entries rather than ensemble-level spectral statistics.
- Applying RMT universality across all energy scales or windows, without checking whether the deterministic system is in the regime where it matches RMT.
- Fixating on the determinant as the only rotationally or unitarily invariant spectral quantity, while neglecting traces and other spectral observables.
