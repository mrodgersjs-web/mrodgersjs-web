# Insights on How Team Topologies Drive Organizational Success • Manuel Pais • GOTO 2024

Manuel Pais argues that Team Topologies is a socio-technical, context-sensitive approach to achieving fast, safe, sustainable value flow and happier teams by decoupling streams, designing teams around them, and introducing platform and enabling behaviors incrementally rather than as a one-size-fits-all reorganization.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat Team Topologies as a socio-technical approach to increase flow and happiness, not as a standalone silver bullet.
- Combine Team Topologies with complementary methods such as Wardley Mapping for strategy and Domain-Driven Design for architecture and customer understanding.
- Define fast flow as multiple ongoing streams of customer value delivered rapidly, safely, and sustainably, with daily delivery as a useful vision.
- Decouple teams by first decoupling the streams, products, or value areas they own.
- Map inter-team dependencies, then redesign teams and interactions to reduce or eliminate blocking dependencies.
- Clarify each team interaction mode so collaboration has a concrete purpose, often to remove a dependency and restore autonomy.
- Build cross-functional teams by adding only the missing skills required for their specific stream, rather than every skill.
- Use Independent Service Heuristics to identify candidate services or products that could stand alone as independent offerings.
- Build internal platforms as products with product management, roadmaps, customer discovery, adoption feedback, and a clear vision.
- Convert blocking dependencies into unblocking, self-service paved roads that teams can pull from as needed.
- Start with the “thinnest viable X”: the smallest implementation of a platform or enabling pattern that delivers value without a full reorg.
- Prioritize behaviors over structures by introducing enabling, self-service, and platform behaviors before creating dedicated teams.
- Use enabling patterns within constraints to break knowledge silos and spread expertise across teams.
- Apply patterns selectively and iteratively in one business area or value stream, expanding step by step instead of rolling out big-bang transformations.

## Failure modes named
- Treating Team Topologies as the only solution to organizational problems.
- Misinterpreting the stream-aligned team as a team that must contain every skill and therefore becomes mediocre at everything.
- Letting enabling teams become expert-for-hire groups that do the work instead of teaching others.
- Allowing complicated-subsystem teams to hoard knowledge and act as isolated kingdoms.
- Letting platform teams become detached from internal customers and act as gatekeepers rather than service providers.
- Adopting the four team types and three interaction modes out of context or as rigid labels.
- Creating slow delivery and business-agility gaps through high coupling and unmanaged dependencies.
- Tolerating ivory towers where only one team or person controls critical knowledge or decisions.
- Waiting for a reorg, new framework, or dedicated teams before changing team behaviors.

## Studio relevance
02 frame - use this card to frame client organizational and flow challenges as socio-technical design problems that benefit from combining approaches, mapping dependencies, and incrementally adopting platform and enabling patterns.
