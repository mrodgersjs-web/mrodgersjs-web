# How to improve health and safety culture of an organization?Lecture 10

Improving health and safety culture requires visible management commitment, competent staff, effective multi-channel safety communication, and timely training at key organizational transitions.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Make safety a visible, core organizational value starting at the top, with management leading by example and following all safety rules.
- Integrate safety into all operational decisions, from decision-making to performance management and resource allocation.
- Allocate dedicated funding for safety training, equipment, and programs.
- Ensure staff have the training, knowledge, experience, and skills appropriate to their specific job hazards.
- Do not assign tasks to employees who lack the required qualifications or experience.
- Provide ongoing training and refresher courses as technology, job requirements, and hazards evolve.
- Use a mix of verbal, written, and graphic communication channels to convey safety information, matching method to message.
- Deploy notice boards, posters, toolbox talks, memos, and handbooks as complementary communication tools.
- Hold short toolbox talks at the start of shifts to discuss hazards, procedures, and recent incidents.
- Provide induction training for new employees covering all relevant safety hazards and procedures.
- Train employees before introducing new equipment, processes, or technologies.
- Train employees whenever safety regulations change.
- Conduct follow-up training after safety incidents to prevent recurrence.

## Failure modes named
- Passive consumption of safety training instead of active learning, note-taking, and questioning.
- Relying solely on verbal communication, which can be misheard or forgotten.
- Relying solely on written communication that may be unreadable or inaccessible to employees with low literacy.
- Assuming graphic communication works for every type of safety message or is always cost-effective.
- Assigning employees to tasks for which they are not qualified or experienced.
