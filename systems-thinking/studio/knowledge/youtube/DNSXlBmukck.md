# Common Mistakes With Vibe Coded Websites

A design review of AI-built startup landing pages that distinguishes between tasteful, brand-building uses of generative tools and the generic "AI slop" patterns that erode credibility and conversion.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Frame the landing page as a customer acquisition channel, not a self-contained showcase or cool web artifact.
- Define brand inputs (color palette, voice, target audience, key value proposition) before prompting an AI tool, so the output reflects intent rather than training defaults.
- Use AI to execute hard-to-build details that reinforce brand or product meaning, not to add effects simply because they are now easy to generate.
- Design hover states to invite clicks: keep the cursor-hand affordance, add subtle pop/glow/lighten, and never make interactive elements fade away or disappear on hover.
- Keep critical information and functionality visible by default; do not bury it behind hover, which has no equivalent on mobile and forces users to hunt.
- Evaluate every animation against the question "does this help a visitor understand or convert?" and remove it if the answer is no.
- QA AI-generated output with the same rigor as hand-written code, because one-shot shipping often hides subtle interaction bugs and broken states.
- Maintain sufficient text contrast and readable hierarchy; light-on-light body copy and unclear labels hurt comprehension.
- Avoid scroll-jacking and forced-scroll sections that feel sluggish and obscure the product's actual message.
- Tease below-the-fold content so visitors naturally scroll, rather than trapping them in a full-viewport hero with no visual continuation.
- Replace vague "10x" or "unlock" claims with concrete evidence of what the product does and for whom.
- Build from the company's differentiated idea outward; use AI to amplify originality, not to arrive at the same purple-gradient, bento-box, fake-dashboard template everyone else is using.

## Failure modes named
- AI design slop: generic, overused patterns that make sites look interchangeable.
- Ubiquitous purple gradients and pinkish-purple accent colors that signal "AI-generated."
- Dumb hover effects: nav items that fade out, buttons that slide awkwardly, or arrows that move the wrong way.
- Hover-only disclosure of essential functionality or information.
- Animations for animation's sake: wandering SVG lines, arbitrary fade-ins, and motion that distracts from the value proposition.
- Scroll-jacking that creates a slow, molasses-like browsing experience and hides content.
- Full-viewport heroes with no visual continuation, leaving users unsure there is more to see.
- Fake-dashboard visuals with rainbow Google-color callout cards and dark icons on light backgrounds.
- Generic bento-box feature grids (icon + headline + blurb) that feel templated.
- Low-contrast or overly light body text.
- Auto-playing video or dynamic backgrounds that overlap and render navigation unreadable.
- Mixed clickability: some elements in a list look interactive but only some respond, confusing users.
- Shipping AI output without human review, resulting in fit-and-finish bugs and broken interactions.
