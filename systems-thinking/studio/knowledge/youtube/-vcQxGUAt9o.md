# Systems Archetypes

The video introduces systems archetypes as recurring causal-loop patterns, demonstrating how short-term fixes in food systems, arms races, personal habits, and shared resources create unintended reinforcing feedback that undermines long-term wellbeing.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Read causal loop diagrams by tracing whether variables move in the same direction or opposite directions.
- Recognize systems archetypes as commonly recurring combinations of reinforcing and balancing feedback loops.
- Map the "fixes that fail" archetype: problem increases → easy fix increases → symptom temporarily decreases, while easy fix produces unintended consequence → original problem worsens.
- Apply fixes that fail to corporate food systems: cheap Walmart food temporarily relieves financial stress, but the same low-wage food system drives local wages down and increases future financial stress.
- Treat fixes that fail with parallel short-term relief and long-term structural change rather than choosing one or the other.
- Map the escalation archetype: each side's weapons stockpile decreases its relative superiority, raising perceived threat and triggering a counter-buildup by the other side.
- Recognize that escalation loops only stop when one party explicitly breaks the pattern and shifts from competition to cooperation or disarmament.
- Use Joanna Macy's three leverage levels—holding actions, structural change, and consciousness shift—to move from "business as usual" toward the "great turning."
- Map the "shifting the burden" archetype: symptom leads to quick fix, quick fix produces side effect, side effect prevents the fundamental solution and deepens dependency.
- Apply shifting the burden to caffeine dependency, debt cycles, and homework procrastination as cases where quick fixes displace root-cause behaviors.
- Map the tragedy of the commons as individual users maximizing private benefit from a shared resource until collective overuse depletes it.
- Use systems archetypes and the iceberg model to distinguish symptoms from root causes and identify high-leverage interventions.

## Failure modes named
- Fixes that fail: applying an easy fix that aggravates the original problem over time.
- Escalation / arms-race dynamics: competitive buildups that perpetuate insecurity rather than resolve it.
- Shifting the burden: relying on quick fixes that create dependency and crowd out fundamental solutions.
- Tragedy of the commons: rational individual use leading to collective depletion of shared resources.
- Business as usual: doing more of the same failed strategy faster and harder instead of changing structure.
- Mistaking symptom relief for solution: treating the balancing loop as sufficient while ignoring the reinforcing loop it feeds.
- The Walmart effect: a solution that is simultaneously a cause of the problem it appears to solve.
