# Do you know the two-pizza rule?

Jeff Bezos's two-pizza rule recommends keeping meetings small enough that two pizzas could feed the entire group, because smaller groups sustain focus, participation, and productivity.

## Procedure / models distilled
- The two-pizza rule: cap meeting attendance at the number of people two pizzas could feed.
- Meeting-size/productivity curve: the more people packed into a meeting, the less productive it is likely to become.
- Use the literal two-pizza heuristic as a physical constraint to enforce attendance limits and improve morale.
- Keep meetings in tight-knit groups so attendees cannot blend into the crowd.
- Reduce ambient disengagement by shrinking group size so people stay focused rather than playing on their phones.
- Before sending an invitation, ask: "What will this individual gain from this meeting?"
- Before sending an invitation, ask: "What will this individual contribute to the meeting?"
- Before sending an invitation, ask: "Is there someone else already attending who could provide the same input?"
- Use the three invitation questions as a gate to ensure every attendee has a unique and relevant role.
- Reclassify meetings from the default #1 office productivity killer into a productivity booster by cutting unnecessary attendees.

## Failure modes named
- Over-packing meetings reduces productivity rather than increasing it.
- Large meetings let attendees hide in the crowd and disengage with their phones.
- Inviting people who neither gain from nor contribute to the meeting wastes their time.
- Inviting redundant contributors who could be represented by a single attendee.
- Allowing meetings to become a low-value default activity rather than a focused, role-critical tool.
