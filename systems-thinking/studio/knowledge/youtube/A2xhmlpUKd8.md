# Douglas North - Effect of Institutions on Market Performance at FCC

Douglas North argues that market performance is shaped by institutions—formal rules, informal norms, and beliefs—that reduce uncertainty and transaction costs, and that effective regulation must be adaptively efficient: stable enough to guide behavior yet flexible enough to evolve with technology and shifting payoffs.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Start from the premise that neoclassical economics is static and frictionless; use it as a tool but recognize it assumes away information costs, institutions, and government.
- Introduce transaction costs as the central friction: once they exist, efficient markets are not guaranteed and the structure of exchange becomes endogenous.
- Model institutions as humanly devised constraints—formal rules plus informal norms and beliefs—that structure interaction under uncertainty.
- Treat the inherited institutional and cultural framework as a constraint set: past beliefs, norms, and organizations limit today's feasible reforms.
- Analyze how organizations and vested interests form around existing institutions and resist changes that threaten their rents.
- Identify adaptive efficiency: design institutions that permit trial and error, support diverse experiments, and eliminate failures such as through bankruptcy.
- Balance flexibility against stability in rulemaking: rules must adapt to technological and market change but cannot be so loose that "anything goes."
- Use informal norms as a reform lever: persistent norms can gradually force formal rules to change when the two are inconsistent.
- Monitor changing relative prices and payoffs at the margin: as they shift, previously acceptable behaviors can become socially unproductive or predatory.
- Apply price theory to short-run price, quality, and supply/demand questions, while keeping a "weather eye" on dynamics and time.
- Operate in a non-ergodic world: recognize novelty, avoid universal historical laws, and update models rather than assume fixed fundamentals.
- Reduce information and communication costs through institutional design, because falling transaction costs reshape what market structures are possible.

## Failure modes named
- Relying on frictionless or static neoclassical models as if institutions and information costs do not matter.
- Assuming that recognizing transaction costs alone guarantees efficient market outcomes.
- Imposing reforms without accounting for path-dependent beliefs, norms, and vested interests.
- Writing rigid regulations that quickly become obsolete in rapidly changing markets.
- Allowing extreme flexibility so that rules provide no stable structure.
- Drawing universal "laws of history" from historical cases and applying them to novel conditions.
- Failing to update market structure as relative payoffs shift, enabling Enron/WorldCom-style opportunism.
- Believing that economic growth is automatic or built into the system.
