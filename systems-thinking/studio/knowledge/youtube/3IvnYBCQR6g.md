# Lessons learned from introducing Team Topologies - Michael Plöd

A consultant's retrospective on introducing Team Topologies, emphasizing honest team-type assessment, boundary design beyond bounded contexts, visible flow, and the HR/management blockers that determine whether the change survives.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat the four team types as behavioral magnets that teams should tend toward, not as rigid classifications that every team must already match.
- Assess teams honestly against the behavioral expectations of their declared team type to avoid loose or performative labeling.
- Use the "team type undefined" label for teams that do not fit the four types to create urgency and a path for improvement.
- Define team boundaries using fracture planes beyond bounded contexts, including regulatory compliance, team location, and quality attributes such as scalability, UX, robustness, and consistency.
- Start boundary design with collaborative domain modeling, then deliberately broaden the perspective to include non-domain fracture planes.
- Make fast flow visible through flow engineering and value stream mapping before and during reorganization.
- Create a dedicated management enabling team to coach middle managers, address their fears, and help them see themselves as gardeners creating soil for teams.
- Engage HR early to review salary bands, career ladders, and job descriptions that might otherwise block enabling behaviors.
- Shift budgeting from annual project funding to value-stream, outcome-based, or team-based funding matched to team type and value stream.
- Run enabling teams as internal consulting firms with explicit service offers and internal pricing that tracks demand.
- Run platform teams as internal SaaS providers with tiered support levels, pricing strategies, and rebates for early adopters.
- Frame Team Topologies as a socio-technical change spanning IT, HR, finance, and management rather than an IT-only reorganization.
- Use the "software architect elevator" metaphor to communicate the change upward to executives and downward to teams in their respective languages.

## Failure modes named
- Reducing Team Topologies to the four team types, three interaction modes, and colorful diagrams while ignoring the underlying behaviors.
- Labeling teams as stream-aligned without checking whether they actually exhibit stream-aligned behaviors.
- Matching teams one-to-one with bounded contexts in a "DDD frenzy" while ignoring other fracture planes.
- Treating Team Topologies as purely an IT transformation and excluding HR, finance, and management from the conversation.
- Ignoring middle-management fears, which gives them both motive and power to block the transformation.
- Salary bands that reward command-and-control roles rather than enabling roles, directly blocking the desired culture shift.
- Annual budgeting cycles that freeze plans and prevent the responsiveness Team Topologies aims to create.
- Applying incremental funding without accounting for the overhead it adds to an already heavy budgeting process.
- Mislabeling hybrid teams as one type instead of acknowledging them as team type undefined.
