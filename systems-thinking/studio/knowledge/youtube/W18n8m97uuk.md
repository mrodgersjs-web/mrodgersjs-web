# Eli Goldratt on What is the Theory of Constraints?

Goldratt compresses all of TOC into a single word and shows the derivation discipline behind it.

## Procedure / models distilled
- The Hillel test: teach me the whole subject "while I stand on one leg" — the answer must be one sentence from which everything else derives, then "go and study" (1:59–3:03).
- Goldratt applied the test to TOC when asked to write the introduction to the TOC handbook: condense the entire body of knowledge — past and future — into one sentence, then one word, and prove every existing piece derives from it one-to-one (3:03–4:26).
- The word that all of TOC derives from: **focus** (5:04–5:07).
- Corollary he draws: TOC's evolution was not a fluke — if the derivation holds, there is only one way the body of knowledge could have evolved (4:00–4:17).

## Failure modes named
- Bodies of knowledge that accrete without a derivable core — you cannot tell real extensions from drift (3:24–3:37).
- Defining a field by its accumulated tools instead of its generating principle (0:14–0:21: "it grew and mushroomed and now what the hell is it").

## Studio relevance
- frame — the one-leg compression test for any methodology: name the generating word before mapping the territory.
