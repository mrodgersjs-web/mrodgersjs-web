# Stoic Decisions that will make YOU MORE INTELLIGENT

Ten Stoic practices for forging internal value by reframing perception, accepting discomfort, acting with discipline, mastering emotions, seeking wisdom, practicing gratitude, embracing impermanence, upholding principles, cooperating with others, and pursuing excellence.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Reframe perception: interpret setbacks (e.g., job loss) as opportunities to pivot or focus on passion projects rather than as disasters.
- Practice voluntary discomfort: deliberately introduce small hardships (fasting, cold baths, sleeping on the floor) to reduce dependence on comfort and build resilience.
- Set clear actionable goals and break them into daily tasks; prioritize consistent action over perfection.
- Cultivate inner tranquility through daily quiet reflection and detachment from outcomes, focusing only on your own response.
- Adopt a perpetual-student mindset: question assumptions, seek deeper truths, and treat “knowing nothing” as the beginning of wisdom.
- Practice daily gratitude by acknowledging what you have, treating each day as a separate life, and weakening envy or resentment.
- Reflect on impermanence and mortality to prioritize meaningful pursuits and let go of trivial ones.
- Define and uphold core principles; evaluate every difficult decision by whether it aligns with those values.
- Act with a spirit of cooperation and empathy; contribute to community well-being and recognize that harming the collective harms the self.
- Pursue excellence as a continuous journey in work, relationships, and personal growth, aiming to be better than yesterday.

## Failure modes named
- Viewing the world as a series of events that happen to you rather than as a canvas for your response.
- Wasting life chasing comfort and avoiding the discomforts that could build strength.
- Knowing what is right but failing to act, or waiting for perfect conditions instead of maintaining consistency.
- Being swept away by emotion instead of standing firm through inner tranquility.
- Believing you know enough and ceasing to question or learn.
- Focusing on what you lack rather than what you have, feeding envy and resentment.
- Treating wealth, health, and relationships as permanent, leading to trivial pursuits.
- Living without clear principles, leaving decisions subject to shifting circumstances.
- Acting only for individual gain while ignoring the interconnected well-being of the community.
- Settling for mediocrity and failing to push for continual improvement.
