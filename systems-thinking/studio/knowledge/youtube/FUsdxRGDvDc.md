# The Importance of Having a North Star Metric for Your Gym feat. Dan Uyemura

Dan Uyemura argues that gym owners should make member check-ins their North Star metric, because reducing churn by keeping members active is the single biggest lever that turns customer value into sustainable revenue and creates a retention-focused culture.

## Procedure / models distilled
- **Pick one change, not ten.** At an event you will hear dozens of ideas; choose the single change that resonates most, execute it fully, and defer the rest to the next quarter so you avoid paralysis.
- **Apply the ikigai / four-circle test to gym ownership.** Sustainable fulfillment requires four overlapping factors: what you love, what you are good at, what the world needs, and what pays; ignoring the “paid” circle makes the business fragile.
- **Define churn as cancellations divided by starting members in a month.** For example, 5 cancellations out of 100 members equals a 5% monthly churn rate.
- **Use the churn “cap” model.** A gym with 20 leads/month and 50% close rate loses members each month equal to churn × membership; at ~9% churn the gym caps at roughly 112 members, while cutting churn to ~4% allows growth past 250 members.
- **Make check-ins the North Star metric.** Check-ins are a leading indicator of value received and retention; if members stop showing up, they will soon quit.
- **Track zero-check-in members as an early-warning list.** Roughly one-third of PushPress gym members show zero monthly check-ins, and these are the members most likely to churn.
- **Run a weekly accountability conversation.** At sign-up, ask how many times per week the member plans to attend and what their goals are, then review weekly: celebrate wins, diagnose misses, and reset “tomorrow.”
- **Sell commitment-oriented memberships.** Offer discounted rates for 6- or 12-month commitments; if someone quits early, refund only the discount portion, so the commitment conversation sets the mindset.
- **Use recurring community events as a retention anchor.** Hold a fun event every two months, announce the next event at the current one, invite friends, and tie event access to active membership.
- **Align every business function around the North Star.** Once check-ins are the metric, decisions about marketing, sales, onboarding, intro sessions, personal training, and events all become easier because they point at the same outcome.
- **Build a value culture by repeating the metric.** When coaches, staff, and members all understand that the gym’s focus is helping the member show up, you create a culture centered on customer value.
- **Run the “check-ins → value → money” flywheel.** More check-ins create more customer value, which produces more revenue, which funds bigger, higher-value decisions for clients, which keeps the flywheel spinning.
- **Don’t try to optimize alone.** The gyms highlighted as low-churn examples all used mentoring groups and/or ancillary services; doing this work solo is unnecessarily hard.

## Failure modes named
- **Change overload:** trying to implement ten improvements at once paralyzes the team and causes nothing to get done.
- **Pride in unpaid purpose:** saying “I get paid in PRs” or “this is just my hobby” ignores the “paid” circle of ikigai and leaves the gym one accident or life event away from closing.
- **Churn denial:** believing you don’t lose many members because you only notice the ones you like; invisible drop-ins who leave quietly still count.
- **Skipping check-ins:** failing to track attendance removes your earliest, most actionable churn signal.
- **Weak commitment conversations:** selling only month-to-month memberships misses the chance to align member mindset around showing up.
- **Underpricing profitability:** treating money as secondary threatens the stability of the community the owner wants to serve.
