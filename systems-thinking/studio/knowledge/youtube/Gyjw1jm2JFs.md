# How to Practice Negotiating & Making Deals | Chris Voss & Dr. Andrew Huberman

Chris Voss explains how to stay ready for unexpected high-stakes negotiations by deliberately practicing low-stakes social skills—labels and calibrated questions—throughout ordinary daily interactions.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat readiness as a daily practice, not a one-time prep: "small Stakes practice for high stakes results."
- Use casual encounters (Lyft/Uber drivers, TSA agents, hotel clerks, grocery clerks, baristas) as low-risk rehearsal spaces for negotiation muscles.
- Throw out verbal observations ("labels") such as "seems like a tough day" or "seems like you're in a good mood" to keep the skill loose and automatic.
- Ask "What do you love about...?" rather than generic rapport questions to trigger an immediate positive state change in the other person.
- Use that same question as a fast values-reveal: the answer shows what someone actually prioritizes, not what their job title implies.
- Manage your own energy before high-stakes moments; avoid "tapping the gas tank out" so fatigue doesn't degrade performance.
- Enter interactions with the intent to leave people better than you found them, accumulating "positive karma" rather than negative residue.
- Recognize that negotiation skill is a perishable capacity—regular micro-use keeps the mental muscles limber for when the bell rings unexpectedly.

## Failure modes named
- Believing negotiation only happens in formal, scheduled "big day" events rather than in sudden phone calls, texts, or casual conversations.
- Asking "how's your day going?" of an upset person, which can trigger an unwanted negative download.
- Saving all practice for high-stakes moments, leaving skills stiff and slow under pressure.
- Arriving depleted or fatigued to an important interaction because energy was not conserved.
- Treating service workers poorly and leaving negative karma that can later rebound.
