# Deep learning nails correlation. Causation is another matter. | Gary Marcus

Gary Marcus argues that deep learning is fundamentally a sophisticated correlation-detection engine and cannot represent or reason about causal mechanisms, so it can flag associations but cannot determine whether one thing causes another or explain why.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat correlation as statistical co-occurrence, not evidence of causal influence.
- Distinguish the empirical observation that two variables move together from the claim that changing one would change the other.
- Look for a common cause (such as age or maturation) that could produce a spurious correlation between two effects.
- Ask whether an intervention on X would change Y before acting on a correlation.
- Frame deep learning as a sophisticated correlation detector rather than a causal reasoner.
- When the goal is explanation or intervention, prefer representations that encode causal relationships.
- Match the analytical tool to the question: correlation-based methods for prediction and selection, causal methods for mechanisms.
- Probe whether the system needs to know why two variables are related, not merely that they are related.

## Failure modes named
- Confusing correlation with causation
- Using deep learning to answer causal or mechanistic questions it cannot represent
- Treating predictive association as sufficient for understanding how to intervene
- Mistaking "what is correlated" for "what causes what"
