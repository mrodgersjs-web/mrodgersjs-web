# What is a PRE-MORTEM? Here’s How To Use It To Reduce Business Risk!

A pre-mortem imagines a project has already failed and works backward to identify the likely causes, helping teams anticipate risks and avoid painful failures before committing.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define the specific project, product, or initiative you are considering before any launch or major commitment.
- Set the mental scene by imagining the project has already launched and completely failed or bombed.
- Ask, “If this failed, what are the most likely causes?” and work backward from that imagined failure.
- Run the exercise with a team to capture diverse perspectives on how things could go wrong.
- Frame the session explicitly as a pre-mortem so participants know the goal is critical analysis, not encouragement.
- Use the “mystery” framing: invite participants to solve for the root cause of the eventual failure.
- Capture the failure causes as a list of risks, assumptions, and weak points to address.
- Compare the identified risks against the cost of committing too early or pursuing the wrong opportunity.
- Use the outputs to challenge confirmation bias and pressure-test the decision to proceed.
- Repeat or revisit the pre-mortem whenever new information emerges or before key milestones.

## Failure modes named
- Relying only on post-mortems, which means learning from failure after the damage is done.
- Asking for feedback in ordinary social contexts where people default to politeness and encouragement rather than honesty.
- Confirmation bias that leads teams to seek evidence a plan will work while ignoring signs it could fail.
- Premature commitment that locks in opportunity costs before risks have been surfaced.
- Failure to frame the exercise correctly, which can suppress the brutal honesty needed to identify real threats.
