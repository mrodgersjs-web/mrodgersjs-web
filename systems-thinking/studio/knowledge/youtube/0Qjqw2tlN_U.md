# Epistemology:  How Do We Know What There Is?  What Is Knowledge?

Bob Doyle argues that information philosophy provides a new grounding for epistemology: knowledge arises when there is an isomorphism between immaterial abstract ideas and concrete material objects, while living systems are dynamic information structures shaped by information-processing algorithms.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Treat information as an immaterial, non-conserved quantity distinct from conserved matter and energy; change in the universe is explained by rearrangements of matter/energy into information structures.
- Model cosmic evolution as the formation of passive information structures (galaxies, stars, planets) through physical forces, and living things as active, growing information structures controlled by algorithms.
- View mind/soul/consciousness as abstract information embodied in the brain, so death destroys information while matter remains.
- Frame epistemology as the ancient "problem of knowledge": how, and whether, we can know the "things themselves" rather than mere appearances.
- Use the empiricist distinction between primary qualities (objective facts of objects) and secondary qualities (subjective perceptual effects) to diagnose the gap between perception and reality.
- Apply the skeptical test: perception is fallible (dreams, hallucinations, errors), so sensory experience alone cannot guarantee knowledge of external objects.
- Recognize the positivist constraint and its cost: limit claims to observable "positive information," but notice that this risks discarding the mind as unobservable metaphysics.
- Treat behaviorism's brain/observable-behavior focus as insufficient, because it leaves out introspection, subjective experience, and the other-minds problem.
- Establish genuine knowledge through isomorphism: a one-to-one mapping or morphism between an abstract idea and a concrete object in the material world.
- Adopt a dualist ontology that separates abstract information from concrete material entities in order to account for both mental content and physical processes.
- Map value, creation, and order onto negative entropy/free energy (Ergo/ergodic processes) and destruction/disorder onto entropy (tropic processes).

## Failure modes named
- Skepticism: because perception can err, one may conclude that we cannot know anything about the things themselves.
- Positivism's bathwater error: restricting knowledge to observable positive information and throwing out the mind as metaphysical nonsense.
- Behaviorist reduction of the mind to brain/behavior, ignoring introspection and subjective experience.
- The other-minds / zombie confusion: inferring consciousness from behavior or brain activity without proof that inner experience exists.
- Treating secondary qualities (e.g., redness) as purely subjective, thereby losing touch with objective primary qualities.
- Reifying information as matter or energy rather than seeing it as a distinct organizing arrangement.
- Anthropomorphizing ergodic/negative-entropy processes into divine agency without independent justification.
- Hubristic grand narratives that conflate thermodynamic order with moral good and entropy with moral evil.
