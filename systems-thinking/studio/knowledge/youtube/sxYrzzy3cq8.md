# How statistics can be misleading - Mark Liddell

Aggregated statistics can reverse their apparent meaning when hidden conditional variables—such as patient health, age, or victim race—are revealed by subgroup analysis.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Compare aggregate outcomes across groups before drilling into conditional subgroups.
- Disaggregate data by plausible confounding factors to test whether overall trends hold.
- Identify lurking variables that significantly influence results but are hidden in aggregate summaries.
- Recognize Simpson's paradox when subgroup trends contradict the aggregate trend.
- Check baseline composition differences between comparison groups.
- Evaluate whether the overall number or the subgrouped data gives the more accurate picture.
- Ask how the data was grouped and whether the categories are meaningful or arbitrary.
- Study the real-world situation the statistics describe before accepting conclusions.
- Treat high-level percentages as hypotheses to be tested against conditional breakdowns.
- Be suspicious when a single aggregate metric is used to drive an important decision.

## Failure modes named
- Simpson's paradox: the same dataset appearing to show opposite trends depending on grouping.
- Lurking / conditional variables hidden inside aggregated data.
- Drawing decisions from overall rates while ignoring subgroup composition.
- Misleading or arbitrary categories that distort interpretation.
- Using data selectively to manipulate decisions or promote an agenda.
