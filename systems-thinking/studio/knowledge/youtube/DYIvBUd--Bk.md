# Performance Testing | Validity & Reliability | CSCS Chapter 12

This video explains why performance testing is used in strength and conditioning, defines core measurement terms, and walks through the major forms of validity and reliability plus their common threats.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Use performance testing to assess athletic talent, identify strengths and weaknesses, set individual goals aligned with team objectives, and determine whether an athlete has the baseline physical abilities needed for competition.
- Choose between laboratory tests (e.g., VO2 max with a metabolic cart) and field tests (e.g., 1.5-mile run) based on available equipment, expertise, and the prediction equations that link field measures to lab gold standards.
- Administer pre-tests to establish baselines and guide initial programming; use mid-tests for formative evaluation and mid-course corrections; use post-tests for summative evaluation of program success.
- Define the construct you intend to measure before selecting a test so the test actually captures the intended ability.
- Apply construct validity by ensuring the test represents the underlying theoretical ability, such as force production for strength.
- Apply face validity by choosing tests that look like they measure the target ability to athletes and coaches, recognizing that some valid tests may initially seem unrelated until performed.
- Apply content validity by checking with experts that the test covers all relevant sub-components of the ability in appropriate proportions.
- Apply criterion-referenced validity by correlating test scores with a gold-standard measure of the same ability.
- Use concurrent validity to compare a new test with an already accepted test measuring the same thing, selecting variants like countermovement jumps versus squat jumps based on sport specificity.
- Use predictive validity cautiously: test scores should correspond with future performance, but real-world outcomes are mixed and do not guarantee success.
- Use discriminant validity to distinguish between related but different constructs, such as peak force versus time-constrained force utilization.
- Treat reliability as a prerequisite for validity: a test cannot be valid if its results are not repeatable.
- Control intra-subject variability by standardizing conditions around sleep, nutrition, time of day, training status, and recovery.
- Control inter-rater and intra-rater variability by training testers, using consistent protocols, and calibrating equipment regularly.

## Failure modes named
- Using maximal push-ups to failure as a "strength" test when it primarily measures local muscular endurance.
- Collecting large amounts of data without qualified staff who can interpret it and turn it into actionable training decisions.
- Labeling a rote change-of-direction drill as an "agility" test when it lacks a reactive cognitive component.
- Assuming high NFL Combine scores reliably predict future professional success despite mixed evidence.
- Treating force plates or other "gold standard" equipment as automatically reliable when they can drift, need recalibration, or fail to record trials.
- Ignoring rater error such as inconsistent stopwatch timing or imprecise skinfold landmarking.
