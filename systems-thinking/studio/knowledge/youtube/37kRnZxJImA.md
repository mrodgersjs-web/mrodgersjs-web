# Statistical Mechanics (Overview)

Statistical mechanics bridges quantum mechanical molecular energies and bulk thermodynamic properties by using probability distributions to move from microstates to macrostates.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Start with the complete set of possible energy states for a system; knowing all energies is prerequisite for calculating all probabilities.
- Apply the Boltzmann probability distribution to relate individual state energy to relative probability: probability scales as e^(−E/kT).
- Normalize probabilities using the partition function Q so that absolute probabilities sum to one.
- Compute intensive entropy from probabilities via S = −k Σ p_i ln(p_i).
- Compute intensive average energy from probabilities and energies via ⟨E⟩ = Σ p_i E_i.
- Treat entropy and average energy as macrostate descriptors that summarize collective rather than individual particle behavior.
- Feed macrostate variables into thermodynamic relationships to derive enthalpy, free energy, pressure, and other bulk properties.
- Recognize quantum mechanics as the upstream source for predicting molecular energies that statistical mechanics consumes.
- View the overall workflow as a pipeline: quantum mechanics → energies → probabilities → statistical mechanics → macrostate variables → thermodynamic relationships → predicted bulk properties.

## Failure modes named
- Attempting to calculate actual probabilities from a single energy rather than the full set of energies and the partition function.
- Omitting the partition function Q and reporting only relative rather than normalized probabilities.
- Confusing the role of statistical mechanics with thermodynamics or quantum mechanics; each occupies a distinct step in the prediction pipeline.
