# C12 Correlation vs  Causation   Deep Dive Understanding the Difference, Spurious Correlations,

A conceptual overview distinguishing correlation as co-movement between variables from causation as a cause-effect relationship, emphasizing hidden third variables and rigorous methods for establishing causal claims.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define correlation as the degree to which two variables move in relation to each other, with positive correlation meaning same direction and negative correlation meaning opposite direction.
- Define causation as a relationship where one event is the result of another event, representing a stronger claim than correlation.
- Use the ice cream sales and drowning incidents example to illustrate how a surface correlation can mask a spurious relationship.
- Identify hidden third variables, such as summer weather, that independently cause both correlated outcomes and produce spurious correlations.
- Apply statistical tools and critical thinking to detect correlations arising from coincidence or invisible third factors.
- Use randomized controlled trials as a traditional method for establishing causality.
- Use longitudinal studies to observe how variables change over time and strengthen causal inference.
- Apply statistical controls to isolate relationships and reduce confounding.
- Treat observational studies as suggestive of causal relationships while recognizing they require further investigation beyond correlation.
- Build causal claims through comprehensive approaches that incorporate multiple data sources, test different hypotheses, and rigorously eliminate biases.
- Accumulate evidence across multiple studies rather than relying on a single study to determine causality.

## Failure modes named
- Assuming correlation implies causation.
- Drawing false conclusions from correlated variables without examining underlying mechanisms.
- Missing hidden third variables or confounders that explain observed correlations.
- Treating coincidental patterns as meaningful causal evidence.
- Accepting observational study results as definitive proof of causation without replication or further investigation.
- Ignoring biases that can distort apparent relationships between variables.
