# The Biggest Ideas in the Universe | 21. Emergence

Emergence is the idea that the same reality can be accurately described by multiple theories at different scales, each with its own autonomous language and domain of applicability, with higher-level theories such as thermodynamics, dualities, and classical gravity arising as compatible, sometimes derivable, descriptions of a more comprehensive underlying theory.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Map multiple theories onto the same reality and require that they agree wherever their domains of applicability overlap.
- Model an emergent theory as a higher-level language whose macro variables are coarse-grained over many microscopic degrees of freedom.
- Use a projection map from the micro state space to the macro state space; when this map commutes with time evolution, the system exhibits weak emergence.
- Treat thermodynamics and fluid mechanics as the emergent language of kinetic theory: atomic positions and momenta coarse-grain into density, pressure, temperature, and velocity field.
- Apply the domain-of-applicability check: a macro theory is not false outside its regime, merely inapplicable there.
- Regard dualities as two very different classical descriptions that both emerge from the same underlying quantum theory.
- Use the AdS/CFT correspondence as a model: a five-dimensional gravitational theory in anti-de Sitter space emerges from a four-dimensional non-gravitational conformal field theory on the boundary, illustrating the holographic principle.
- Treat classical physics, including gravity, as an emergent, approximate description of an underlying quantum mechanical reality.
- Distinguish weak emergence, where the macro theory is autonomous but reducible in principle from the micro theory, from strong emergence, where macro behavior is claimed not to be derivable from micro laws.
- Evaluate strong-emergence claims by asking what new equations or violations of the micro Lagrangian they require; in a local quantum field theory this typically means abandoning locality.
- Avoid the assumption that "fundamental" means "more important"; choose the level of description by practical utility and domain, not by prestige.
- Check that a proposed emergent theory has a domain that overlaps with, or is a subset of, the micro theory's domain; if it is claimed to reach outside, that is strong emergence and needs new physics.

## Failure modes named
- Calling a theory "more fundamental" and therefore more important, when its importance depends on the question being asked.
- Applying a macro theory outside its domain of applicability and concluding that the theory is wrong.
- Confusing different theories about reality with different realities themselves.
- Asserting strong emergence without specifying how the microscopic Lagrangian fails or what new laws replace it.
- Assuming that because a higher-level description is autonomous, the lower-level description is irrelevant or nonexistent.
- Treating dualities as two separate realities rather than one quantum theory expressed in two classical languages.
