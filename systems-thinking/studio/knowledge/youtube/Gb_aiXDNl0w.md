# Simulation #323 Raman Frey - Incentive System Design

Raman Frey argues that civilization is hitting an upstream incentive-design crisis: capitalism has become a global monoculture optimizing "more is better," and long-term flourishing requires diversifying incentive systems to reward altruism, temper consumption, and raise the floor of human security.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Distinguish "incentive systems" from narrow economic inducements; include social, neurobiological, habitual, and cultural levers.
- Think upstream: redesign the generator conditions that produce downstream harms rather than only remediating symptoms.
- Treat capitalism as a monoculture of commerce that has assimilated or erased diverse historical incentive systems.
- Apply Bucky Fuller's principle: build new systems that render the unsatisfactory old system obsolete instead of reforming it from within.
- Raise the floor of subsistence and psychological security so latent talent and cognitive capacity can manifest.
- Prioritize early-childhood emotional safety as foundational infrastructure for pro-social adults.
- Study historical and anthropological alternatives to market logic, such as Graeber's "Debt" and egalitarian hunter-gatherer status-leveling.
- Run small, deployable experiments rather than waiting for top-down universal reform.
- Calibrate rewards so altruistic behavior is reinforced and psychopathic/hoarding behavior is not a competitive advantage.
- Build "temperance economics" that discourages hyper-consumption and structurally prevents abject poverty.
- Redirect wealth flows toward the middle rather than relying on post-facto redistribution.
- Use influencer marketing and cultural platforms to propagate pro-social norms and healthy behaviors.
- Design incentives across domains—commerce, education, parenting, governance—so good actions become the path of least resistance.

## Failure modes named
- Downstream remediation bias: treating symptoms instead of redesigning generator functions.
- Monoculture of commerce: capitalism assimilating all alternatives into a single "more is better" logic.
- Infinite GDP growth pursued on a finite planet.
- Hyper-consumption, wealth polarization, and tragedy of the commons as civilizational suicide vectors.
- Rewarding psychopathy and empathy-detachment at the apex of economic competition.
- Perverse childhood imitations that normalize bravado, dominance, and zero-sum status games.
- Post-facto redistribution as a band-aid for systems that inherently drive wealth upward.
