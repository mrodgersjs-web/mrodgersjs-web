# I Replaced My Social Media Designer With ONE AI Prompt

Luke Carter demonstrates a human-in-the-loop workflow where an agentic content system publishes SEO/GEO articles, a Claude API "viral carousel" skill turns each article into slide copy, and GPT Image 2 with reference-style images generates on-brand social carousels to drive traffic back to the site.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Build a "digital home" CMS where an agent chooses content ideas, writes evergreen articles, and publishes them to your website daily.
- Create a dedicated "socials" section in the CMS that pulls in all published articles as source feedstock for carousels.
- Select an article you want to repurpose and trigger a Claude API skill that reads the full article and outputs carousel slide copy.
- Keep the workflow semi-autonomous: automate the writing layer, but require a human approval/editing step before image generation.
- Review each generated slide, edit copy as needed, and copy the slide content into the GPT Image 2 prompt.
- Curate 2–3 reference carousel images that match your desired brand style and the copy density of your slides.
- Include at least a title-slide reference, a text-heavy-slide reference, and an image-style reference so GPT can replicate layout, typography, and illustration style.
- Use a master GPT Image 2 prompt that instructs the model to match the reference style exactly and generate new photos/illustrations that fit the slide copy while staying visually cohesive.
- Generate the carousel, then iterate with natural-language edits, spacing tweaks, logo swaps, and style variations until it is on-brand.
- Publish the finished carousel to social media with a call to action that drives traffic back to the original article.
- Layer a weekly trends loop that analyzes content performance and lets agents decide what to publish next, keeping the carousel feed aligned with live strategy.

## Failure modes named
- Automating the entire end-to-end process "for the sake of it" and removing the human-in-the-loop review over copy and design.
- Creating viral carousels before building the SEO/GEO-optimized evergreen article foundation they depend on.
- Choosing reference images that do not match your actual brand look and feel, producing generic or off-brand output.
- Picking a carousel format that cannot accommodate the amount of copy on each slide.
- Feeding AI the same hooks, frameworks, and closing lines everyone else uses, making content sound identical.
- Publishing content that is not rooted in the real questions, problems, and searches of your target clients.
