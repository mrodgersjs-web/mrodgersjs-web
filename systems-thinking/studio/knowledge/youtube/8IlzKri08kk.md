# Introduction to Cells: The Grand Cell Tour

A guided tour through cell theory, the prokaryote/eukaryote divide, and the major organelles found in eukaryotic cells.

## Procedure / models distilled
- State the three tenets of modern cell theory: (1) the cell is the smallest living unit in all organisms, (2) all living things are made of one or more cells, and (3) all cells come from pre-existing cells.
- Classify any organism as either unicellular (one cell) or multicellular (many cells).
- Split all cells into two domains: prokaryotes (Bacteria and Archaea) lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles; eukaryotes (plants, animals, fungi, protists) have both.
- Recognize the four structures shared by all cells: genetic material, cytoplasm, ribosomes, and a cell/plasma membrane.
- Use the mnemonic "pro rhymes with no" to remember prokaryotes have no nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles, and "eu rhymes with do" to remember eukaryotes do have them.
- Picture the cell membrane as selectively permeable gatekeeper that lets only certain materials pass in or out to maintain homeostasis.
- Treat cytoplasm as the jelly-like medium that fills the cell and surrounds its internal structures in both prokaryotes and eukaryotes.
- Invoke the cytoskeleton as a fiber network that supports the cell and its organelles and can enable movement.
- Assign ribosomes the job of making proteins; note they are not membrane-bound and may be free in the cytoplasm or attached to rough ER.
- Identify the nucleus as the control center of eukaryotes that houses DNA and contains the nucleolus where ribosomes are produced.
- Model the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) as a molecular highway: rough ER has attached ribosomes and synthesizes/transports proteins; smooth ER detoxifies and makes lipids.
- Describe the Golgi apparatus as the packaging center that receives, modifies, sorts, and ships molecules in vesicles, including sending some to the membrane for secretion.
- Explain that mitochondria produce ATP via cellular respiration using glucose and oxygen, acting like cellular power plants.
- Distinguish plant cells from animal cells: plants have chloroplasts for photosynthesis, a large central vacuole, and a cell wall; animals do not.
- Trace the secretion pathway for a protein: DNA instructions in nucleus → ribosome → rough ER → transport vesicle → Golgi apparatus → secretory vesicle → cell membrane → outside the cell.

## Failure modes named
- Confusing prokaryotes with eukaryotes, especially forgetting which group has a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
- Assuming all organelles are membrane-bound; ribosomes are not.
- Thinking all cells are identical rather than recognizing animal and plant cell differences.
- Believing energy production happens without glucose and oxygen, or misunderstanding that mitochondria need both for efficient ATP production.
