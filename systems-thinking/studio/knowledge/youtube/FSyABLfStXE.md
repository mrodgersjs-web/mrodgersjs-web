# Alternatives and BATNA in Interest Based Negotiation - Noam Ebner

The video defines BATNA as the single best outside option you can pursue if the current negotiation fails, and explains how to identify it and use it as a non-coercive shield against power plays in both positional and interest-based bargaining.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define "alternatives" as all actions you can take unilaterally outside the current negotiation if no deal is reached.
- Recognize that alternatives exist for both sides and vary in desirability based on certainty, value, and fit.
- Distinguish between raw alternatives and the BATNA: the single best alternative that serves as your benchmark.
- Avoid collecting alternatives for their own sake; identify the one best option that beats or matches the current deal.
- Use your BATNA to set a reservation point or walk-away standard before talks proceed.
- In positional bargaining, compare any proposed deal against your BATNA to decide whether to accept or walk.
- In interest-based negotiation, treat the BATNA as a shield rather than a sword to resist one-sided power plays.
- Reveal your BATNA as objective market information, not as a threat or ultimatum.
- Invite the other side to improve the current offer once your BATNA makes the status quo less attractive.
- Reassess your BATNA over time because outside options can change as markets, relationships, or facts evolve.
- Evaluate the other party's likely BATNA to estimate their constraints and predict their resistance to your proposals.

## Failure modes named
- Treating every alternative as equally good instead of selecting the single best one.
- Gathering too many alternatives instead of focusing on the one that matters.
- Assuming an alternative is desirable when it is uncertain or low-value.
- Using the BATNA as a threat or ultimatum rather than as a transparent benchmark.
- Failing to identify a BATNA, which leaves you exposed to the other party's power.
- Revealing a weak BATNA or misrepresenting a real one, damaging credibility.
- Letting positional pressure override interests because no attractive outside option exists.
