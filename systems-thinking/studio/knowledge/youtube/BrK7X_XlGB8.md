# A visual guide to Bayesian thinking

Julia Galef explains Bayes' rule using a visual rectangle-area analogy to combine prior probabilities with likelihood ratios, then distills three practical principles for updating everyday beliefs.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Represent competing hypotheses as a divided rectangle whose areas show their prior probabilities (base rates).
- Subdivide each hypothesis by the probability of observing the evidence under that hypothesis.
- Compare the areas of the evidence-consistent regions to estimate posterior odds visually.
- Compute posterior odds by multiplying prior odds by the likelihood ratio of the evidence.
- Principle 1 — Remember your priors: do not focus only on the evidence and forget the base rate of the hypothesis.
- Principle 2 — Ask "what would I expect to see if I were wrong?": evaluate how likely the evidence is under the alternative hypothesis.
- Principle 3 — Update incrementally: treat small or medium pieces of evidence as gradual updates rather than dismissing or overweighing them.
- Use rough numerical estimates when exact values are unavailable to keep reasoning anchored.
- Treat anecdotes, approvals, and observations as likelihood ratios, not conclusive proof.

## Failure modes named
- Base rate neglect: ignoring prior frequency and overreacting to a single piece of evidence.
- Confirming pet theories by explaining evidence only under the preferred hypothesis.
- Overweighting evidence that is also plausible under the alternative hypothesis.
- Explaining away evidence entirely with alternative stories instead of shifting probability incrementally.
- Freaking out over weak evidence because the prior probability of the feared outcome was forgotten.
