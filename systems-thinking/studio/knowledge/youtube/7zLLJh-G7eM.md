# Pragmatism: Christian Worldview with R.C. Sproul

R.C. Sproul distinguishes the philosophy of pragmatism from mere practicality, tracing how its "whatever works" theory of truth grows out of metaphysical skepticism, reshapes American problem-solving culture, and ultimately collapses into relativism and short-term expediency unless measured by transcendent norms.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Differentiate "pragmatism" as a theory of truth from "practicality" as a test for truth; one equates truth/goodness with results, the other asks whether truth works without defining truth as results.
- Recognize pragmatism as a specifically American offspring of broader secularism, shaped by industrial, scientific, and medical confidence plus post-Enlightenment metaphysical skepticism.
- Define metaphysics as inquiry into ultimate/supra-natural reality and note pragmatism's prior agnosticism or skepticism toward it.
- Trace pragmatism's intellectual genealogy from Harvard's Metaphysical Club through William James, Charles Peirce, Oliver Wendell Holmes, and John Dewey.
- Use "what works?" as a diagnostic lens to identify when decisions, policies, or cultural styles are driven by results rather than principles.
- Detect truth redefinition: classical correspondence truth becomes "truth is the cash value of an idea" or "what works for me/for us."
- Apply the follow-up probe: "works to what end?"—any claim that something works presupposes a higher norm of what counts as good.
- Evaluate short-term versus long-term consequences; a short-term fix that creates two new problems compounds exponentially over time.
- Map ethical conflict onto the Royce parody: "tell the expedient, the whole expedient, and nothing but the expedient" versus telling the truth.
- Observe that everyone operates from an implicit theory/worldview, even if inarticulate or contradictory; behavior is the clearest expression of that theory.
- Assess pragmatism as living on borrowed capital: it uses unexamined values and principles it cannot itself validate.

## Failure modes named
- Conflating "pragmatic" with "practical" and therefore confusing a philosophy of life with a healthy concern for practice.
- Reducing truth to subjective utility, e.g., "God is true for you, but not for me."
- Focusing on short-term problem-solving while ignoring long-range repercussions.
- Assuming scientific and technological success makes metaphysical/theological questions irrelevant.
- Acting as if one can operate without any metaphysical or theoretical commitments.
- Embracing expedient solutions that trade away eternal norms and principles.
- Letting "what works" become a relativistic standard where contradictory beliefs are both considered true.
- Solving one problem pragmatically and generating two or more downstream problems, escalating into systemic collapse.
