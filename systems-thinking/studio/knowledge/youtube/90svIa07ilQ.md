# Every Negotiation Tactic & How To Use Them Yourself, Explained

The video explains how to use ten core psychological negotiation tactics—anchoring, mirroring, tactical silence, the flinch, good cop/bad cop, door-in-the-face, BATNA, the nibble, ultimatums, and trading concessions—to shape perceptions, extract information, and avoid one-sided deals.

## Procedure / models distilled
- **Anchoring:** open with the first number, bold but believable, so it becomes the mental reference point for every later counteroffer.
- **Mirroring:** repeat the last few words someone said as a question to trigger them to explain more and reveal hidden limits.
- **Tactical silence:** after an offer, pause calmly and hold eye contact; the discomfort pressures the other side to fill the gap with concessions or information.
- **The flinch:** show a brief visible or verbal reaction of surprise/disappointment to make the other side doubt whether their number is too high.
- **Good cop/bad cop:** pair a harsh, inflexible party with a sympathetic one so the counterpart feels relief and concedes to the “ally.”
- **Door in the face:** lead with a large request that is rejected, then propose your real smaller request so it feels like a fair compromise and triggers reciprocity.
- **BATNA:** identify and genuinely strengthen your best alternative before negotiating; real walkaway options create confidence and prevent desperation from showing.
- **The nibble:** after main terms are locked in, ask for one small extra concession; the other side often agrees to avoid restarting the deal.
- **Ultimatum:** deliver a clear take-it-or-leave-it final offer only when you have strong leverage and are truly willing to walk away.
- **Trading, not giving:** never concede for free; make every request conditional on a reciprocal move from the other side.
- **Calibrate boldness to credibility:** anchors, flinches, and door-in-the-face asks must look plausible—if they seem absurd, the tactic collapses.
- **Pressure without aggression:** combine silence, flinch, and mirroring so the other side negotiates against themselves while you gather intelligence.
- **Invest in options early:** line up multiple jobs, quotes, or buyers before the negotiation; a strong BATNA is the foundation of every other tactic.

## Failure modes named
- Anchoring too extreme destroys credibility and frames you as unreasonable.
- Overused or robotic mirroring becomes transparent and breaks rapport.
- Tactical silence held too long or paired with hostile body language can shut the other side down.
- A fake or exaggerated flinch undermines trust and invites skepticism.
- Good cop/bad cop, if detected, damages long-term relationships and future leverage.
- A door-in-the-face opening ask that is too outrageous looks like a joke and eliminates the contrast effect.
- Bluffing a strong BATNA; when tested, you either collapse or accept worse terms.
- Nibbling for too much at the finish line blows up a deal that was already closing.
- Issuing an ultimatum without genuine willingness to walk away locks you into losing credibility or losing the deal.
- Giving free concessions signals weakness, invites escalating demands, and trains the other side to keep asking.
