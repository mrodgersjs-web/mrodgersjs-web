# Michio Kaku: Feedback loops are creating consciousness | Big Think

Michio Kaku argues that consciousness is the sum total of feedback loops required to model oneself in space, society, and time, producing a hierarchy of awareness from plants to animals, humans, and machines.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define consciousness as the total set of feedback loops needed to create a model of yourself in space, in society, and in time.
- Treat the thermostat as the base unit of consciousness: one feedback loop measuring one variable.
- Quantify biological complexity by counting feedback loops: plants (~5), reptiles (~hundreds for space), social animals (limbic system), humans (prefrontal cortex).
- Classify consciousness into four levels: Level 0 (plants, minimal loops), Level 1 (reptiles/robots, spatial modeling), Level 2 (social animals, hierarchy and pack dynamics), Level 3 (humans, future simulation).
- Map the reptilian brain (posterior brain) to spatial orientation and modeling where you are in space.
- Map the limbic system or "monkey brain" to social modeling, including elders, children, pack mentality, and hierarchy.
- Map the prefrontal cortex to future simulation, the capacity that distinguishes humans from other animals.
- Reframe memory as primarily a resource for simulating the future, not just recalling the past.
- Apply the same feedback-loop hierarchy to evaluate machine and robot consciousness rather than judging machines by a separate standard.
- Recognize that robots can simulate narrow, single-direction futures (e.g., airflow) but cannot yet simulate the future in all directions like humans.
- Replace the IQ exam with a broader measure of intelligence based on the ability to see the future in all its forms.
- Treat IQ exams as measures of clerical skill and test-taking ability, not general intelligence.
- Evaluate intelligence across domains (physicist, bank robber, gymnast) by future-oriented problem solving rather than by a single score.

## Failure modes named
- Assuming intelligence is universally necessary for survival; many species thrive without high-level future simulation.
- Projecting human cognitive priorities (memory, future planning) onto animals that do not need them for their ecological niche.
- Treating IQ as a complete or authoritative measure of intelligence when it mainly captures clerical skills.
- Conflating high IQ scores with broad intelligence, as seen in professions like clergy or law that score well on tests but may not excel at multi-domain future modeling.
- Expecting robots to be conscious in human-like ways when they currently operate at Level 1 with narrow, task-specific future modeling.
