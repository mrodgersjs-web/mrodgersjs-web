# Why You Can’t Stick To Anything (The Polymath Advantage)

The video reframes chronic “dabbling” not as a character flaw but as a neuroplastic brain pattern driven by dopamine-sensitive reward prediction, a weak anterior midcingulate cortex, and boredom intolerance—and it offers concrete protocols to train the brain for mastery.

## Procedure / models distilled
- **Knowledge Instinct + novelty bias:** your brain’s drive to reduce uncertainty is tuned more to the *speed* of gaining information than to actually possessing knowledge.
- **Novice High → Plateau curve:** early learning feels like falling in love because information gain is steep; once you grasp the core logic, the curve flattens and dopamine drops.
- **Reward Prediction Error sensitivity:** dopamine spikes when new information arrives quickly and shuts off when the rate of learning slows, making the plateau feel unrewarding.
- **aMCC as a willpower muscle:** the anterior midcingulate cortex fires when you do something you don’t want to do; it grows with use and shrinks with avoidance.
- **15% Push protocol:** when the urge to quit appears, extend effort by roughly 15%, then stop—just enough to load the aMCC without overwhelming it.
- **Dabbler vs. Master fork:** the master pushes through the plateau to find deeper patterns; the dabbler quits at the first friction or ego hit.
- **Honest mastery check:** distinguish quitting because you truly mastered the skill from quitting because it got hard.
- **ADHD external scaffolding:** for ADHD brains, add task visualization, time visualization, body doubling, and gamification instead of relying on raw willpower.
- **Boredom Intolerance model:** modern supernormal stimuli raise the stimulation baseline so ordinary skill practice feels suffocating.
- **Dopamine Reset / Stimulus Control:** during practice, remove phones, music, and other high-dopamine inputs so boredom can become a signal for deep encoding.
- **Boredom as clean slate:** boredom marks the shift from consumption to creation and is necessary for deep learning.
- **6 Month Challenge:** pick one interest, commit for six months, aim to experience the plateau, and use the 15% Push to train the aMCC.
- **Polymath-in-training identity:** proving you can push through the dip changes your self-label from “dabbler” to someone capable of mastery.

## Failure modes named
- Mistaking the “Novice High” for long-term passion and abandoning the skill when it fades.
- Labeling yourself as flaky, lacking grit, or a “Jack of all trades, master of none” rather than recognizing a trainable pattern.
- Confusing true laziness with acting-without-fuel during dopamine withdrawal.
- Quitting at the first obstacle because of ego injury or fear, not because the skill is mastered.
- Treating willpower as a fixed character trait instead of a trainable brain region.
- Letting the aMCC atrophy by repeatedly dropping the weight at boredom or frustration.
- Trying to brute-force the plateau with raw willpower when ADHD is present, leading to burnout and shame.
- Letting supernormal stimuli recalibrate stimulation expectations so quiet practice feels unbearable.
- Interpreting boredom as a stop signal instead of a cue for deep encoding.
- Overloading the aMCC by forcing four-hour sessions instead of using progressive 15% pushes.
