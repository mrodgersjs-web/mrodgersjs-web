# Stoicism for Beginners: How to Make Better Decisions Like an Emperor

A practical Stoic guide to escaping decision paralysis by separating what you can control from what you cannot, then choosing from values and reason instead of fear.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Apply the **dichotomy of control**: list what is up to you (values, intentions, reasoning, preparation, response) and what is not (outcomes, other people, market, luck, timing); judge the decision by reasoning quality, not results.
- Treat **emotions as signals, not executives**: notice fear and excitement, but do not let them make the final call.
- Take an **intentional 24–48 hour pause** before a major decision; do not fill it with more research or opinions.
- Draw a **two-column inventory** of "within my control" vs. "not within my control" and notice where the paralysis actually lives.
- **Separate signal from noise** in fear: signal fear names a specific risk and an action; noise fear dissolves into vague catastrophe.
- Run a **pre-mortem** (premeditatio malorum): assume the decision went realistically badly, identify what happened and how you would respond, then prepare for those vulnerabilities.
- Do a **values-alignment check**: ask whether the choice moves you toward or away from who you actually want to be, not who others expect.
- **Reason it out loud** or write one paragraph of the decision in your own words so the weaknesses in logic become audible.
- **Commit fully** once the decision is made; stop monitoring early signs as evidence you chose wrong.
- Build **eudaimonia** and self-trust by repeatedly choosing with clarity; confidence is built by the decision, not required before it.

## Failure modes named
- Loss aversion: the pain of losing feels roughly twice as strong as the pleasure of an equivalent gain, warping risk perception.
- Certainty addiction: endless research and opinion-gathering as avoidance dressed as preparation.
- Social mirror problem: deciding for an imagined tribunal of parents, peers, or your past self instead of for yourself.
- Overthinking spiral / rumination: fear disguises itself as analysis and doubt dresses as due diligence.
- Emotional hijacking: letting excitement or terror make the decision and bypass critical details.
- Half-commitment / post-decision auditing: keeping one foot in the old option and second-guessing early results undermines execution.
