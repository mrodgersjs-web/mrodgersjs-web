# Competitive RIVALRY vs. Competitive DYNAMICS?

Competitive rivalry analyzes how two specific firms interact, while competitive dynamics examines the speed and pattern of actions and reactions across all competitors within slow-cycle, fast-cycle, or standard-cycle markets.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Define competitive rivalry as the study of the relationship between two specific firms and their direct competitive interactions.
- Assess market commonality and resource similarity to map the overlap between rival firms.
- Evaluate awareness, motivation, and ability as the three drivers of competitive behavior between firms.
- Factor in first-mover incentives, company size, and product/service quality relative to price when analyzing dyadic rivalry.
- Define competitive dynamics as the study of how quickly all competitors in an industry or market respond to one another's actions.
- Measure market speed as the core variable: the rate at which competitors can react in real time.
- Classify the industry into one of three market-cycle types: slow cycle, fast cycle, or standard cycle.
- In slow-cycle markets, identify legally protected advantages such as copyrights and patents that can block competition for years or decades.
- In fast-cycle markets, assume advantages are temporary; expect rapid imitation, reverse engineering, technology diffusion, and trend-chasing.
- In standard-cycle markets, treat competitive advantage as partially sustainable through continual quality upgrades and customer relationship building.
- Prioritize large market share, brand names, consistent quality, and operational cost control in standard-cycle markets.
- Diagnose your own industry by asking: how quickly can competitors respond, what legal protections exist, how fast do trends shift, and how strong is brand loyalty.

## Failure modes named
- Conflating competitive rivalry (two-firm interaction) with competitive dynamics (whole-market speed and action-reaction patterns).
- Treating standard-cycle markets as merely a midpoint between slow and fast cycles, rather than as a distinct logic centered on customer relationships and loyalty.
- Assuming competitive advantages are durable in fast-cycle markets where imitation and obsolescence happen rapidly.
- Overlooking legal protections in slow-cycle markets and underestimating how long they can shield profits.
- Neglecting brand loyalty and operational discipline in standard-cycle markets where repeat customers are the primary source of advantage.
