# Complex Adaptive Systems - Learning Session

An introduction to complex adaptive systems, explaining how autonomous agents following local rules produce emergent order, and how evolution, resilience, robustness, and anti-fragility shape the systems we seek to change.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Distinguish complex systems from complex adaptive systems by checking whether the parts have autonomy and adaptive capacity, such as weather versus traffic.
- Identify the agents in a system and the local interaction rules they follow, since these generate the global pattern.
- Look for emergence: order and organization arise from local interactions without any central controller.
- Apply the model that simple local rules can generate complex global patterns, as seen in starling murmurations.
- Use game theory to map cooperation and competition dynamics among adaptive agents.
- Recognize evolution as the long-term mechanism that shapes the structures and functions of complex adaptive systems.
- Harness evolutionary algorithms to accelerate adaptation by iterating designs, testing them in simulated environments, and selecting better variants.
- Map homeostasis: systems self-regulate to maintain functionality around an equilibrium state.
- Assess robustness as the capacity to resist disturbance and remain close to equilibrium.
- Assess resilience as the capacity to adapt, recover, and find a new viable state after disturbance.
- Evaluate decentralization as a source of resilience: distributed systems survive localized failures better than centralized ones.
- Apply anti-fragility: design systems that learn and grow stronger through shocks, mistakes, and failures, like the human immune system.

## Failure modes named
- Imposing top-down order on a complex adaptive system through centralized plans and hierarchical control.
- Treating a complex adaptive system as if it were a mechanical or centrally controllable system.
- Building centralized architectures that are vulnerable to attack or failure because they depend on a single critical node.
- Engineering conventional systems that fracture under shock rather than adapting, healing, or learning from disturbance.
- Avoiding disturbance entirely, which prevents the system from building resilience or anti-fragility.
