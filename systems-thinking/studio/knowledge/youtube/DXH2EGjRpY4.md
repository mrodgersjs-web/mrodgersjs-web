# How to Win with Game Theory & Defeat Smart Opponents | Kevin Zollman | Big Think

Game theorist Kevin Zollman explains that in true zero-sum conflicts against sophisticated opponents the best approach is a randomized minimax strategy, while against less sophisticated opponents strategy must be tailored to the specific interaction.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Identify whether the situation is truly zero-sum—one party’s gain equals the other’s loss.
- Assess the opponent’s sophistication before selecting a strategy.
- Against highly sophisticated opponents, assume they can out-think you and avoid predictable, deterministic play.
- Use a mixed strategy: randomize actions so the opponent cannot exploit your patterns.
- Make randomization principled rather than arbitrary; choose probabilities that neutralize the opponent’s best response.
- Apply the minimax principle: determine the worst-case loss the opponent can force, then pick the action that minimizes that maximum loss.
- Rely on minimax guarantees only in zero-sum games, where it yields the best possible outcome.
- Avoid default minimax or randomization in non-zero-sum interactions, because it can eliminate mutual value.
- Against unsophisticated opponents, shift from minimax to adaptive out-thinking: model what they expect and potentially do the opposite.
- Treat bluffing and counter-bluffing as recursive mind games whose correct move depends on the opponent’s actual depth of reasoning.
- Accept that no universal strategy exists for unsophisticated opponents; strategy must be interaction-specific.

## Failure modes named
- Misclassifying a non-zero-sum interaction as zero-sum and applying minimax, destroying cooperative surplus.
- Being predictable against a sophisticated opponent, enabling exploitation.
- Randomizing blindly without a sound probability distribution, which still leaves room for exploitation.
- Overestimating your own sophistication relative to a smart opponent and using an elaborate deterministic plan.
- Underestimating a weaker opponent or applying a sophisticated playbook without adjusting to context.
- Engaging in recursive bluff/counter-bluff reasoning without calibrating the opponent’s actual level of strategic thought.
