# Stock Flow Diagram using Bathtub Dynamics (Systems Thinking, session 2)

Stocks, flows, causal-loop and stock-flow diagramming taught through the bathtub, then mapped to climate.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Event-based viewpoint (one incident, no history) vs systems viewpoint (pattern of behavior over time) — dynamics means changing with respect to time (0:59–1:41).
- System anatomy: elements (stocks/flows), interconnection via feedback, and a goal the feedback serves (1:53–2:36).
- Meadows' test: "if you understand the dynamics of stocks and flows, you understand a good deal about the behavior of complex systems" (3:02–3:11).
- Bathtub model: water level = **stock**; faucet = inflow; drain = outflow; inflow with no outflow overflows the tub (4:03–5:01).
- Causal loop diagram (CLD) of the tub: water level —(−)→ perceived gap; desired level —(+)→ gap; gap —(+)→ faucet position —(+)→ water flow —(+)→ water level; one negative link ⇒ a **balancing (self-controlling) feedback loop** (5:26–7:53).
- Stock-flow diagram (SFD): stock in a box fed by a valve (inflow); the gap signal drives the valve (7:57–9:25).
- Transfer pattern to climate: emissions = inflow, atmospheric CO2 = stock/water level, net carbon removal = outflow (9:46–10:26).
- Dynamic equilibrium: 5 L/min in with 5 L/min out holds the level constant — a system can be static in level while busy in flow (10:43–11:25).

## Failure modes named
- Reading a stock by its flows: emissions "stabilizing" while removal lags still raises the level — the bathtub keeps filling (10:53–11:20).
- Event-level analysis that misses history and trend (1:00–1:16).
- Treating a balancing loop's negative sign as "bad" rather than self-controlling (7:34–7:53).

## Studio relevance
- diagnose — the stock/flow/bathtub translation layer for turning any accumulation problem (debt, backlog, CO2) into a diagrammable system.
