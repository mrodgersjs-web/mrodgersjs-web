# A product-market-fit masterclass | Kareem Amin (Clay)

Kareem Amin explains Clay's six-year path to product-market fit, arguing that the strongest signal is customers buying despite missing features, and that committing to a narrow ICP and managing founder psychology are the real unlocks.

## Procedure / models distilled
- Build "make it work, then make it simple": ship a working product before optimizing simplicity.
- Recognize real demand by willingness-to-wait: when the need is large enough, customers will buy an incomplete product and wait for missing features; this is the main indicator the idea is worthwhile.
- Treat outbound as operations, not just messaging: successful outbound requires finding the ICP, nurturing specificity, and continuously refining who you target.
- Commit to a single ICP and speak its language: narrowing the product to one persona lets you limit options, reduce friction, and innovate within clear boundaries.
- Use constraints to enable innovation: focusing on one use case (e.g., salespeople doing outbound) creates productive boundaries that drive better product decisions than remaining horizontal.
- Expect a spiral, not a linear path: ICP decisions are revisited multiple times; teams get sidetracked and must keep returning to the same commitment until it finally shows in the product.
- Cut features that serve non-target personas: removing paths like project management or engineering workflows makes the product more seamless for the chosen ICP.
- Feed the process, not the product: shift thinking from "what features should we build" to "what process are we enabling and how do we feed that process."
- Practice founder introspection through meditation and therapy: techniques like Internal Family Systems help surface conflicting internal motivations so decisions become clearer and more focused.
- Reframe work as gardening, not building: instead of forcing the company to be the biggest or most meaningful thing, let it reveal what it wants to become and nurture that growth.
- Remove "meaning-pressure" from the company: finding meaning in micro moments—customers saving time or doing the impossible—improves decision quality more than loading the startup with existential purpose.
- Double down on what is already working: when one metric is strong (e.g., growth) and another is acceptable (e.g., retention), push harder on the strength rather than over-optimizing the adequate metric.

## Failure modes named
- Believing you are "just missing one thing" and adding features to chase a sale instead of finding customers who want what already exists.
- Staying horizontal too long because the product could theoretically serve many personas, which confuses feature set and product language.
- Getting sidetracked after committing to an ICP, causing the commitment to fail to show up in the actual product.
- Avoiding the claustrophobia of focus: founders who value freedom may resist narrowing the product because it feels constraining.
- Loading the startup with personal fulfillment expectations, which degrades decision making and leads to abstract strategy spirals.
- Optimizing already-okay metrics instead of amplifying the strongest growth loop.
- Treating outbound as a simple list-download-and-message activity rather than a complex, iterative operation.
