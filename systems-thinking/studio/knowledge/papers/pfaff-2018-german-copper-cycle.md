# Resource efficiency in the German copper cycle: Analysis of stock and flow dynamics resulting from different efficiency measures

## Claim

Circular-economy potential is bounded by physical accumulation: how much secondary material can substitute primary depends on the size and release timing of retired material stocks. Stock-and-flow dynamics — not efficiency ratios — determine what efficiency measures actually deliver.

## Method

Dynamic stock-and-flow (material flow analysis) model of the German copper cycle: stocks of in-use and retired copper, flows of production/consumption/recycling, simulated under different efficiency measures to compare resulting resource-demand trajectories.

## Studio relevance

- **frame**: the cleanest physical exemplar of the stock/flow distinction — copper in use is a stock; recycling is a flow limited by how fast the stock retires. Any "rate vs level" confusion in a frame can be referred to this shape.
- **diagnose**: efficiency-measure comparison is delay-aware diagnosis: interventions whose benefit arrives only as the old stock turns over.
- **leverage**: shows why parameter tuning (efficiency %) under-delivers when the binding constraint is stock turnover structure (rank 10–12 vs rank 9 delay logic).
