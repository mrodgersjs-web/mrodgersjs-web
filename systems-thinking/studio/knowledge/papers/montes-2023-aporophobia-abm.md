# An Agent-Based Model for Poverty and Discrimination Policy-Making

## Claim

Traditional redistribution policies are losing effectiveness (global poverty reduction is decelerating). Discrimination against the poor — aporophobia — at the institutional level may itself brake poverty reduction; an agent-based model (AABM) can quantify that impact and support policy-making around it.

## Method

Design and proposal of an aporophobia ABM: institutional-level discrimination encoded into agent interactions, first applied to a case study in the city of Barcelona, intended for policy evaluation.

## Studio relevance

- **frame**: worked example of framing a social-system question as agents + interaction rules where aggregate stock/flow models would hide the mechanism (who discriminates against whom, and what that does to flows of opportunity).
- **leverage**: the point of the AABM is intervention exploration — test redistribution vs anti-discrimination levers and observe emergent poverty trajectories.
- **diagnose**: demonstrates encoding an invisible institutional mechanism as an explicit model element so its consequences become observable.
