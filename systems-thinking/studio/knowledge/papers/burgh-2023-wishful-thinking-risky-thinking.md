# Wishful Thinking is Risky Thinking

## Claim

Wishful thinking — beliefs distorted toward what one desires — has a formal decision-theoretic structure with costs and benefits, and it is directly expressible in risk measures: distorted beliefs make decision-makers choose options with more skewness and more risk. Extreme cases assign zero probability to undesirable states and positive probability to otherwise impossible states.

## Method

Formal modeling: a decision-theoretic model incorporating the costs and benefits of biased beliefs, establishing the equivalence between wishful thinking and risk-measure behavior, with characterization of induced risk preferences.

## Studio relevance

- **diagnose**: the named failure mode to check before accepting any diagnosis or forecast produced inside a motivated team: is the belief about system state distorted because the outcome is desired? Zero-probability-on-bad-states is the formal version of "that failure mode can't happen here."
- **frame**: when eliciting a frame from stakeholders, wishful priors corrupt the loop structure itself (link signs and delays assumed optimistic).
- **ground**: formal citation for treating optimism bias as measurable risk distortion rather than a personality remark.
