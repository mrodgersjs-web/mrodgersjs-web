# The Dynamics of Internet Traffic: Self-Similarity, Self-Organization, and Complex Phenomena

## Claim

Internet traffic — "the most complex system ever created" — exhibits rich complex dynamics: self-similar traffic patterns, self-organization, traffic oscillations, and large-scale emergent effects (e.g., worm epidemics). Neither the network-engineering view nor the physics view alone captures it; both perspectives are needed, and each has characteristic blind spots.

## Method

Review/synthesis of the complex-dynamics literature on Internet traffic, deliberately written from a dual network-engineering + physics perspective, covering less-treated phenomena: traffic oscillations, large-scale effects of worm traffic, and comparisons between the Internet and biological systems.

## Studio relevance

- **diagnose**: a production engineering system where self-similarity and self-organization are measured facts — the exemplar to cite when told "our infra can't be exhibiting feedback effects, it's just servers."
- **frame**: the engineering-view vs physics-view dichotomy maps onto practitioner-view vs systems-view when framing any large distributed system.
- **ground**: empirical anchor that emergent phenomena appear in built systems, not just natural ones (pairs with Abbott 2006).
