# Software Engineering for Collective Cyber-Physical Ecosystems

## Claim

Large-scale cyber-physical ecosystems are usually engineered as "composites" (heterogeneous functional complexes assembled top-down), but a complementary "collective" perspective — uniform, collaborative, self-organizing groups of entities, inspired by self-organizing systems and swarm robotics — is increasingly viable and changes what software engineering for such systems means.

## Method

Research-agenda/position article: motivations and state of the art of the collective computing paradigm, its distinctive software-engineering challenges (programming self-organization, macrobehavior from micro-rules), and an outline of implications and open directions.

## Studio relevance

- **leverage**: engineering for self-organization is Meadows rank 4 (power to self-organize system structure) made concrete in software — the design move for agent fleets expected to reconfigure themselves.
- **frame**: composite-vs-collective is a framing decision: does the system get designed top-down as parts, or bottom-up as a collective with emergent macrobehavior?
- **diagnose**: when a "collective" misbehaves, diagnose at the interaction-rule level, not the component level.
