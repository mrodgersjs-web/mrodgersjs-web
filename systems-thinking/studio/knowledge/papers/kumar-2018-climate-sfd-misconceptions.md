# Alleviating misconceptions about Earth's climate: evidence of behavioral learning in stock-and-flow simulations

## Claim

Stock-and-flow simulation tools that give direct experience of cause-and-effect relationships can correct persistent systems misconceptions — and the corrected reasoning transfers to subsequent, different tasks, not just the exercised one.

## Method

Randomized experiment: 120 participants assigned to problems varying use of the Dynamic Climate Change Simulator (DCCS), followed by a paper-and-pencil Climate Stabilization (CS) transfer task; misconceptions measured before/after.

## Studio relevance

- **ground**: experimental evidence that interacting with stock-flow structure builds transferable systems reasoning — the pedagogical basis for making agents (and humans) *simulate* rather than merely read about loops.
- **diagnose**: the climate-misconception class (CO₂ accumulation vs emissions flow) is the same error as "we cut the rate, why is the stock still growing?" — a recurring diagnostic confusion the studio should name explicitly.
- **frame**: justifies requiring a runnable or walkable stock-flow mental model in every frame.
