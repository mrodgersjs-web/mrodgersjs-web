# What is System Dynamics Modeling? Defining Characteristics and the Opportunities they Create

## Claim

System dynamics modeling can be pinned down by five defining characteristics: (1) models are based on causal feedback structure, (2) accumulations and delays are foundational, (3) models are equation-based, (4) the concept of time is continuous, and (5) analysis focuses on feedback dynamics. These characteristics give the field a shared identity, clarify its impact, and expose research opportunities as it progresses.

## Method

Conceptual/definitional analysis. The authors derive the characteristic set from the field's core philosophy and historical work, choose it to be strict enough to distinguish quantitative SD from adjacent methods yet flexible enough to stay relevant, then work through implications and open research directions.

## Studio relevance

- **frame**: use the five characteristics as the acceptance test for any model the studio calls "system dynamics" — causal structure, stocks+delays, equations, continuous time, feedback analysis. A diagram without stocks and delays is a sketch, not an SD model.
- **diagnose**: characteristics 1–2 tell you what must be present before feedback diagnosis (archetypes) is legitimate.
- **ground**: citable definitional anchor when the corpus or an agent disputes SD vocabulary.
