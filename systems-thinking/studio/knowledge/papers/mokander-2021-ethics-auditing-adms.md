# Ethics-Based Auditing of Automated Decision-Making Systems: Intervention Points and Policy Implications

## Claim

Governance mechanisms designed for human decision-makers fail when applied to automated decision-making systems (ADMS). Ethics-based auditing (EBA) — structured assessment of ADMS against principles/norms — can help organizations verify claims about their ADMS and give decision-subjects justifications, but only under specific conditions on *where* in the system lifecycle the audit (intervention) is placed and what policy surrounds it.

## Method

Conceptual governance analysis: outline the conditions under which EBA works as an ADMS governance mechanism, with emphasis on choosing intervention points and their policy implications (companion to a prior paper on EBA's nature and scope).

## Studio relevance

- **leverage**: an explicit intervention-point selection discipline for automated/agentic systems — where you insert the audit (rank-6 information flow) determines whether it can work at all. Directly applicable to governing agent fleets, not just ML models.
- **frame**: adding an audit loop to a frame = adding a balancing loop; this paper says where such loops can and cannot attach.
- **ground**: citable for "governance loops must be placed, not just added."
