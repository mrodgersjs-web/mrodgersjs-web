# Inducing and Mitigating a Self-Reinforcing Degradation in Decision-making Teams

## Claim

Positive (self-reinforcing) feedback in a command organization's decision workload can drive overload and saturation of decision-making elements, and ultimately cascading collapse of the organization as overload and erroneous decisions propagate. The dynamics are structural: they arise from the feedback architecture, not from any individual's incompetence.

## Method

System-theoretic stability analysis of organizational decision-making: a simple dynamic model of decision demand vs. decision capacity showing entry into a self-reinforcing workload spiral, extended to more complex networked organizations that exhibit the same cascade dynamics; then mitigation strategies analyzed against the induced failures.

## Studio relevance

- **diagnose**: this is the "escalation → saturation → collapse" archetype demonstrated quantitatively on an organization — the formal backing for flagging any team loop where rework/decisions generate more rework/decisions (incident triage, on-call spirals, approval queues).
- **leverage**: the mitigation side shows which structural changes (capacity, loop attenuation) actually break the spiral — delay and gain adjustments, Meadows ranks 9 and 7.
- **frame**: mandates a decision-capacity stock in any org frame where workload escalation is suspected.
